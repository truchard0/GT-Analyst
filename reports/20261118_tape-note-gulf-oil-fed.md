## Oil: the strait is shut and crude is falling anyway

Brent has lost its war premium because barrels are leaving the Gulf by routes that ship tracking cannot see, and the market is now trading a 2027 surplus instead of the closure.

> **Chart 1.** Front-month Brent settlements, 23 Oct to 17 Nov 2026 (10 Nov is the low reported by Kpler); wire reports *(drawn in the Claude Doc and in the docx/pdf exports)*

Front-month Brent settlements, 23 Oct to 17 Nov 2026 (10 Nov is the low reported by Kpler); wire reports

The week brought more attacks, not fewer. Iran's Guards claim six tankers mined since 15 November, UKMTO confirmed projectile strikes, and an LPG carrier was hit twice off Khasab and abandoned with one seafarer missing (reported 17 Nov, 15:00 UTC). Brent still settled at $93.14 that evening (reported 17 Nov, 22:36 UTC).

> **Chart 2.** IMF PortWatch, Kpler and IEA figures as compiled by hormuz.now, 13 to 15 Nov 2026 *(drawn in the Claude Doc and in the docx/pdf exports)*

IMF PortWatch, Kpler and IEA figures as compiled by hormuz.now, 13 to 15 Nov 2026

The gap between the meters is the story. Tankers cross with transponders off or under US naval coordination, Saudi crude moves through the East-West pipeline (about 5.8 mb/d on 12 October), and about 2.6 mb/d is transferred ship-to-ship in the Gulf of Oman. War-risk cover still costs 7 to 9% of hull value against 0.25% before the war, so the route is expensive, not closed.

Three further weights sit on the price. Goldman Sachs forecasts a 2 mb/d surplus and an $88 Brent average for 2027 (reported 16 Nov). Supertanker rates are down more than 60% from October's record (16 Nov). OPEC+ sets 2027 quotas on 29 November, the day before the month-end settlement.

One caution against extrapolating: Tankertrackers reports fewer empty tankers heading into the Gulf (17 Nov), which would cap the export revival if it continues.

**Read-across.** In price terms this has stopped behaving like an oil-supply shock. Crude is falling while bond yields rise, so the inflation impulse now comes from core prices and the Fed, not from the barrel. That channel is what feeds the rates calls in this note; the oil price itself is context, not a call.

## Iran: tit-for-tat, not a campaign

Fire resumed after a two-month lull, but nothing on the tape shows a decision to launch a planned campaign, so the frozen conflict stays the 85% path through 30 November.

> **Chart 3.** Scenario tree to 30 November 2026 · 3 branches, desk probabilities *(drawn in the Claude Doc and in the docx/pdf exports)*

Scenario tree to 30 November 2026 · 3 branches, desk probabilities

What happened, with report times:

- Iran's Guards claimed a third drone attack in under a week on the US radar at Isa Air Base in Bahrain. Central Command says all were intercepted with no damage (17 Nov).

- US aircraft hit a Guards missile launch site on Qeshm Island in what a US official called self-defense; Iran reports little damage (Bloomberg, reported 17 Nov, 20:14 UTC). It is the third US strike day on Iranian territory this month, after 2 and 6 November.

- A third carrier, USS Theodore Roosevelt, joined 5th Fleet on 15 November while the Lincoln's last escorts went home (USNI News, reported 16 Nov, 21:42 UTC).

- Kuwait reported intercepting drones late on 17 November (Al Jazeera, 23:38 UTC).

**Why not a campaign.** Returning fire and launching a campaign are different decisions. Commanders return fire under standing rules; a campaign needs a presidential order, and the president is in Asia for a summit where he says he will raise Iran. The carrier arrival is partly a rotation. The route to a campaign now runs through an Iranian strike that kills Americans.

**Why not a deal.** Trump said in Manila that a deal is "very close to getting it done"; four days earlier he said no deal was needed. We read both as talk. The costly moves point the other way: the US widened its blockade to Iran's land and Caspian routes under a task force created on 7 November, and Tehran answered with drones. Iran's speaker says the country will not stay in "neither war nor peace", which fits a squeezed side raising the price of the status quo at low rungs.

**The call we add.** At least one more US strike on Iranian soil by 30 November, 72%. Three strike days in 16 would give 93% over the next 13 days at an unchanged pace. We cut that to 72% because the pace followed a long lull and the 19 November summit could bring a pause.

Two items we note without a call. The Houthis have retaken Bab el-Mandeb and are moving on Aden (Long War Journal, 16 Nov), which puts a second chokepoint back in play. Trump said the US takes "very seriously" a report of an Iranian dirty-bomb plot against Washington, while his homeland security secretary said the homeland is secure (17 Nov); we treat that as sentiment.

## Fed: December is priced; the open questions are size and January

Futures put about 95% on a quarter-point hike on 9 December and we agree at 92%. What moved it was core inflation, not oil.

> **Chart 4.** CME FedWatch via CNBC (December, 17 Nov 2026) and TheStreet (January, March) *(drawn in the Claude Doc and in the docx/pdf exports)*

CME FedWatch via CNBC (December, 17 Nov 2026) and TheStreet (January, March)

- **The driver changed.** Core CPI rose 0.3% in October for a third straight month (10 Nov). Since then Brent fell and hike odds rose, so the Fed's problem has moved from the barrel to underlying prices.

- **The committee is leaning further.** Governor Waller said a half-point move should be considered if inflation stays hot, and St. Louis Fed president Musalem said up to four more hikes may be needed by spring (17 Nov). October's hold already carried two dissents for a hike. Futures put about 7% on a half-point move.

- **The tape is a real-rate shock.** The 10-year Treasury has closed above 5% for 27 sessions (about 5.10%), the dollar is at a 19-month high, and gold is near $3,850, a 14-month low. The S&P 500 closed at 7,340.53 on 17 November, its lowest since 29 July, with all 11 sectors down. Gold and long-duration equities falling together while the dollar rises is the signature of rates, not of war.

- **The calendar protects the call.** November CPI is on 10 December, the day after the decision. Only payrolls on 4 December or a market break can still change the meeting.

What would flip it is funding stress, not data: a Fed official called this week for a standing plan to rescue the Treasury market, and homebuilder sentiment is at a 14-year low. Minutes of the October meeting are due today at 14:00 ET; the point to read is how many participants beyond the two dissenters saw a hike coming.

## AI: Nvidia's numbers beat its scandal

Nvidia reported $115.8bn of revenue against $109.1bn expected and raised next year's growth outlook to 75%, five days after Jensen Huang resigned. Demand is intact, so the 25% fall in the shares over the past month was about governance, export control and rates.

> **Chart 5.** Nvidia Q3 FY27 release, 17 Nov 2026; consensus per LSEG as reported by Reuters and CNBC *(drawn in the Claude Doc and in the docx/pdf exports)*

Nvidia Q3 FY27 release, 17 Nov 2026; consensus per LSEG as reported by Reuters and CNBC

- **The print (17 Nov, after the close).** Revenue rose 103% on the year, with data center at $107.6bn. The outlook for the year to January 2028 went from 70% growth to 75%, and it assumes no data-center compute sales to China. The shares rose about 3% after hours.

- **The scandal.** Huang resigned on 12 November after reports of a relationship with a woman believed to be a Chinese agent, linked to the Megaspeed export investigation. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim chief executive, and the chair says the board will not rehire Huang (Reuters, reported 17 Nov, 23:28 UTC).

- **The risk to watch is financing, not demand.** Nvidia carries about $108.5bn of financial guarantees (10-Q for the quarter to 26 July; about $118bn in October per press), inside about $530bn of total commitments plus guarantees (Tom's Hardware, 16 Nov), three customers made up 36% of the quarter's revenue, and Reuters reports the financing machine may run with "less risk appetite" without Huang. With the 10-year above 5%, vendor finance is the channel through which a governance shock could become an order shock.

- **Policy.** A US curb on Chinese access to data centers is advancing despite lobbying by Amazon, Google and Meta (Reuters, 17 Nov), and Nvidia has warned buyers of legal exposure for serving Chinese clients.

- **Next test.** Anthropic lists on Nasdaq as ANT on 19 November and is expected to price above its $70 to $78 range (CNBC, 17 Nov), a direct read on appetite for AI paper in a hiking cycle.

We make no directional call on the AI complex. Our news-based gauge of AI financing stress is below its four-week norm, which removes the case for an underperform call, and the rate shock argues against chasing the bounce. For 2027 we raise the odds of another year of 30%-plus capex growth to 40% from 25%, cut a slowdown to under 25% growth to 42% from 55%, and hold an outright capex decline at 18%.

## Catalysts and what would change the view

The next three weeks are front-loaded: the Shenzhen summit tomorrow, then OPEC+ and the Iran deadline at month-end, then the Fed.

Four developments would move these calls:

- **An Iranian strike that kills Americans.** The campaign path goes from 10% toward even odds.

- **A framework at Shenzhen that Tehran endorses.** Deal odds rise to about 20%.

- **A disorderly break in Treasuries or equities.** This is the one live threat to the December hike call.

- **Nvidia or a large customer pulling back on guarantees or financing.** That turns an intact demand story into order risk and would end our no-call on the AI complex.

This note states direction and mechanism only. It is analysis, not investment advice, and it gives no position sizes.
