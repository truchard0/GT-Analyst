# Call the AI Prints, Not the Price

Nov 18, 2026 · GT-Analyst desk

The AI trade can be forecast in layers, and the layer most people want, the share price, is the one the evidence says cannot be. Reported revenue can be named to about 1% on a normal quarter, better than Wall Street consensus did over the last six months. Next-quarter guides and capex cannot be named tightly. Financing is the layer that moves first, and the marginal AI builder now reads debt-funded on all five of our tests. Price direction to mid-2027 is a no-call, published as odds: 35% repair, 40% range, 25% a second leg down for the SMH semiconductor fund.

## Five layers, five levels of predictability

Most errors on this trade come from answering one layer's question with another layer's evidence. We sort each item first.

| Layer | What it covers | Predictability | What we call |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Prints | Revenue and next-quarter guides against the company's own guide | High for revenue, medium for guides | Thresholds at 80-92%; numeric ranges |
| 2. Capex plans | Buyers' spending guides | Good one guidance cycle ahead, low beyond | "No cut" and threshold calls at 65-90% |
| 3. Financing | Debt, leases, guarantees, ratings, the IPO window | Medium, and it moves first | State calls: a default, a rating |
| 4. Constraints and policy | Power, packaging, export rules | Power is measurable; government decisions are not | Measured outcomes only |
| 5. Price | Share-price direction and levels | Low | No call, except a bounded statement with a base rate of 85% or more |

The master rule: never infer price from a print. On 17 November Nvidia beat consensus revenue by $6.7bn and its last after-hours trade was up 0.9%. In our backtest of 28 beats, the stock closed higher the next session only 11 times.

## Prints: revenue can be named to about 1%, the next guide cannot

We tested a plain rule on every report from 14 AI suppliers and buyers published between 18 May and 17 November: take the company's own guide midpoint and add its average beat over its last four reports. Each forecast used only reports published before it.

&#91;embedded content: data/ai\_prints\_backtest/results\_numeric.json · 29 reports, 18 May-17 Nov 2026; consensus from data/ai\_prints\_backtest/consensus.csv\]

The revenue misses cluster in surprise quarters. Dell's May report came in 25% above its guide and Micron's June report 24% above; consensus missed them too, by 19% and 14%. Without Micron and Dell the median revenue miss was 0.96%. Next-quarter guides are different: our numbers ran about 5% low on average, because companies guided step-ups their own history did not contain, and analysts saw more of that coming.

The same rule gives the next prints below. Each range is the width that would have held 80% of actuals in the backtest; tested out of sample, that width held 79%.

&#91;embedded content: data/ai\_prints\_backtest/forward\_20261118.json · registered 18 Nov 2026 before any of these reports\]

Micron's range is wide because its last four beats include the two largest surprises in the sample. These are statements about reported revenue, not share prices.

**One flag on TSMC.** Our 90% call that TSMC reaches its $53.3bn Q4 midpoint rests partly on a record October. Read against TSMC's usual Q4 shape, October is behind pace, not ahead:

| Year | October as % of the Q4 guide midpoint | November-December vs October | Q4 vs midpoint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2024 | 37.1% | -11.8% | +1.4% |
| 2025 | 36.6% | -7.7% | +2.8% |
| 2026 | 34.6% | due \~10 Dec and \~10 Jan | due 14 or 21 Jan |

If 2026 repeats either year's shape, Q4 lands at $50.5-51.8bn, below the midpoint. The midpoint needs November and December to hold close to October's level. The 90% is under review: our red-team puts it nearer 65%. November revenue, out about 10 December, settles most of it; below about NT$560bn the midpoint is out of reach unless December rises.

## Capex: spending is still rising, and the first 2027 numbers arrive in late January

&#91;embedded content: data/ai\_prints\_backtest/capex\_history.csv · company filings; each company's own capex definition\]

No hyperscaler has given a 2027 number yet. Alphabet says capex will "increase significantly", Amazon will spend "meaningfully more" than about $220bn, Microsoft expects capex to accelerate in the first half of 2027 after trimming 2026 twice on third-party capacity delays, and Meta is still building its budget. To hit their 2026 guides the four must spend about $218.5bn in the fourth quarter, an annual pace near $874bn against a 2026 total near $730bn. Holding that pace is about 20% growth in 2027, which is why we put 88% on none of the four guiding 2027 below 2026.

The case against is cash. Free cash flow was negative at Alphabet and Meta in the September quarter and at Amazon over twelve months, and 52% of fund managers in the November Bank of America survey expect a hyperscaler to announce a cut within six months. A trimmed plan is a looser test than a 2027 guide below 2026, but it is the risk we watch.

Our capex numbers are less precise than our revenue numbers. Quarterly capex missed by a median 9.5% in the backtest, the same as consensus, so the next-quarter capex ranges are wide:

| Company, quarter | Last quarter | Point | 80% range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oracle, Sep-Nov 2026 | 28.5 | 30.5 | 16.4-44.7 |
| Microsoft, Oct-Dec 2026 | 45.2 | 50.3 | 38.8-61.8 |
| Meta, Q4 2026 | 34.9 | 47.7 | 30.6-64.8 |
| Alphabet, Q4 2026 | 51.4 | 65.1 | 59.4-70.9 |
| Amazon, Q4 2026 | 77.3 | 69.4 | 56.8-82.2 |

US$ bn, each company's own definition; source `data/ai_prints_backtest/forward_20261118.json`. A capex guide inside its year is sticky: in the backtest "guide unchanged" beat our model on annual guides 7 times out of 9, so we make no call on mid-year revisions.

## Financing: if this cycle fails, it fails first in the bond market

Two laws from past capex booms set the frame. Equities peaked before capex in all four booms with usable dates (UK railways 1845, Cisco 2000, Nvidia 2018, the chip index 2022), so rising capex guides do not mark a price floor. And the depth of the eventual cut scaled with outside funding: debt-funded telecom carriers cut about 60% from 2000 to 2002, while cash-funded hyperscalers cut 2% for two quarters in 2019. The variable to track is the funding mix, not demand.

| Test | Cash-funded | Debt-funded | Reading on 18 Nov 2026 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hyperscaler free cash flow | Positive | Negative | Negative at Alphabet, Meta, Oracle and (12 months) Amazon; Microsoft positive |
| Bond issuance against forecast | At or below | Above | $296bn year to date against Goldman's $250bn full-year forecast |
| Off-balance-sheet vehicles | Rare | Pricing wider each deal | 6.58% in 2025 to above 7% in July 2026, same tenant |
| The weakest borrower's access | Open | Shut | No high-yield data-center deal since late September; CoreWeave CDS about 1,113bp |
| The vendor as guarantor | No | Yes | Nvidia guarantees of $108.5bn (July) to about $118bn (October, press) |

All five read debt-funded for the marginal builder. The four big buyers still have balance sheets, so the neoclouds and single-tenant vehicles sit in the deep-cut class if access stays shut, and the hyperscalers sit between that and 2019.

**A correction on Nvidia.** The widely repeated "$530bn of guarantees" is total commitments plus guarantees. In the 10-Q for the quarter to 26 July 2026, guarantees were $108.5bn ($105.0bn on one OpenAI-leased campus, $3.5bn on land, power and shell); the rest is $366bn of commitments and $56bn of additional commitments, mostly Nvidia's own supply purchases. Our 18 November tape note repeated the wrong figure and has been corrected. The test is the next 10-Q: flat or shrinking support lines would mean Nvidia is pulling back, and its interim chief executive said on 17 November that guidance now considers "customers' access to capital".

**The two credit calls.** CoreWeave's bonds yield about 15.7% and its five-year CDS implies distress, but distress over five years is not default in four and a half months: on a flat hazard rate the market price implies about 7% to the end of March, close to our 8%. It funds new building through secured loans matched to contracts and expects about $9bn of customer prepayments. Oracle is one notch above junk at S&P with a stable outlook, and sold $10bn of stock at an 11.2% discount in November to defend the rating; a junk rating by March needs S&P to move off stable or Moody's to cut two notches. The two lean on the same counterparties: Nvidia's backstop for the neoclouds, OpenAI's funding for Oracle's backlog.

## Price: one bounded call, and direction as odds

Prints do not predict the reaction, and valuation explains little of one-year returns, so we make one bounded statement about price and publish direction as odds.

&#91;embedded content: data/ai\_prints\_backtest/prices.csv · SMH daily closes, 11 May-16 Nov 2026\]

The bounded statement is that SMH stays below its 2026 high on 30 June 2027 (90%). It rests on the history of chip-index drawdowns measured from the peak:

&#91;embedded content: research\_notes/AI trade forecasting framework/historical\_analogs\_base\_rates.md · QuantFlowLab table (secondary source); current episode from data/ai\_prints\_backtest/prices.csv\]

Six of the seven drawdowns had not regained their high 12.3 months after the peak, and none of the six still falling five months in did so in time. That supports 86-88%, a little under the 90% we log. The strongest case against is in the same table: if the low is already in, four of seven recovered within 7.5 months of the trough. In every episode still falling at month five, the low came later, between 5.6 and 16.2 months after the peak.

## What we do not call, and the limits

| No call | Why |
| --- | --- |
| AI share-price direction | Prints do not predict the reaction; valuation explains little of one-year returns |
| The Anthropic listing (ANT) | The analyst writing this note is an Anthropic model |
| Government decisions on chip exports | The 16 November rule came days before a summit; such decisions are not forecastable with confidence |
| The PJM capacity auction | New rules exclude new large loads, so three cap-clears are no longer a base rate |

Three limits. The backtest covers one six-month up-cycle in which every company raised its guide, so it says nothing about a downturn. The rule was designed by an analyst who had seen some of the outcomes; only the row-by-row forecasts are blind, and the clean test is the 19 forward ranges and 13 calls above. Several historical base rates, including the chip-drawdown table, rest on secondary sources and carry half weight until checked against primary data.

This note states direction and mechanism only. It is analysis, not investment advice, and it gives no position sizes.
