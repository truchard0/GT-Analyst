## Game-theoretic read
- **Chessboard (§4).** Iran's *king* (theocratic political system) has been physically decapitated. The characteristic failure mode of a theocracy is **extremist/factional splintering**; of an autocracy, **succession crisis + elite defection**. Iran is a hybrid theocracy — so expect *both*. Mojtaba's absence is the observable onset.
- **Allies are separate players (§4).** Israel's optimal play diverges hard from the US's: a US-brokered *stable* Iran deal is a *threat* to Israel, which prefers permanent Iranian weakness. Turkey (Erdogan) wants the deal to hold. Never model these as a bloc.
- **Formal core — Fearon commitment problem (§2).** The deeper reason the peace is fragile: post-decapitation, **no one can credibly commit for Iran** (who signs? the heir won't appear). A bargain no counterparty can enforce is inherently unstable — the textbook commitment-problem path to renewed conflict.
- **The honest fork (flip-test, §10.2).** Counter-hypothesis: decapitation produced genuine Iranian *capitulation* — the regime is too broken to fight, so peace holds by exhaustion, not resolution. This is the main thing that would make the calls below wrong, and it's why none are scored >0.70.
