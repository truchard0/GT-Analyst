## Where we are
- **Flows:** IMF PortWatch shows 3.5 transits a day over the four weeks to 25 October, against 81 a day before the war
  (June 2025 to February 2026), down 96%. The only reopening was in late June, when traffic peaked at about 30 a day
  and Brent fell to $71.80 (week of 29 June).
- **Brent:** the front month closed at its war high of $117.64 on 30 October. It traded at $111.6 early on 1 November
  as the talks continued.
- **Mines and talks:**
  - The IRGC says tankers hit mines on 29 October and 1 November, and a Qatari LNG carrier was struck on 1 November.
  - Iran's negotiators held a third day of talks with Qatari mediators in Doha. Araghchi offers to reopen Hormuz within
    two days if the US meets Iran's conditions; there is no breakthrough.
- **Supply offsets:** OPEC+ held its December targets, and Europe is releasing 42 million barrels.
- **Rates:** the 2-year fell from 4.70% to 4.56% over 27–30 October while Brent rose. It is still about 70bp above the
  middle of the Fed's 3.75–4.00% range.

## Who profits from the spike
- **Documented:**
  - Large bearish Brent and WTI blocks were placed minutes to hours before four Iran announcements, 23 March to
    21 April. LSEG data as reported by ABC puts them at about $2.65bn; Reuters counted about $7bn across contracts.
  - The White House warned staff on 24 March against trading on nonpublic information.
  - The CFTC chairman said enforcement is "looking at all these trades".
  - The President's managed accounts sold ExxonMobil on ceasefire day, per his OGE 278-T filings.
  - ExxonMobil earned $15.4bn in Q3 (vs $7.5bn a year earlier) and Chevron $9.9bn (vs $3.5bn).
- **Reported:**
  - A DOJ and CFTC probe of the four episodes.
  - A CFTC enforcement referral that was retracted at the direction of the chairman's adviser (Bloomberg, October).
  - Polymarket wallets that profited before the 28 February strike.
- **Not found:** no identified trader, no official tied to a trade, and no charges, Inspector General review or
  hearing.
- **Read:** the timing is suspicious and the intent unproven. A deliberately engineered spike is unlikely, because
  gasoline up a third into a midterm hurts the President's party. The documented transfer is to producers and
  refiners.
- **Law and market structure:**
  - ICE Brent trades in London under the FCA. ICE shares large-trader data with the CFTC only for its WTI contract.
  - CEA §4c(a)(3)–(4) bars officials from trading on nonpublic information and anyone from misappropriating it; Rule
    180.1 also applies.
  - These provisions were first charged in April 2026, in a Polymarket case.
- **Literature:**
  - Bernile, Hu & Tang (2016, JFE): leaks during FOMC news embargoes are measurable.
  - Kurov et al. (2019, JFQA): drift before releases is common, so drift alone is not proof.
  - Gu & Kurov (2018, JFM): the same pattern appears before gas-storage reports.
  - Ge, Kurov & Wolfe (2019, JFR) and Bianchi et al. (2023, JME): presidential posts move prices.
  - Congressional trading: Ziobrowski et al. find an edge; Eggers & Hainmueller (2013) find none.
  - Fattouh, Kilian & Mahadeva (2013) and Kilian & Murphy (2014): fundamentals, not speculation, set oil levels.
  - Singleton (2014): investor flows mattered at the margin in 2008.
