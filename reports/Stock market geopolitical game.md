# The Midterm Clock Holds Markets Hostage

As of Saturday 24 October 2026, US stocks are being moved less by any one player than by a shared calendar, and almost every player is waiting for 3 November. The S&P 500 closed at **7,623.68 on 10/23, about 2.4% below its 10/6 record of ~7,815**. That calm hides a violent rotation: since 10/2, **SMH is down 16.4% and energy up 10.4%** ([Lance Roberts](https://lanceroberts.substack.com/p/bull-bear-report-week-of-october-c76)). Three forces drive the board, and one chain connects them. First, the Iran war has turned into a tanker-and-blockade contest that put **Brent at $116.72 on 10/23**, its highest settlement since 4/29 ([GV Wire/Reuters](https://gvwire.com/2026/10/23/oil-prices-jump-3-on-houthi-attacks-oil-spill-highest-settlement-since-april)). Second, that oil price feeds a **3.7% CPI** and a Fed under Kevin Warsh that has already hiked once and is priced to hike again on 12/9. Third, a **10-year yield near 5%** makes debt-funded AI capex expensive just as CoreWeave's credit cracks and Meta's free cash flow turns negative. Each decider is constrained by a dated deadline. Trump pledged on 10/8 not to attack Iran before the midterms. Iran is front-loading oil pain before the same vote. Netanyahu faces his own election on 10/27. The Fed meets six days before Election Day. US-China rare-earth and export-control suspensions expire around 11/9-10. OPEC+ decides 2027 policy on 11/29. The engine's lesson, that restraint is political, lapses on a date and usually breaks because Iran provokes it, fits the evidence gathered today. Today's research confirms the shape of the engine's 10/23 prior (scenarios A/B/C at 0.50/0.28/0.22, a mixture median near SPY 760) but corrects several of its inputs. An ECB hike on 10/29 is close to zero, OPEC+ is very unlikely to move quotas on 11/1, and one China node was badly worded; all of these were applied to the book on 10/25. The apparent $5 oil gap is a contract roll: December Brent settled 116.72 while the engine's continuous series had already moved to January at 111.70. The desk's own options data (Act VI) adds three readings. The S&P sits in a short-gamma book going into event week. The Nasdaq volatility curve is inverted while the S&P curve is not. And the most speculative AI names have already fallen 20-56% in a month. The highest-risk window for markets is **4 November to mid-December**, when US restraint, Iranian pressure, Israeli coalition limbo, the Fed's December decision and the 12/11 funding deadline all fall due within about six weeks. This is general analysis, not personalised investment advice, and it recommends no trades.

## Act I: a quiet index sits on top of a violent rotation

The headline index barely moved in October, but the stocks inside it changed places. On 10/22 the Nasdaq fell **2.6% to 26,008.86** (its worst day since June) and the S&P 500 fell 1.2% to 7,663.43, while the Dow and the Russell 2000 closed flat to up ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/nasdaq-today-26008-after-a-2-6-drop-as-oil-hits-116-and-the-10-year-tests-5/)). The PHLX semiconductor index lost **6.72% that day, about $200bn of market value**, and all 30 members fell ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329406/20261023/chip-stocks-lose-200-billion-equipment-makers-warning-counters-nvidias-ai-optimism.htm)). Since the equal-weight/Nasdaq-100 ratio bottomed on 10/7, **76% of S&P 500 members have beaten the index**, but only 42% of tech constituents are higher ([Citadel Securities](https://www.citadelsecurities.com/news-and-insights/global-market-intelligence/october-update/)). On FactSet's numbers the market is not expensive: the forward P/E was **18.7 on 10/23**, below its 5-year (19.8) and 10-year (19.1) averages. That is because Q3 blended EPS growth is running at **29.5%**, with **Energy at +136%** ([FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/sp-500-earnings-season-update-october-23-2026)). The VIX closed at 19.70 on 10/23 ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/nasdaq-today-26008-after-a-2-6-drop-as-oil-hits-116-and-the-10-year-tests-5/)), roughly double SPY's 9.8% realised volatility. In the engine's words, the market is "paying for event risk" ([engine outlook 10/23](20261023_market_outlook.md)).

The other assets tell the same story of an oil shock running through rates. The 10-year Treasury was **about 4.96-5.00% on 10/22-10/23** after an early-October peak ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield)). Sources disagree on that peak. Forbes Advisor lists a 52-week high of **5.31% on 10/5**. "Highest since 2002" is the sourced framing, not "since 2007". Gold, which would normally be the war hedge, is the clearest casualty of the rates channel. It set a record of **$5,589.38 on 1/28** and briefly traded below $4,000 on 10/23 (about $3,986.74), roughly 28% off the high ([Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/gold-etf-allocation-retirement-october-2026/); [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/nasdaq-today-26008-after-a-2-6-drop-as-oil-hits-116-and-the-10-year-tests-5/)). ## Act II: the Gulf war became a fight over sea lanes and dates

### Washington holds its fire until the votes are counted

Trump wants lower pump prices before 11/3 and a visible win afterwards. He fears a refinery-driven fuel spike, US casualties and empty magazines. On **10/8** he wrote that the US "will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections" ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/energy/oil-prices-stocks-trump-iran-rcna602248)). The constraints behind that pledge are on the record. **60% disapprove** of his handling of Iran (Reuters/Ipsos, October). On 8/4 Reuters reported PrSM and ATACMS stocks "virtually exhausted", and about **80% of THAAD interceptors** were reported used by 8/5 ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/us-israel-plans-possible-iran-strikes-after-midterms-1827372); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war)). Gasoline was **$4.2957 on 10/24**, the first October ever above $4 ([AAA](https://gasprices.aaa.com/?state=US)). In April, **77% of voters** said Trump bears at least a fair amount of responsibility for gas prices ([Reuters/Ipsos via KTVU](https://www.ktvu.com/money/reuters-americans-blame-trump-rising-gas-prices)).

The pledge has a clear expiry, and the planning for what comes after it is already done. The NYT reported on 10/21-22 that US and Israeli militaries have drawn up a **"short, intense" post-midterm round** against energy, nuclear (including Pickaxe Mountain) and missile/drone targets. Trump has not signed off. The build-up is due to finish in **early November**. Some officials said the targeting would spare refineries to protect gasoline prices ([IBTimes UK/NYT](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/us-israel-plans-possible-iran-strikes-after-midterms-1827372)). The Atlantic reported that he is weighing "several days" of strikes to force Tehran back to talks ([National Security Journal](https://nationalsecurityjournal.org/iran-just-got-handed-a-new-countdown-trump-is-weighing-several-days-of-fresh-strikes-once-the-midterms-are-over-reports-say/)). A **third carrier is expected at the end of November** ([Fox live](https://www.foxnews.com/live-news/us-iran-war-strait-hormuz-oil-prices-10-22-26)). State ordered non-emergency staff out of Saudi Arabia on 10/21-22 and quietly cut staff in Lebanon, the UAE, Qatar and Jordan ([Iran International](https://www.iranintl.com/en/liveblog/202610187555)). On 10/24 it issued travel advisories for 13 Middle East countries ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/10/24/us-issues-travel-advisories-for-13-middle-east-countries-after-houthi-attacks/)). At a rally on 10/23 Trump said "We may do another little Epic Fury". Reuters also reports him saying the US might instead let the conflict wind down gradually ([JPost/Reuters](https://www.jpost.com/international/article-912674); [GV Wire](https://gvwire.com/2026/10/23/oil-prices-jump-3-on-houthi-attacks-oil-spill-highest-settlement-since-april)).

Timing reports conflict. The Atlantic (10/7-8, via a secondary outlet) said the White House asked for strike options executable **before** the vote ([Political.org](https://political.org/2026/10/08/oil-prices-surge-on-reports-white-house-weighing-new-iran-strikes-before-midterms/)). The NYT and FT accounts place the plans **after** it.

### Tehran is spending a wasting asset before the vote

Iran wants the blockade lifted, its frozen assets released and the war ended on all fronts. It fears the post-vote strike package and economic collapse. It has loaded **no crude for export since late August** ([CNBC via search](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/crude-oil-tanker-strait-hormuz-iran-attack.html)). CENTCOM says the blockade had stopped at least **$25bn** of Iranian crude by its 100th day on 10/22 ([Fox live](https://www.foxnews.com/live-news/us-iran-war-strait-hormuz-oil-prices-10-22-26)). Its own officials explain the strategy. Two senior officials told Reuters on 10/14 that Tehran changed the type and pace of its attacks around **10/4**, partly to influence the US midterms ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/14/iran-steps-up-hormuz-surveillance-attacks-soar-oil-flows-crash-again)). The Washington Post reported on 10/18 that Tehran expects Trump to care less about prices after the vote and is "scrambling to inflict maximum pain now" ([WaPo](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/10/18/tehran-seeks-punish-trump-midterms-with-oil-price-pain-iranian-officials-say/)).

The results show up in the shipping data. The IMO counted **27 attacks on commercial ships in October as of 10/22**, the most of any month of the war ([USNI via search](https://news.usni.org/2026/10/22/october-marks-highest-number-of-strait-of-hormuz-attacks)). A Reuters review counts more than 30 VLCCs damaged in the war, 11 of them in October ([IBTimes AU](https://www.ibtimes.com.au/strait-hormuz-ship-attacks-record-oil-traffic-1877334)). On 10/22 the IRGC claimed seven ships hit in one day, which is unverified and which Trump called "fake". It also threatened US bases if escorts continue ([Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/665644/world/irans-irgc-threatens-us-bases-over-hormuz-escorts-claims-strikes-on-7-ships)).

Iran has also published the price of a US strike. On 10/22 Rezaei threatened Iranian strikes on oil facilities, undersea cables, **data centres and AI facilities** across up to seven Gulf countries ([Saudi Gazette](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/665644/world/irans-irgc-threatens-us-bases-over-hormuz-escorts-claims-strikes-on-7-ships)). Its talks position has hardened from three conditions (9/19) to seven (10/13). Trump rejected a seven-day ceasefire proposal on 9/26 and said on 10/23, "I think we're not negotiating" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war); [Iran International](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202610139692)). On the nuclear file, the IAEA board referred Iran to the Security Council on 9/9 by **23-3-8**. A "triple-urgency" bill to leave the NPT was filed on 9/20 but has not been enacted ([Arms Control Association](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2026-10/news/iaea-reports-iran-un-security-council); [PressTV](https://www.presstv.co.uk/Detail/2026/09/20/776678/Iran-lawmakers-NPT-withdrawal)). Pezeshkian is due in Moscow to meet Putin on **10/27** ([Iran International](https://www.iranintl.com/en/liveblog/202610187555)).

*Inference:* Iran's leverage is tied to the calendar. Before 11/3, every tanker hit raises Trump's political cost. After 11/3 the same tactic buys less and risks the strike package. Tehran's two plausible moves around the vote are therefore a last surge in harassment, or a de-escalation offer right after the polls close to head off the strikes. The NPT bill is the card most likely to be played if Pickaxe Mountain is hit.

### Jerusalem's election freezes the main hawk in place

Netanyahu wants the regime gone. On 10/21 he called overthrowing it "the only way to remove this threat once and for all" ([Fox live](https://www.foxnews.com/live-news/us-iran-war-strait-hormuz-oil-prices-10-22-26)). The FT reported on 10/21 that he had ordered a multi-day strike for the weekend of 10/10, larger than February's Roaring Lion, and that the IDF cancelled it after Trump objected. Netanyahu called the report "not true" ([JPost](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-912360); [JNS](https://www.jns.org/news/israel-news/netanyahu-denies-report-trump-blocked-iran-strike)). In public he said on 10/14 that a strike before the vote was "not convenient for us" ([US News/Reuters](https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-10-14/netanyahu-says-he-is-not-seeking-to-attack-iran-again-before-israel-election)). IDF chief Zamir reportedly warned that renewed war could force the election to be postponed ([Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/r1ybtvboml)). Hegseth met Netanyahu unannounced on **10/19**, and nothing was disclosed ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/hegseth-visits-israel-elections-iran-war-netanyahu/)).

The vote itself looks set to change little in the short run. All three final TV polls on 10/23 put Eisenkot's Yashar first (22-23 seats against Likud's 19-21). They gave Netanyahu's bloc **50 seats in all three** and the Eisenkot bloc 52-56, with the Arab parties at 14-16 seats holding the balance ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/elections/2026-10-23/ty-article/last-polls-before-election-day-put-eisenkot-ahead-both-sides-short-of-majority/000001a1-69fe-d34e-a1b9-ffff12940000)). Results are due to be certified on **11/4** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Israeli_legislative_election)). Under the standard rules, a 28-day mandate follows the president's consultations. *Inference:* that puts the first deadline around 12/9 and the extended one around 12/23, so Netanyahu stays caretaker prime minister, with war powers, through the whole post-midterm window. The engine's book gives 0.84 that no bloc reaches 61 and 0.90 that Netanyahu is still prime minister on 12/1 ([engine outlook](20261023_market_outlook.md)), and the final polls confirm both.

*Inference:* the most market-relevant outcome is a deadlock. It leaves Israel's war-maker in office, with his personal legal exposure rising if an Eisenkot government forms and passes its bill barring indicted prime ministers ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/10/20/israel-election-netanyahu-eisenkot-trump-palestinians-gaza/)), at the same moment Washington's restraint lapses. Lebanon remains "frozen-hot". The Tampa military talks of 10/20-22 reportedly ended without agreement, but that rests on a single unconfirmed source ([JFeed](https://www.jfeed.com/news/israel-lebanon-talks-tampa-no-agreement)).

### The Gulf states and the Houthis turn Saudi Arabia into the second front

The Gulf monarchies want the war over and Hormuz reopened. On 10/24 Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Egypt and Pakistan jointly called for dialogue and a full reopening ([Iran International](https://www.iranintl.com/en/liveblog/202610187555)). Yet they are now targets. The Houthis took Mokha on 9/10 and reportedly Perim Island on 9/11 ([Time](https://time.com/article/2026/09/11/yemen-houthi-red-sea-bab-el-mandeb-shipping-iran-war/)). Then came an airport campaign that, by Wikipedia's tally, killed **22 and injured more than 380** across Abha, Riyadh and Dammam from 6 to 21 October ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2026_Houthi_attacks_on_Saudi_Arabian_airports)). CBS's per-day counts exceed that total, so treat casualties as approximate ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-us-trump-saudi-arabia-houthi/)). On 10/21 debris from a drone aimed at the Shaybah field killed two workers. On 10/22 Aramco evacuated its Dhahran headquarters and **Saudi stocks fell 2.5%** ([Energy News Beat](https://energynewsbeat.co/crude-oil/brent-tops-114-as-houthi-attacks-reach-saudi-oil-and-aramco-clears-its-headquarters/)). Perim's current status is disputed: ISW/CTP reported government-aligned forces retook it around 10/9, on anti-Houthi sources ([Critical Threats](https://www.criticalthreats.org/analysis/iran-update-october-9-2026)).

MBS asked Trump twice on 9/9 for US strikes on the Houthis. As of 10/23, Trump had offered intelligence only ([Time](https://time.com/article/2026/09/11/yemen-houthi-red-sea-bab-el-mandeb-shipping-iran-war/); [News Tribune](https://www.newstribune.com/news/2026/oct/23/us-pulls-diplomats-from-saudi-arabia-as-houthi/)). Qatar is the main US-Iran channel, and its LNG has been under force majeure since **3/4**, now extended to early December ([Modern Diplomacy](https://moderndiplomacy.eu/2026/10/21/eu-russian-lng-ban-winter-2027-forecast/)). The IMF now sees **GCC GDP at -4.8% in 2026** ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/10/13/imf-cuts-2026-growth-forecasts-for-gulf-economies-due-to-prolonged-hormuz-closure/)).

*Inference:* the Houthi front lets Iran raise costs for Saudi Arabia and for oil without firing from its own soil. That keeps the pressure campaign under the line that would end US restraint. The airports and Dhahran, not Hormuz itself, drove the late-October leg in Brent.

### Why barrels recovered while prices rose

The war's central paradox is that physical flows have largely come back. Middle East exports averaged **over 24 mb/d in the week to 10/18, against 23.3 mb/d before the war**, and Hormuz carried about 14 mb/d, about 82% of pre-war (Kpler) ([Wikipedia citing Kpler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Strait_of_Hormuz_crisis)). Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline to Yanbu now runs **about 7 mb/d** ([Energy News Beat](https://energynewsbeat.co/crude-oil/brent-tops-114-as-houthi-attacks-reach-saudi-oil-and-aramco-clears-its-headquarters/)). Yet a US-to-Asia VLCC cost about **$77m** in early October, against $9.2m on average last year ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/energy/oil-prices-stocks-trump-iran-rcna602248)).

*Inference:* the price is set by risk premium (insurance, freight, attack headlines), not by lost barrels. That makes it unusually sensitive to signals. A credible deal could compress it within days. A post-vote strike that triggers Iran's threatened hits on Gulf infrastructure would add the barrels back to the problem.

The engine's war reactions frame the equity side. In the 10 sessions after the three strike onsets (3/2, 7/8, 8/31), **Brent rose 22-38% and SPY moved 0% to -2.5%**. In the 10 sessions after stand-downs, **SPY rose 0.9-7.9%** ([engine outlook](20261023_market_outlook.md)).

### The engine's restraint lens applied to now

The engine's 10/23 backtest (doctrine 16.20) found three things ([doctrine](../.claude/skills/jiang-doctrine/SKILL.md)):
- Decider-incentive priors beat simple persistence only in the political stretch of the war. Every week from 9/16 to 10/7 was forecast quiet and stayed quiet.
- Every earlier stand-down broke on an Iranian trigger: attacks on US ships (5/7), on bases (6/10-6/28), on vessels (7/7) and on US troops (8/31).
- Pauses lasted 29, 31 and 43+ days.

Rule R37 refuses any strike claim above 0.5 inside a known suppress window with no live trigger. It also states that a lapsed window "raises the hazard, it does not fire it" ([constraint calendar](../data/constraint_calendar.json)). The current pause began in **early September**. AFP reports "neither side has launched major attacks on land-based targets since early September" ([Bangkok Post/AFP](https://www.bangkokpost.com/world/3342763/signs-point-to-renewed-major-us-military-ops-against-iran)). The engine dates the start to 9/10, but researchers found no source for that exact day.

*Inference:* by 11/3 the pause will be about eight weeks old, the longest of the war. It is held there by a stated pledge, not by any change in the fundamentals. The live trigger to watch is not a decision in Washington. It is whether the IRGC carries out its threat against escort ships or US bases.

## Act III: oil's other dealers, OPEC+ without the UAE, Russia's diesel bargain, China's truce

### OPEC+ cannot add barrels its members cannot ship

OPEC+ is a smaller and weaker cartel than the brief assumed. **The UAE left OPEC and OPEC+ effective 5/1/2026**, after a long dispute over its 3.5 mb/d quota. ADNOC targets 5 mb/d of capacity by 2027 ([Enerdata](https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/uae-announces-exit-opec-effective-1-may-2026-after-59-years.html)). On **10/4** a seven-member core (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan, Oman) held November quotas steady. Those seven pumped **25.0 mb/d in August, about 5 mb/d below February's pre-war level**, and well below quota. Reuters reported that changes were unlikely before 2027 and a capacity audit ([Reuters via StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/OPEC++agrees+to+keep+November+oil+output+targets+steady/27144369.html)). *Inference:* the 11/1 meeting is low-signal, because Gulf output is capped by war, not by quota. The 11/29 meeting matters for the post-war price path. With the UAE free to pump without a quota once Hormuz normalises, a peace outcome is more bearish for oil than in past cycles, because Saudi Arabia's ability to defend price is weaker. The engine's 0.55 that quotas stay unchanged on 11/1 looks too low against Reuters' "unlikely before 2027".

### Moscow trades diesel for time

Putin wants sanctions relief without a ceasefire. The White House wants Russian diesel for a US distillate market that was **32% below its five-year average on the East Coast** in September ([EIA STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/)). Around **10/9** the US agreed to lift sanctions on Russian fuel. Re-tightening would revive the Graham tariffs on China and India just as the US-China truce nears its own 1/10 cliff. Putin's proposed Trump-Xi-Putin summit, which Trump is reportedly open to ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/19/trump-summit-xi-putin-denuclearization-ukraine)), adds a further wildcard for oil and defence names.

## Act IV: Warsh's Fed and a synchronized global squeeze set the price of money

### The Fed under Warsh hikes into an oil shock

Warsh wants credibility without forward guidance. "I don't prejudge meetings," he said on 10/16 ([Axios via search](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/16/warsh-federal-reserve-interest-rates)). The data give both sides of the mandate something to point at. A post-midterm loss for the Republicans would raise White House pressure to cut. Any sign the Fed bends would show up first in the term premium, which is already about **0.80% (ACM) to 0.99% (Kim-Wright)** ([MacroMicro](https://en.macromicro.me/series/20480/us-10-treasury-term-premium); [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/THREEFYTP10)).

### Treasury plumbing and the gold paradox

Official buyers are leaving the Treasury market. Japan's holdings fell to **$1.068tn in August** as it spent reserves defending the yen, and China's to an 18-year low. Total foreign holdings still rose to about $9.33tn, implying that private and leveraged buyers are filling the gap ([Economy.ac](https://economy.ac/news/2026/10/202610295902)). Treasury has kept coupon sizes unchanged "for at least the next several quarters" and adjusts through bills, which made up 22.2% of marketable debt on 7/31 ([Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0590)). Its next financing estimates come on **11/2** and the refunding statement on **11/4**, straddling the vote ([Treasury QRA](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/financing-the-government/quarterly-refunding/most-recent-quarterly-refunding-documents)).

*Inference:* yen intervention near 160 means Japan selling dollar reserves, which is bearish for US duration. A fast yen rally, set off by a December BoJ hike or falling US yields, would force deleveraging of yen-funded positions in high-beta US tech. That is the August-2024 template, and it would hit the same semis complex already under pressure.

The same chain explains why gold falls during a war. An oil shock raises inflation expectations and Fed-hike odds. That lifts real yields, which are about **2.65-2.76% on the 10-year TIPS** ([TreasuryRateWatch](https://treasuryratewatch.com/daily/tips-real-yields)), and supports the dollar. Higher real yields overwhelm the safe-haven bid, even with record ETF tonnage and central-bank buying (289 t in Q2) ([Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/gold-etf-allocation-retirement-october-2026/)). *Inference:* gold should behave very differently across the scenarios. A strike that lifts yields keeps it capped. A growth scare that drags yields down, or any visible erosion of Fed independence, releases it.

## Conclusion

The new insight from today's research is that the deadlines are linked, not merely stacked. The 11/3 vote ends three different restraints at once: Trump's pledge, Iran's incentive to inflict pre-election pain, and the Fed's reluctance to hike before Election Day. It hands decisions to a caretaker Netanyahu who will remain in office through December whatever Israel's voters do. The market therefore faces a single regime change on 11/4, not a series of separate events. Its outcome depends less on what Washington decides than on whether Tehran provokes, which the engine's own backtest identifies as the way every earlier pause ended. Oil is the transmission belt, and today it is stretched tighter than the engine's 10/23 numbers assumed. The AI complex now depends on the bond market, so even a contained strike reaches chip stocks through CPI and yields rather than through the Gulf.

The less obvious implication is about the right tail. A peace deal is the one move on the board that would cut energy earnings, the largest single contributor to this year's 29.5% EPS growth, while easing the yields that are squeezing AI funding. *Inference:* the index-level gain from de-escalation is therefore smaller than the oil move would suggest, and it would come with a sharp rotation back from energy toward long-duration growth. The engine's mixture median near SPY 760 already reflects that offset. The corrections this research called for were applied to the book on 10/25. The ECB, BoJ, OPEC+ and Fed nodes were repriced, the China truce node was reworded, and the restraint calendar was re-sourced. The apparent oil gap turned out to be a contract roll rather than a stale price.
