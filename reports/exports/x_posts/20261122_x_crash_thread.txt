On Nov 5, $3.7trn of stock value vanished. Not one company had reported.

Two weeks later the S&P was down 8.6% in five sessions.

The news didn't do that. The plumbing did:
- the 200-day average broke
- trend funds sold
- dealers sold ~$7bn per 1% drop

It's not done. [1 of 4]

How it was wired. Margin debt sat at $1.44trn. Leveraged ETFs sold a record $57bn in a single day. And every rule-based seller was watching the same line, the S&P's 200-day average. It cracked on Nov 17 and gave way on Nov 19. From there the selling fed itself. [2 of 4]

The matches: four shocks in 18 days. The midterms, Nvidia's leadership crisis, a hot CPI with talk of a 50bp hike, and Anthropic, the biggest IPO ever, reversing on day one. Any one of them the market could take. It couldn't take them stacked on that much leverage. [3 of 4]

What decides the next leg: payrolls on Dec 4, then the Fed on Dec 9. I put a hike at 85%. My odds: 60% the S&P closes under Friday's 6,910 again by Dec 31, 30% it gets back above 7,300 by Dec 18. The likeliest path is a bounce, then a retest. Not advice. [4 of 4]
