# Call the AI Prints, Not the Price

GT-Analyst desk · 18 November 2026 · Analysis of direction and mechanism only. This is not investment advice and it contains no position sizes.

The AI trade can be forecast, but only in layers, and the layer most people want forecast, the share price, is the one the evidence says cannot be. Reported revenue and next-quarter guides measured against a company's own guidance are highly predictable; buyers' capex plans are predictable about one guidance cycle ahead; the state of the financing chain is measurable and leads everything else; government decisions are not forecastable with confidence; and price direction is a no-call. On that basis we publish thirteen dated calls between 70% and 92% on prints, capex guides, credit states and one bounded price level, and we publish the direction of AI equities to mid-2027 only as scenario odds: 35% repair, 40% range, 25% second leg down, measured on the SMH semiconductor fund's close on 30 June 2027. The master rule is never to infer price from a print: on 17 November 2026 Nvidia beat consensus revenue by $6.7bn and its last after-hours trade was up 0.9%. History adds two laws that organise the rest. Equities peak before capex does, and the depth of the eventual capex cut scales with how much of the build was financed externally (debt-funded builders cut 50-80%, cash-funded hyperscalers 2%). The marginal AI builder now sits on the debt-funded side of all five of our regime tests, so if this cycle fails, it fails first in the bond market and not in the order book.

Dates are 2026 unless stated. Figures marked † rest on secondary sources, newsletters or search summaries and have not been checked against a primary document; treat them as indicative.

## Five layers, five different levels of predictability

The question "what happens to the AI trade" is five questions with different answers, and most forecasting errors come from answering one with evidence about another. We sort every item into a layer before forming a view, and each layer permits a different kind of call.

| Layer | What it covers | Predictability | What we will call |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Prints | Reported revenue and next-quarter guides, against the company's own guidance | High | Threshold calls at 80-92% |
| 2. Capex plans | Buyers' forward spending guidance | Good one guidance cycle ahead, low beyond | "No cut" and threshold calls at 65-90%, never above 65% past one cycle |
| 3. Financing | Debt, leases, guarantees, vendor finance, ratings, the IPO window | Medium; the leading layer and the first point of failure | State calls: a default, a rating, a deal clearing |
| 4. Constraints and policy | Power, packaging, export rules, tariffs | Power is measurable; government decisions are not forecastable with confidence | Measured outcomes; decisions capped at 35-65%, which mostly means no call |
| 5. Price | Index and share-price direction and levels | Low | No call, except a bounded statement with a base rate of 85% or more, or an underperformance call of up to 65% once two financing triggers have fired |

### Prints are predictable because companies clear their own bar

Large technology companies beat expectations as a matter of routine. The S&P 500's ten-year EPS beat rate is 75%† ([Benzinga, citing FactSet](https://www.benzinga.com/analyst-stock-ratings/analyst-color/25/06/45758803/sp-500-q1-earnings-stun-again-the-street-with-broad-based-beats-double-digit-growth)), six of the Magnificent 7 beat in the first quarter of 2025 and all seven in the first quarter of 2026 ([FactSet, 21 May 2026](https://advantage.factset.com/hubfs/Website/Resources%20Section/Research%20Desk/Earnings%20Insight/EarningsInsight_052126.pdf)). The sharper regularity is against the company's own guide. **Nvidia reported $115.84bn on 17 November against its own guide of $108.0bn plus or minus 2%**, 7.3% above the midpoint ([Nvidia press release, 17 Nov](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-third-quarter-fiscal-2027); [CNBC, 17 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). Micron's 30 September report came in at $54.2bn against a $51.07bn consensus ([Micron 8-K, 30 Sep](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723125/000072312526000018/a2026q4ex991-pressrelease.htm)). Every supplier that has reported since September guided up: Lam put 2027 wafer-fab equipment spending above $200bn and Applied Materials expects growth of more than 30% ([Applied Materials, 12 Nov](https://ir.appliedmaterials.com/news-releases/news-release-details/applied-materials-announces-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-year-2026); [Lam call highlights, 21 Oct](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9136007/lam-research-corp-lrcx-q1-2027-earnings-call-highlights-record-revenue-firstever-40-operating-margin-and-a-bullish-200-billion-wfe-outlook)).

Every threshold in the table is therefore the company's own guide, never an aggregator's consensus. The reason is practical: in mid-November two vendor pages still showed Micron's next-quarter revenue at $57.24bn and $56.6bn, below the bottom of the company's own guided range of $60.0-63.0bn, while a third showed $63.79bn ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/quote/MU/earnings-forecasts); [Earnings Whispers](https://www.earningswhispers.com/epsdetails/MU); [Chartmill](https://www.chartmill.com/stock/quote/MU/estimates)). A call struck against stale consensus measures the data vendor, not the company.

### Price is not predictable from prints, valuation or concentration

A beat is the expected outcome, so it carries little information about the reaction. **Nvidia has closed higher the day after earnings only 55% of the time since 2016**†, and over the eight reports to May 2026 the average next-day move was -0.9% with only a quarter positive†; February 2026 was the fourth straight decline after a beat-and-raise† ([Motley Fool, 22 May 2026](https://fool.com/investing/2026/05/22/nvidia-just-crushed-earnings-estimates-but-the-sto); [Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/229873/nvidias-earnings-on-nov-19-what-history-tells-us-about-nvidia-stocks-moves-following-earnings-releases)). The 17 November print is the live example. Revenue beat consensus by $6.7bn ($115.84bn against $109.10bn) and the $130.0bn guide stood $5bn above the $125.1bn consensus ([CNBC, 17 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). Reports through the evening put the after-hours gain anywhere from 1% to 5%; the last extended-hours trade we have, at 9:33 pm ET, was $156.88, up 0.9%, against an options-implied move of about 8% ([Robinhood NVDA](https://robinhood.com/stocks/NVDA); [Invezz, 17 Nov](https://invezz.com/news/2026/11/17/nvidia-stock-faces-a-300-billion-earnings-gamble-as-options-signal-8-swing/)). The same quarter supplied three more cases: Alphabet fell about 12.6% on the day it reported Google Cloud revenue up 103% ([MarketAnalysis tracker, 1 Nov](https://marketanalysis.com/ai-capex-tracker-november-2026/)), Micron slipped after hours on 30 September after beating consensus revenue by $3.1bn ([Investing.com transcript, 30 Sep](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-micron-tops-q4-2026-estimates-as-demand-stays-hot-93CH-4925992)), and ASML fell on a beat ([Investing.com transcript, 14 Oct](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-asml-tops-q3-2026-estimates-but-shares-fall-93CH-4948118)).

Valuation does not rescue a price forecast at this horizon. Regressing ten-year real returns on the cyclically adjusted P/E gives an R-squared of 29% ([Dimensional](https://www.dimensional.com/insights/cape-fear-should-investors-be-concerned-with-market-valuations)); at one year it is about 0.05† ([eco3min](https://eco3min.fr/en/qa/valuations-matter-long-term/)). Index concentration explains 4-7% of 6- and 12-month forward returns ([State Street](https://ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/insights/market-concentration-risk-and-how-to-diversify-now)). So the fact that Nvidia trades near 12 times forward earnings, its lowest since 2011-12† ([TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262300737-nvidia-stock-nvda-forward-pe-drops-11-7x-hitting-15-year-low-tradingkey)), is not a call. In memory it can be the opposite of one: Micron traded at 4-5 times forward earnings at its 2018 peak† ([UncoverAlpha](https://www.uncoveralpha.com/p/every-memory-cycle-ends-the-same)).

What the research does support forecasting is crash risk rather than direction. Greenwood, Shleifer and You find that a sharp industry run-up does not on average predict low returns, but the probability of a 40% drawdown within two years rises to about 53% after a 100% net-of-market run-up and about 80% after 150% ([NBER w23191](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23191/w23191.pdf)). The date of the peak is exactly what that result says cannot be known.

Positioning is a mixed signal and we do not lean on it. The November Bank of America survey (taken 6-12 November) shows the net technology overweight falling to 12% from 37%, the largest one-month drop since June 2006, yet cash is only 3.6% and semiconductors are still named the most crowded trade by 39% of respondents† ([Startup Fortune, citing the survey](https://startupfortune.com/fund-managers-cut-tech-the-most-since-2006-but-they-still-own-it/)).

## History's two laws put financing ahead of demand

### Equities peak before capex, so rising capex guides do not mark a floor

In every past boom with usable dates, the equity peak came before the capex peak and never after the capex decline began. UK railway shares peaked on 6 October 1845† and railway spending ran at about 7% of GDP in 1847† ([Campbell, CEPR](https://cepr.org/article/railway-mania-not-so-great-expectations); [Odlyzko](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/mania18.pdf)). Cisco's record close was 27 March 2000† ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/cisco-stock-still-isnt-back-to-its-2000-high-chart-of-the-week-123018268.html)), inside the year US telecom capex peaked at $121bn before falling to $49bn by 2002 ([Doms, FRBSF](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/er19-34bk.pdf)). Nvidia fell 54% in the fourth quarter of 2018† before hyperscale capex turned 2% negative in the first two quarters of 2019†, and the Philadelphia semiconductor index bottomed on 14 October 2022† before the 2023 capex cuts ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/05/12/chip-stocks-are-bleeding-today-the-2018-and-2022-selloffs-tell-you-exactly-what-comes-next/); [Synergy Research](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/hyperscale-operator-capex-takes-a-breather-in-q1-down-2-from-2018); [QuantFlowLab](https://quantflowlab.com/?p=936)). That is four episodes of four; shale, the 1870s railroads and bitcoin mining lack verified equity dates.

The consequence explains what looks like a contradiction in our table. We put 88% on no hyperscaler guiding 2027 capex below 2026 and 90% on SMH failing to regain its high by mid-2027. Falling chip equities alongside rising capex guides is the normal late-cycle pattern. The semiconductor index closed 34.3% below its 22 June record on 16 November while the four largest buyers guide 2026 capex up about 77% ([24/7 Wall St, 17 Nov](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/17/chip-stocks-are-having-their-worst-two-months-since-2008/); [YieldTheory](https://www.yieldtheory.app/research/hyperscaler-capex-2027-forecast)). Capex data is a lagging confirmation and cannot be used to call a price floor.

### The depth of the cut scales with external financing

Debt-funded builders cut hard and cash-funded ones barely cut. US telecom carriers cut capex about 60% from 2000 to 2002 and competitive local carriers nearly 80% ([Doms, FRBSF](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/er19-34bk.pdf)); shale upstream capex fell 51% in three quarters from late 2014† ([Natural Gas Intelligence](https://www.naturalgasintel.com/?p=235228)). **Cash-funded hyperscalers cut 2% year on year for two quarters in 2019**, and full-year 2019 capex still rose about 1%† ([Synergy Research](https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/hyperscale-operator-capex-takes-a-breather-in-q1-down-2-from-2018)). The variable to track is therefore the funding mix, not demand, and the regime test in the next section is how we track it.

Intensity is already at the levels of the telecom peak. Carriers spent 30-40% of revenue at the 2000 peak against a 20% norm (one trade-press quote†, [Lightwave](https://www.lightwaveonline.com/business/earnings-statements/article/16648149/surviving-the-crunch-in-capital-expenditures)); Meta spent 44% and Microsoft 39% in 2025† ([Platformonomics](https://platformonomics.com/?p=34926)), and UBS puts 2026 hyperscaler capex near 100% of operating cash flow against a ten-year average of 40%† ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/ai-hyperscalers-159b-debt-issuance/)). In the latest reported quarter, capex exceeded operating cash flow at Oracle, Alphabet ($51.4bn against $42.3bn) and Meta ($34.91bn against $32.79bn); only Microsoft covered its capex ($45.2bn against $58.0bn) ([MarketAnalysis tracker, 1 Nov](https://marketanalysis.com/ai-capex-tracker-november-2026/); [sec-api Meta Q3](https://sec-api.io/insights/financial-analysis-of-meta-platforms-meta-q3-2026-earnings)).

### Vendor financing is the classic tell, and the $530bn figure needs correcting

Lucent's customer financing commitments were about 24% of revenue at the peak and unwound in roughly 15 months with $3.5bn of provisions† ([Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/)). Nvidia now plays the equivalent role at far larger scale, and a widely repeated number misstates it. **Nvidia's "$530bn" is total commitments plus guarantees, not guarantees.** In the 10-Q for the quarter to 26 July 2026 it is a third-party sum of $366bn of commitments, $56bn of additional commitments and $108.5bn of maximum exposure under guarantees ([Nvidia 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm); [AI Weekly, relaying SemiAnalysis, 11 Sep](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/nvidias-off-balance-sheet-backstops-hit-530b-in-latest-10-q)†). Guarantees alone were $108.5bn, and about $118bn at 25 October per press reading of the filing ([CNBC, 17 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). About $164.5bn is customer credit support, dominated by one $105bn lease guarantee on a campus leased to OpenAI† ([XenoSpectrum, 20 Oct](https://xenospectrum.com/en/nvidia-customer-backstop-equity-ratio/)). The largest block, $279bn, is Nvidia's own supply-chain purchase commitments, which are a claim on Nvidia if demand falls and not a backstop to customers.

The measurable test is the next 10-Q: flat or shrinking support lines would mean withdrawal. The October-quarter filing had not been located at the time of writing, so the $118bn figure rests on press. Management has changed too. Nvidia announced its chief executive's resignation on 12 November ([Nvidia 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm)), and the interim chief executive said on the 17 November call that guidance considers "customers' access to capital" ([CNBC, 17 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). The vendor's willingness to extend its balance sheet has gone from an assumed constant to a variable.

### Three further base rates, and one rule we do not use

Semiconductor drawdowns have a shape: seven completed drawdowns of 20% or more in the Philadelphia index since 2004 had a median depth of 35%, a median nine months from peak to trough and a median five months from trough to a new high† ([QuantFlowLab](https://quantflowlab.com/?p=936)). Memory equities lead memory fundamentals by one to two quarters and fall 50-60% per cycle† ([UncoverAlpha](https://www.uncoveralpha.com/p/every-memory-cycle-ends-the-same)). Hiking cycles alone do not sink technology shares: the Nasdaq was flat to up in four of five cycles since 1994, with 2022 the exception† ([CME Group](https://www.cmegroup.com/openmarkets/equity-index/2022/How-Rising-Rates-Could-Influence-Tech-Earnings.html)). In 2026 itself SMH rose 16% from the Fed's 16 September hike to 5 October while the 10-year yield climbed to 5.31% ([Robinhood SMH](https://robinhood.com/stocks/SMH); [24/7 Wall St, 29 Oct](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/29/the-10-year-treasury-yield-just-jumped-back-above-5/)). Rates matter to this trade through refinancing cost, not through a discount-rate reflex.

The rule we decline to use is "stocks peak when growth peaks". Truist finds semiconductor stocks have topped about one quarter after revenue growth peaks†, while memory in 2018 and a practitioner study point the other way† ([StockWireX summary of Truist, 20 Oct](https://stockwirex.com/?p=51733)). The growth-rate peak opens a risk window; it does not date a top.

## The marginal builder reads five of five on the debt-funded side

The second law makes the funding regime the most important classification in the framework. We test it five ways.

| Test | Cash-funded reading | Debt-funded reading | Reading on 18 Nov 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyperscaler free cash flow | Positive | Negative | Negative at Alphabet and Meta in the September quarter, at Oracle in its August quarter and at Amazon over twelve months; Microsoft positive |
| Bond issuance against forecast | At or below | Above | $296bn year to date against Goldman's $250bn full-year forecast |
| Off-balance-sheet vehicles | Rare | Pricing wider each deal | 6.58% (2025) to above 7% (July 2026); QTS placed about $300m of $800m |
| The weakest borrower's access | Open | Shut | No high-yield data-center deal since late September; CoreWeave five-year CDS about 1,113bp |
| The vendor as guarantor | No | Yes | Nvidia guarantees of $108.5bn (July) to about $118bn (October, press) |

All five read debt-funded for the marginal builder. Hyperscalers had sold $296bn of bonds by early November, and Goldman forecasts $440bn in 2027 plus $300bn of project-level issuance ([CNBC, 6 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/coreweave-bond-yield-tops-16percent-as-ai-credit-stress-rises.html)); other tallies run from $244bn to $371bn on different scopes and dates†. Goldman estimates about 36% of 2026 capex is debt-funded against 24% in 2025† ([Exencial, on Goldman, 16 Nov](https://exencialrp.substack.com/p/goldman-sachs-says-hyperscalers-can)). Meta's Louisiana vehicle priced at 6.58% in 2025 and its El Paso vehicle needed "north of 7%" in July 2026 for the same tenant ([Capacity, 24 Jul](https://capacityglobal.com/news/metas-12bn-el-paso-bond-exposes-ai-debt-repricing/)). Blackstone's QTS launched an $800m placement on 26 October and had raised about $300m by 13 November† ([ZeroHedge, relaying Bloomberg and bank desks](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/first-junk-now-investment-grade-blackstones-own-data-center-giant-cant-sell-800-million)). CoreWeave's five-year CDS peaked at 1,289bp on 5 November and stood near 1,113bp on 17 November ([CNBC, 6 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/coreweave-bond-yield-tops-16percent-as-ai-credit-stress-rises.html); [CNBC, 17 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/apollo-ceo-marc-rowan-on-ai-debt.html)).

The reading is a mixture, not a verdict on the whole sector. The four big buyers still have balance sheets, so the neoclouds and single-tenant vehicles sit in the 50-80% cut class if access stays shut and the four hyperscalers sit somewhere between that and 2019's 2%. Any statement about aggregate capex has to say which class it describes. The repricing is running from the outside in: hyperscaler corporate bonds still clear in size at rising concessions, single-tenant project bonds have repriced by more than 40 basis points for the same tenant, and paper without a hyperscaler behind it is being cut or pulled. Broad credit is not stressed. The investment-grade index sat near 89bp in early November while AI-linked credit traded about 44bp wide of it, against 10bp tight a year earlier† ([ZeroHedge, relaying Goldman and Barclays](https://www.zerohedge.com/markets/first-junk-now-investment-grade-blackstones-own-data-center-giant-cant-sell-800-million)).

The official sector now describes the same structure. The IMF's October report counts 14 circular transactions since 2023 with about $300bn of equity, $650bn of associated debt and $1.4tn of future revenue commitments ([IMF GFSR, Oct 2026, ch. 1](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/gfsr/2026/october/english/ch1.pdf)). The Fed's 13 November stability report has 87% of surveyed contacts citing AI as a salient risk, up from 50% in May† ([wire relay](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/world/40073507)). There have been no defaults, and the only major rating action is Oracle's July cut. That is weak comfort, because only two of 27 data-center bonds counted by Barclays finance fully operating sites† (same relay), so most of the stock has not yet faced a payment test.

We watch ten gauges. Each has a level, a tightening trigger and a loosening trigger. Two or more tightening triggers at one review raise the odds of the financing-accident branch by at least 10 points; two or more loosening triggers raise the repair branch by at least 10 points. On the 17 November readings no trigger on either side has fired.

## The evidence for and against each call

### Supplier prints: Broadcom, TSMC and Nvidia

**Broadcom (82%, resolves 11 December).** Broadcom reports on 9 December, a date the company has confirmed ([Broadcom IR](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcom-inc-announce-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-year-2026)). AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7bn in the quarter to 2 August, up 54% on the prior quarter, and the company guided $21.7bn for the quarter to 1 November, a further 30% ([Broadcom press release](https://investors.broadcom.com/node/64671/pdf)). The call needs only that the next guide exceeds whatever the November quarter delivered. In its favour, no supplier in the chain has guided down, and Anthropic's prospectus lists about $161.2bn of Broadcom equipment leases† ([Implicator, on the S-1](https://www.implicator.ai/anthropic-ipo-prospectus-518-billion-compute-commitments.md)). Against it, custom chips are lumpy by customer, the 16 November export rule gives management a reason to withhold or hedge a number, and Broadcom's CDS widened 49% in the month to 6 November ([CNBC, 6 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/coreweave-bond-yield-tops-16percent-as-ai-credit-stress-rises.html)). It sits below 90% because it is a sequential-growth test, not a company clearing its own midpoint. The report lands on the same evening as the Fed decision, which will muddy the price reaction and has no bearing on the call.

**TSMC revenue (90%, resolves 22 January 2027).** TSMC guided fourth-quarter revenue of $52.3-54.3bn on 15 October at an assumed exchange rate of 31.8 ([TSMC 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001046179/000104617926000726/a3q26e_withguidancexfinal.htm)). **October revenue was a record NT$586.89bn, 34.6% of the midpoint in one month** ([TSMC 6-K, 10 Nov](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001046179/000104617926000796/tsm-revenue20261110.htm)). If November and December merely hold October's level the quarter lands above the top of the range. The case against deserves precision. On our arithmetic the midpoint needs November and December to average about NT$554bn at the guided exchange rate, which is 6% below October but about 8% above the August-September level of roughly NT$513bn. A slide back to that earlier level would miss even the bottom of the range. One research strand put the requirement at "about NT$540bn a month to reach the top of the range"; that figure does not reconcile with the guide and we do not use it. The published "analyst range" of NT$538-570bn a month is simply the guide's low and high ends restated ([InvestingCube](https://www.investingcube.com/shares/tsmc-october-revenue-jumps-59-7-can-the-chipmaker-beat-its-q4-forecast/)). The monthly releases around 10 December and 8-11 January will settle most of this before the earnings call; a November print below about NT$510bn would be the first hard evidence of a supply-chain slowdown.

**TSMC capex (70%, resolves 22 January 2027).** The 2026 budget is $60-64bn, so a $68bn midpoint is about 10% growth. Management said on 15 October that 2026-2028 spending will be "significantly higher" than the prior three years and that AI accelerator revenue growth will "probably be higher" than previously guided ([TSMC call transcript, 15 Oct](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/15/tsmc-tsm-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Equipment makers' 2027 outlooks point the same way, and one broker preview floated $75-80bn† ([Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/analytics/traders-blog/tsmc-earnings-q3-2026-preview)). Against that, TSMC said it was "too early" to discuss later years, and it has the option of holding capex flat if its customers' financing tightens. This is a capex-plan call one cycle ahead, hence 70%.

**Nvidia (90%, resolves 10 March 2027).** The guide is $130.0bn plus or minus 3% and assumes no data-center compute revenue from China ([Nvidia press release, 17 Nov](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-third-quarter-fiscal-2027)). The company has just cleared its previous midpoint by 7.3%, Rubin systems have begun shipping, and customers in the five countries named in the 16 November rule are at most 1.3% of revenue† ([Techi](https://www.techi.com/us-ai-chip-export-rule-nvidia-revenue-exposure/)). The risks are specific. The quarter has 14 weeks, so the underlying weekly run-rate rises only about 4% against a 12% headline step. Four direct customers each account for 11-13% of revenue. Neocloud commitments of $32bn are contingent on financing ([CNBC, 17 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q3-2027.html)). The licensing rule's 90-day clock for existing data centers runs to roughly mid-February if it starts at publication. The report date is a pattern estimate of late February to early March.

### Memory: three calls on one cycle

Memory is where the cycle shows first, so we hold one high-confidence print call and two lower-confidence cycle tests.

**Micron revenue (86%, resolves 22 December).** Micron guided $61.5bn plus or minus $1.5bn on 30 September, after a quarter that beat consensus by $3.1bn, and said agreements cover "the vast majority" of calendar-2027 high-bandwidth memory supply at significantly higher prices ([Micron prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). TrendForce raised its fourth-quarter DRAM contract forecast from +10-15% to about +20% during the quarter ([TrendForce, 30 Sep](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260930-13258.html); [TrendForce, 20 Oct](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/10/20/news-dram-prices-seen-rising-20-qoq-in-4q26-despite-slower-gains-ai-demand)). The risk is timing: shipments slipping across the quarter-end. The 15 December date is a vendor estimate.

**Micron's next guide and the 1Q27 contract price (72% each).** These are the memory-cycle test, and the evidence is balanced enough that we stop at 72%. For continued gains: SK Hynix reports firm contracts on more than half of 2027-28 capacity, Samsung says 2027 high-bandwidth memory is effectively sold out† ([Tech-Insider, 29 Oct](https://tech-insider.org/samsung-q3-2026-chip-profit-miss-107-44-trillion-won-ai-hbm4/)), and KB Securities puts supply at 60-70% of demand in 2027-28† ([BigGo, 18 Nov](https://finance.biggo.com/news/e13f55ed-f761-4111-bd34-f48b91722d7e); [BigGo, 30 Oct](https://finance.biggo.com/news/873d9ecf-d02b-4f2f-99ea-a8169e870b8e)). High-bandwidth memory is priced annually and TrendForce expects it to rise sharply in 2027 ([TrendForce, 2 Jun](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260602-13074.html)). Against: the gains are narrowing. PC DRAM contracts are up 13-18% "amid tense negotiations" and some PC makers are revising fourth- and first-quarter procurement† ([Xenospectrum, on TrendForce 11 Nov](https://xenospectrum.com/en/pc-dram-procurement-supply-plan/)). One pre-earnings estimate had Samsung's DRAM price gain slowing from 16.5% in the third quarter to 5.4% in the fourth† ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/samsung-heads-for-100-trillion-won-profit-milestone-in-q3-but-stronger-won-may-limit-upside-surprise/12896150)). Operating margins near 80% leave little room to rise. The equities have already moved. Micron closed 43% below its June high on 17 November ([Robinhood MU](https://robinhood.com/stocks/MU)), and Samsung and SK Hynix are down about 55% on average† ([BigGo, 18 Nov](https://finance.biggo.com/news/e13f55ed-f761-4111-bd34-f48b91722d7e)). If memory shares lead fundamentals by one to two quarters as in past cycles, a peak in pricing falls somewhere between now and mid-2027. No negative contract-price quarter or customer inventory build has printed in any source we read, which is why the calls sit above the no-call zone. A US price-fixing class action that survived a motion to dismiss on 14 November, with answers due 7 December, is a slow overhang that raises the cost of further supply discipline ([TrendForce, 16 Nov](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/16/news-u-s-judge-rejects-samsung-sk-hynix-microns-dram-price-fixing-dismissal-bid-citing-plausible-collusion/)).

### Hyperscaler capex: the first numbers for 2027 arrive in late January

No hyperscaler has given a 2027 number. Alphabet says capex will "increase significantly", Amazon will spend "meaningfully more" than about $220bn, Microsoft expects capex to accelerate in the first half of calendar 2027, and Meta will keep investing "aggressively" ([YieldTheory](https://www.yieldtheory.app/research/hyperscaler-capex-2027-forecast); [AI Weekly, on Amazon](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/amazons-aws-growth-hits-39-in-q3-as-capex-guide-jolts-stock)).

**Microsoft December quarter (87%, resolves 5 February 2027).** Management said on 28 October that December-quarter capex would exceed $50bn, on the basis that includes finance leases, and reaching its calendar-2026 figure of about $170bn requires about $51.9bn ([BigGo call summary, 28 Oct](https://finance.biggo.com/news/US_MSFT_2026-10-28); [YieldTheory](https://www.yieldtheory.app/research/hyperscaler-capex-2027-forecast)). The reason this is not 92% is that Microsoft missed the same kind of number last quarter: $45.2bn against a $50.67bn consensus, blamed on delayed third-party capacity† ([DataStudios](https://www.datastudios.org/post/microsoft-q1-fy27-results-azure-48-percent-33-2-billion-capex-45-2-billion)). It has trimmed its full-year figure twice. The constraint is supply and power, not intent.

**No guide below 2026 (88%, resolves 12 February 2027).** The four companies must spend about $218.5bn in the fourth quarter to hit 2026 guidance, an annualised exit rate of $873.8bn against a 2026 total near $730bn. Merely holding the fourth-quarter pace is about 20% growth in 2027 ([YieldTheory](https://www.yieldtheory.app/research/hyperscaler-capex-2027-forecast)). Cloud growth accelerated at all three large providers in the third quarter, and backlog at the four largest cloud sellers sums to about $2.5tn on reported figures ([Alphabet 8-K exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000165204426000080/googexhibit991q32026.htm); [DataStudios, on Microsoft](https://www.datastudios.org/post/microsoft-q1-fy27-results-azure-48-percent-33-2-billion-capex-45-2-billion); [AI Weekly, on Amazon](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/amazons-aws-growth-hits-39-in-q3-as-capex-guide-jolts-stock)). The opposing evidence is real. **52% of fund managers expect at least one hyperscaler to announce a capex cut within six months**† ([Startup Fortune, on the BofA survey](https://startupfortune.com/fund-managers-cut-tech-the-most-since-2006-but-they-still-own-it/)), although a trimmed plan is a looser test than a 2027 guide below 2026. Bank of America sees aggregate hyperscaler free cash flow at -$64bn in 2026 and -$144bn in 2027† ([TFTC](https://www.tftc.io/ai-capex-cash-flow-negative-2027-peak-hyperscalers)). Microsoft's contracted backlog went flat on the quarter, the first stall in the series. The backlog is also not independent demand: a large share is OpenAI and Anthropic commitments. Microsoft guides by fiscal year, so its statement will have to be read on its own terms.

**Alphabet (70%, resolves 12 February 2027).** The 2026 guide is $195-205bn, so $240bn is 20% growth on the midpoint. Hitting 2026 guidance implies a fourth quarter of about $68bn, an annualised pace of $272bn, already above the threshold; the only per-company consensus found, an August snapshot that predates third-quarter results, had $284.8bn ([YieldTheory](https://www.yieldtheory.app/research/hyperscaler-capex-2027-forecast)). The case against is the balance sheet. Free cash flow was -$9.1bn in the third quarter, long-term debt was $125.1bn against $21.6bn a year earlier, the company sold $12.4bn of stock, and the shares fell about 12.6% on the report ([MarketAnalysis tracker, 1 Nov](https://marketanalysis.com/ai-capex-tracker-november-2026/)). Management could trade capex for cash flow or give only words. Either would count against the call.

Beyond the first guides the picture is much less certain, and we publish it as scenario odds, not calls: **another year of 30% or more growth in 2027 hyperscaler capex 40%; a slowdown to under 25% growth 40%; an outright decline 20%.** These odds describe how 2027 spending turns out, which is a different question from the first guides given by 12 February. Street estimates for the five largest buyers run from about $1.1tn to $1.3tn for 2027†, and no published estimate we found puts digestion in 2027; the recent dissent (SemiAnalysis cutting 2028 accelerator capex forecasts by 28%†, Bank of America's free-cash-flow trough) points to 2028 ([24/7 Wall St, 17 Nov](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/17/chip-stocks-are-having-their-worst-two-months-since-2008/)). That timing would be decided by credit markets. Reported lab revenue is growing fast but is small against the spend: Anthropic's run-rate was about $81bn at 31 October and OpenAI's about $50bn at end-September, against roughly $800bn of 2026 capex for the five largest buyers ([24/7 Wall St, 11 Nov](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/11/anthropic-says-its-revenue-run-rate-jumped-to-an-81-billion-run-rate/); [TechCrunch, 11 Nov](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/openais-quarterly-revenue-reportedly-crosses-10b-for-the-first-time/)).

### Credit states: CoreWeave and Oracle are stressed, not failing

**CoreWeave (92%, resolves 31 March 2027).** The market prices distress: unsecured bonds yield 15.66% and the CDS implied about 63% five-year default odds in mid-October† ([CNBC, 17 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/apollo-ceo-marc-rowan-on-ai-debt.html); [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/coreweave-debt-60-percent-default-odds-104-billion-backlog)). Distress over five years is not default in four and a half months. On a flat hazard rate, 63% over five years is about 7% to the end of March, close to our 8%; that conversion is our arithmetic and ignores the risk premium in CDS prices. The company reported on 10 November that it will not use the unsecured market at current yields, expects about $9bn of customer prepayments over three months, has repaid more than $4bn of debt from contract cash flows, and funds remaining build-out through contract-matched secured loans† ([Constellation Research, 10 Nov](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/coreweaves-q3-shows-revenue-surge-ceo-aims-allay-debt-worries)). Its first debt-service coverage test is 31 October 2027† ([Tiger Brokers brief](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/2600095668)). The pressure points are also dated. Principal of $2.4bn falls due in the rest of 2026 and about $11bn in 2027† ([FourWeekMBA, on the 10-Q](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-coreweaves-2027-debt-payments-jump-77-to-11b-10-q-shows/)). Fourth-quarter interest is guided at $1.05-1.15bn, against full-year adjusted operating income guidance of about $1.3bn. The whole structure leans on Nvidia: Apollo's chief executive said on 17 November that if Nvidia's guarantor function is removed, "access to capital for a large number of neoclouds is going to disappear" ([CNBC, 17 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/apollo-ceo-marc-rowan-on-ai-debt.html)). No neocloud has defaulted to date.

**Oracle (85%, resolves 31 March 2027).** Oracle is one notch above junk at S&P (BBB-, stable, since July), Baa2 with a negative outlook at Moody's and BBB stable at Fitch, the latter two affirmed in early November ([IFR, 7-13 Nov](https://www.ifre.com/topic-codes/2509450/cds-market-flashes-warning-on-ai-ratings-downgrades)). A junk rating by March needs S&P to move from a stable outlook or Moody's to cut two notches. Oracle has said keeping the rating is its first priority† ([Wealth Professional](https://www.wealthprofessional.ca/investments/equity-markets/oracles-default-insurance-hits-high-yield-levels-as-ai-debt-mounts/393817)) and has acted on it, selling $10bn of stock at an 11.2% discount alongside a $10bn bond in November after a $20bn at-the-market programme† ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/oracle-10b-bond-sale-share-offering)). Rating agencies also lag the market. Against the call, its CDS near 300bp trades at a single-B-equivalent level, fiscal 2026 free cash flow was -$23.7bn†, and S&P estimates roughly half of its contracted backlog is OpenAI†, whose payments reportedly begin in 2027 ([Let's Data Science, 5 Aug](https://letsdatascience.com/news/oracle-credit-risk-rises-amid-ai-buildout-e40ec988); [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/sps-downgrade-leaves-oracle-financing-openais-bet-years-before-it-pays-off/)). OpenAI's November funding round was cut to about $20bn at $1.25tn from $30bn at about $1.4tn, with about $11bn in a first close† ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/openai-closes-11b-first-tranche-at-1-25t-valuation-well-below-its-1-4t-ask/)). Oracle's credit is a levered claim on OpenAI's ability to fund itself, which is why an OpenAI payment deferral is the flip condition. Oracle's December report is the next test.

## Price: one bounded call and three branches

### SMH below its 2026 high on 30 June 2027 (90%)

This is the only price statement we make, and it is a bounded one. SMH closed at 460.10 on 17 November, 31.5% below its 22 June high of 671.83 and 12% below its 200-day average ([Robinhood SMH](https://robinhood.com/stocks/SMH)). Regaining the high by 30 June requires a 46% rise in seven and a half months.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 Jul 2007 | 20 Nov 2008 | -68.7% | 16.2 months | 62.1 months |
| 27 Dec 2021 | 14 Oct 2022 | -46.5% | 9.6 months | 14.0 months |
| 10 Jul 2024 | 8 Apr 2025 | -39.7% | 8.9 months | 5.1 months |
| 19 Feb 2020 | 18 Mar 2020 | -35.0% | 0.9 months | 2.6 months |
| 1 Feb 2006 | 21 Jul 2006 | -29.3% | 5.6 months | 11.9 months |
| 12 Mar 2018 | 24 Dec 2018 | -26.0% | 9.4 months | 3.3 months |
| 1 Jun 2015 | 11 Feb 2016 | -25.1% | 8.4 months | 5.4 months |

Philadelphia semiconductor index drawdowns of 20% or more, 2004-2026, closing basis† ([QuantFlowLab](https://quantflowlab.com/?p=936)).

The case for the call: the drawdown is five months old against a median nine months from peak to trough, so a trough is not established; the 17 November session made a marginal new intraday low. The index is already at the median depth. The October 2022 trough, made while the Fed was still raising rates, took 14 months to recover, and the Fed is hiking now, with a December increase about 95% priced on 17 November ([CNBC rates, 17 Nov](https://cnbc.com/2026/11/17/treasury-yields-bonds-inflation.html)). The one drawdown tied to a credit event took 62 months, and this one has a credit dimension the 2018 and 2024-25 episodes lacked.

The case against is in the same table, and it is the strongest counter-evidence to any of our calls. Four of the seven drawdowns regained their highs within 5.4 months of the trough. The 2020 and 2024-25 recoveries each required gains larger than the 46% needed here and delivered them in 2.6 and 5.1 months. If the low is already in, the raw table alone would not support 90%. Our probability rests on the trough being unconfirmed, the Fed hiking, and the weakest borrowers being shut out of the bond market. Rate cuts, or a capex surprise combined with credit repair, would break it.


No branch clears 60%, which is what "no call" means here. The levels have anchors: 520 is 13% above the 17 November close and near the 200-day average of 524.45, and 400 is 13% below. A repeat of the 2022 hiking-cycle drawdown of 46.5% from the high would put SMH near 360, close to its March 2026 low of 359.86 ([Robinhood SMH](https://robinhood.com/stocks/SMH)). The tree exists to be moved by the indicator board, and its moves are the product: the first 2027 capex guides, the reopening or continued closure of the high-yield window, CoreWeave's CDS against 700bp and 1,289bp, and Nvidia's next 10-Q each shift weight between branches. We would make a directional underperformance call, at no more than 65%, only after two tightening triggers have fired with our credit-stress composite above its threshold. Neither condition holds today.

**The Anthropic listing as a financing gauge.** Terms set on 10 November are 825 million shares at $70-78, up to about $64.4bn, with a $26bn concurrent private placement to suppliers and partners; pricing is expected on the evening of 18 November and first trade on 19 November, and neither outcome was known at the time of writing ([Renaissance Capital, 10 Nov](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/122657/anthropic-sets-terms-for-61-billion-ipo-targeting-a-1.2-trillion-market-cap); [Newsquawk](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/anthropic-ant-expects-ipo-price-of-usd-70-78shr-to-sell-80135mln-ipo-shares-with-the-co-seeking-to-raise-usd-644bln-to-price-on-november-18th-and-trade-on-november-19th)). Books were reported six to seven times covered† ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/172403-anthropic-ipo-six-times-oversubscribed-as-it-targets-64-4b-raise/)). Its relevance to the chain is mechanical: the proceeds land at a counterparty on which several neocloud backlogs and supplier leases partly depend, at a time when the equity window is shut for the periphery (Solidigm, Firmus, AirTrunk and SB Energy listings were shelved in October†; [CNBC, 23 Oct](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/ai-turmoil-neoclouds-struggle-credit-risks-jump-solidigm-pulls-ipo.html); [Serrari Group, 16 Nov](https://serrarigroup.com/ai-data-center-junk-bonds-hit-37bn-as-credit-risks-rise/)). We say nothing about where the shares trade.

**Export policy.** The record of the past year shows reversals in both directions. The administration licensed H200 sales to China for a 25% revenue share† ([Asia Times, 17 Nov](https://asiatimes.com/2026/11/china-hoards-nvidias-ai-chips-as-us-curbs-remote-access/)), chip controls were largely absent from the September summit's deliverables, and then Commerce issued a tightening rule on 16 November that the bureau says was written at the direction of Treasury and the National Security Council ([CNBC, 16 Nov](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/nvidia-commerce-china-lutnick.html); [Korea Times, 27 Sep](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20260927/why-us-chip-controls-took-a-back-seat-at-xi-trump-summit)). US officials say export controls are not on the table in Shenzhen ([Reuters, 16 Nov](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/chip-tensions-loom-over-trumpxi-summit-as-us-and-china-push-ai-safety-steps-5028728)). A rule issued days before a leaders' meeting is a bargaining chip until it survives the meeting. We treat its fate as a coin-flip-range question and publish no call.

**A correction on the "China data center curb".** The Reuters exclusive of 17 November under that label describes a Federal Communications Commission plan to block imports of Chinese data-center equipment such as optical transceivers; it is not a chip rule and it "could still get shelved" ([Reuters, 17 Nov](https://lufkindailynews.com/news_reuters/business/exclusive-trumps-china-data-center-curb-advances-despite-amazon-google-meta-lobbying/article_28f30955-41a7-5c98-a4f7-6ff5d8fe92ca.html)). The chip rule is the 16 November Commerce interim final rule, which requires a license to ship advanced chips to data centers serving China-headquartered firms above a compute threshold and adds license requirements for Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil ([TrendForce, 17 Nov](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/17/news-u-s-targets-chinas-ai-compute-loophole-tightens-chip-exports-to-malaysia-singapore-and-three-others/)). The first is a supply-side risk to US build cost and schedule; the second is a demand-side limit on third-country buyers. The other correction, on Nvidia's $530bn, is set out above: it is total commitments plus guarantees, of which guarantees were $108.5bn.

**Power.** Power is the measurable half of the constraints layer. PJM cleared at its price cap in three consecutive auctions and was 6,831 MW short of its reliability requirement for 2028/29 ([PJM Inside Lines](https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-capacity-auction-procures-138318-mw-of-generation-resources-as-work-continues-to-address-growing-electricity-demand/)). From the 2029/30 auction, PJM's board plan excludes new large loads above the prior forecast from auction demand until their capacity is offered ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/pjm-board-backstop-capacity-auction-data-center-curtailment/826347/)). That changes the demand side of the auction, so three cap-clears under the old rules do not give a base rate for the fourth; one research strand treated a fourth cap-clear as the base case and we do not adopt that. Even the date is uncertain: one calendar gives results on 22 December and another lists 9 December† ([ESAI calendar](https://www.esaipower.com/capacity-market-auction-calendar/pjm-capacity-auction-calendar/)). On deployment, estimates of 2027 slippage range from a few gigawatts to about 15 GW on incompatible definitions† ([Tom's Hardware, 18 Oct](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/300-plus-local-data-center-bans-have-only-delayed-just-three-us-projects-bans-much-more-bark-than-bite-only-2-3-gigawatts-of-data-centers-are-held-up-versus-38-gigawatts-expected-online-in-2027); [Sightline, via ABC17, 19 Oct](https://abc17news.com/stacker-technology/2026/10/19/tech-companies-need-more-data-centers-so-why-are-nearly-half-of-all-planned-projects-delayed-or-canceled/)), and the constraint bites hardest on 2028-29 additions. The policy swing factor is state-level. Restrictions passed in 46 of 47 local data-center ballot measures in 2026† ([Ballotpedia, 15 Nov](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/11/15/voters-have-decided-on-47-measures-related-to-data-centers-in-2026-in-december-they-will-consider-four-more/)), Texas has frozen new large-load approvals, and the governor-elect has pledged to keep the freeze ([AP, via ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Technology/wireStory/data-centers-beating-midterm-elections-reckoning-ahead-tech-137902293); [MLQ, on ERCOT](https://mlq.ai/news/texas-data-centers-face-a-december-limbo-as-ercots-batch-zero-review-meets-a-change-of-governor-what-it-means/)†). These are government decisions, so they sit in the no-call half.

## Conclusion

The framework's claim is narrower than "the AI trade is predictable" and more useful: the trade's observable facts arrive in a known order, and the order tells you what each fact can and cannot say. Prints confirm demand that was already contracted. Capex guides confirm intent one cycle out. Neither says anything reliable about price, because equities have historically turned before capex and because the cut, when it comes, is set by who financed the build. That moves the analyst's attention from order books, where every number is still rising, to a short list of credit gauges, where the first failure would appear. On those gauges the marginal builder is already debt-funded, yet no trigger has fired and no borrower has missed a payment. The evidence supports confident calls on prints and credit states through March, a bounded statement on the old high, and no call on direction.

Three limits apply. Every rule here was fitted to research done in 2026 and has not yet been tested forward. We will score it by layer: if fewer than 80% of print calls hit once ten have resolved, the premise that prints are predictable has failed and that confidence band comes down to 65-80%. Much of the historical record behind the two laws rests on secondary sources, and the vendor-finance and semiconductor-drawdown statistics in particular should be checked against primary data before they carry more weight. The call most exposed to its reference class is the 90% on SMH, where the raw recovery record is more favourable to a rebound than our probability; a miss there would mean the trough was already in.
