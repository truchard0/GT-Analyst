## 1. PREDICTION DISCIPLINE (the meta-method)

This is the most transferable part of Jiang's approach and should govern every output the agent produces:

1. **Work only from public information.** No claimed inside knowledge. The analysis must be reproducible from the newsfeed itself.
2. **State predictions explicitly and falsifiably** — binary or near-binary outcomes with rough time horizons ("ground invasion: yes, within 1–2 years"), not vibes.
3. **Stake the theory on the predictions.** Jiang's own standard: if the linked predictions miss, the underlying model is wrong — go back and diagnose the reasoning failure rather than rationalizing.
4. **Framework, not prophecy.** Outputs are "this might happen for these structural reasons," held with explicit uncertainty. When outcomes diverge, the divergence is data.
5. **Chain predictions to a single model.** He derives multiple predictions from one structural analysis so the model is testable as a whole.

Agent implication: every analysis ends with (a) numbered predictions, (b) probability estimates, (c) falsification conditions, (d) a log entry for later scoring.

## 2. THE LAW OF ESCALATION

**Core claim: control beats dominance.** Escalation dominance (having the bigger weapons / higher rungs available) is the naive metric. The decisive variable is **calibration** — the ability to time, shape, and target actions to serve a strategic objective while preserving options.

Key components:

- **Ladders are climbed sequentially, not skipped.** Emotional, political, and justificatory pressures force step-by-step escalation. Diagnostic use: if commentators claim an actor will jump several rungs (e.g., straight to nuclear use), check whether the intermediate rungs (e.g., secret weapons, chem/bio) have appeared. No intermediate rungs → discount the jump scenario heavily.
- **Standard state escalation ladder (attacker side):** decapitation strikes → military targets → economic embargo/blockade → civilian infrastructure → "secret weapons" demonstrations → chemical/biological → nuclear. Map where each actor currently sits.
- **Ladders are asymmetric.** Each player has a different ladder with different ceilings. Model both separately, then compare *option richness*, not ceiling height.
- **Decision trees vs. ladders.** A player with a clear objective can build a branching, selective decision tree (e.g., graduated pressure that exempts some parties, tolls others, punishes others) rather than a blunt linear ladder. Selectivity converts coercion into alliance-restructuring.
- **Three winning attributes:** focus (unity of purpose), clarity (knowing how to achieve it), resolve (willingness to bear costs). Assess each actor on all three; societal correlates are forced unity/suppression of dissent, censorship, and mobilization toward total-war economics — all observable in news flow.
- **Four dimensions of conflict: narrative, political, economic, military — with military typically least decisive.** Every escalation step must be justified across all four (world opinion, alliance relations, trade continuity, troop/public morale). These constraints are why "just nuke them" logic fails.
- **The credibility trap (bully dynamic).** A hegemon's power rests on the perceived certainty and cost of its punishment. A challenger who absorbs punishment visibly and cheaply destroys that credibility, forcing the hegemon to either retreat (status collapse) or overcommit (self-destruction). Calibrated provocation can therefore *steer* a stronger player into strategically ruinous moves. The challenger holds the initiative (active); the credibility-bound hegemon reacts (passive).
- **Active vs. passive; clear vs. ambiguous objectives.** An actor whose war aim is vague ("destroy X") cannot calibrate. An actor with a concrete objective (control a chokepoint, expel a rival's forward presence) can. Ambiguity of stated objective is a bearish indicator for that actor's campaign.

## 3. ATTRITION & FORCE-STRUCTURE LOGIC (cost pyramid)

Real wars are attrition contests, so sustainable force composition must match production cost: cheap/replaceable mass at the base (infantry), progressively expensive assets above (armor → naval → air). An **inverted pyramid** (air-dominant, thin ground mass) wins only "video-game wars" against non-resisting opponents; against a resilient enemy it forces a choice between quick loss-cutting or reversion to a real pyramid (mass ground forces, draft, industrial mobilization). Watch for **mission creep** as the mechanism of that reversion.

News-flow indicators: munitions production announcements, draft/recruitment policy, shipbuilding/industrial mobilization, casualty tolerance rhetoric.

## 4. THE CHESSBOARD ACTOR MODEL

Model every significant player as a chess set:

- **King = political system.** Wars are won by putting the *political system* in check, not by defeating the military. Each system has a characteristic failure mode: democracies → polarization/civil conflict; autocracies → succession crisis and elite defection against the leader; hybrid democracy-theocracies → factional civil war; theocracies → extremist splintering. Adversaries attack the failure mode, not the army.
- **Queen = grand strategy.** How the actor conceives of dominating its environment. Crucially, grand strategy **constrains** as much as it enables — players can only act within its contours, which is what makes them predictable. Actors *without* a grand strategy (in Jiang's account, e.g., civilizationally isolationist powers) cannot mobilize for total war and are modeled as environment, not protagonists. (Treat this specific classification as a contestable assumption — see §6.)
- **Bishops/Knights/Rooks = attack vectors.** The concrete instruments: technology & precision strike, reserve-currency financing, media/narrative reach, geography, faith/ideology, proxies, intelligence services, diaspora networks.
- **Pawns = expendables.** What the actor will sacrifice without political cost: vassal allies, soldiers, proxies, drones/missiles. Identifying the pawns tells you where escalation lands first.
- **Grand strategy is a cultural expression.** It is rooted in the civilization's core texts and self-narrative (achievement/rebellion vs. duty/submission vs. martyrdom/eschatology). Use cultural priors to estimate pain tolerance, surrender propensity, and preferred victory conditions — e.g., martyrdom traditions predict non-surrender under bombardment.
- **Allies are separate players.** Nominal allies routinely hold *conflicting* optimal strategies (one may benefit from the other's exhaustion or defeat). Never merge allied players into a bloc; derive each actor's individually optimal play and look for divergence — that divergence is often where the highest-value predictions live.
- **Structural desperation predicts risk-seeking.** A player whose status-quo trajectory is terminal (resource depletion, demographic decline, fading relevance) rationally embraces chaos and long-shot gambles, because the expected value of the status quo is worse. (This maps cleanly onto prospect theory's loss-domain risk-seeking — a strong convergence between Jiang and behavioral literature.)

## 5. MACRO-REGIME LAYER

Background models Jiang runs beneath event analysis:

- **Hegemonic decay framework.** A hegemony rests on identifiable pillars (military supremacy, ideological/scientific prestige, reserve currency). Track pillar erosion independently; regime change in the world order follows multi-pillar decay, not single events.
- **Maritime vs. heartland logic (Mackinder-derived).** A naval hegemon's core imperative is preventing continental consolidation (rail/land trade integration among Eurasian powers). Predicts intervention wherever heartland integration advances; predicts chokepoint conflicts (straits, canals) as the sharpest flashpoints.
- **Chokepoint economics.** Control of trade arteries = control of the system. Chokepoint events (Hormuz, Red Sea, undersea cables, shadow-fleet seizures) get top analytical priority and have the most direct market transmission (energy, shipping, insurance).
- **Elite overproduction / internal-conflict driver (Turchin-style).** External conflict is substantially driven by intra-elite competition inside each state. Model domestic factional wars (e.g., nationalist/religious/tech coalitions vs. transnational finance) as a second game board that shapes the external one. (Useful lens; specific factional narratives vary in evidentiary quality — see §6.)
- **Multipolarity dynamics.** As hegemonic order decays, expect: rearmament of formerly constrained regional powers, opportunistic middle powers exploiting chaos, fluid/shifting alliances rather than stable blocs, and "resilience over efficiency" as the new state objective (reshoring, stockpiling, spiritual/national mobilization).
- **Systemic overextension corrects violently.** Unsustainable equilibria (population, debt, supply-chain efficiency) don't glide down; they snap. Position analysis for regime breaks, not mean reversion, when structural indicators are stretched.

**Signals Jiang explicitly monitors in news flow:** prediction-market positioning (large informed bets), censorship and information-control rollouts, mobilization/draft indicators, principal-level rhetoric shifts, who-is-meeting-whom diplomacy, infrastructure attacks (energy, water, desalination), and behavioral tells of populations under strain.

## 6. EPISTEMIC QUARANTINE (critical — read before deploying)

- **Tier A — Structural tools (high transfer value).** Everything in §§1–5 above as *method*: escalation calibration, actor decomposition, allied-divergence analysis, cost pyramids, falsifiable prediction discipline. - **Tier B — Contestable empirical claims (use as hypotheses only).** E.g., "China/India lack grand strategy and don't matter," specific war-outcome calls, "military is the least important dimension." Encode as priors with explicit uncertainty; test against outcomes.

The agent should recognize these claims when they appear in source material, may note them as *narratives circulating*, but must never adopt them as causal models or factual premises. Published criticism of Jiang notes reliance on selective historical analogy, unshown game-theoretic work, untestable assumptions, and a conspicuous omission of Chinese foreign policy from his analyses — build that skepticism in.

## 8. AGENT OUTPUT SCHEMA (per news event)

1. **Event** — one-line factual statement + source quality weighting
2. **Players** — each actor decomposed via chessboard model (king/queen/vectors/pawns); allies modeled separately
3. **Game structure** — type (bargaining, chicken, deterrence, attrition, stag hunt); complete vs. incomplete information; repeated vs. one-shot
4. **Ladder position** — where each actor sits on its escalation ladder; which rungs the event represents; skipped-rung check
5. **Four-dimension read** — narrative/political/economic/military implications of the move
6. **Objectives & divergence map** — each player's optimal play; where allies' interests split
7. **Predictions** — numbered, probabilistic, time-boxed
8. **Falsification conditions** — what observable event kills each prediction
9. **Market translation** — asset-class transmission (energy, defense, gold, FX, rates, shipping/insurance), priced-in assessment, horizon matching
10. **Log** — timestamped for calibration scoring

## 10. BATCH 2 ADDITIONS (from full GT#16 and GT#18)

### 10.1 Strategy-Direction Test (GT#16) — Tier A
For any actor at war (or in a coercive standoff), determine the *direction of conformity* between the military instrument and the other three dimensions:
- **Rigid pattern (bearish for that actor):** the actor forces narrative, political, and economic spheres to conform to a fixed military strategy — pressuring allies to comply, strong-arming media, distorting markets to protect the war plan. Predicts: no reflection, doubling down after setbacks, escalation into self-destructive options, brittle resupply because domestic/allied support erodes.
- **Adaptive pattern (bullish):** the actor calibrates military action to *serve* economic, political, and narrative objectives — selective enforcement (letting some parties' shipping pass, tolling others), coalition-splitting diplomacy, narrative positioning for global sympathy. Predicts: reflection, flexibility, resilience, growing recruitment of support over time.
Diagnostics are all news-observable: who is being ordered vs. courted, whether media is threatened vs. persuaded, whether enforcement is blanket vs. selective.

### 10.2 Sustainability Constraint Triad (GT#16) — Tier A
Any protracted campaign is bounded by three constraints; track each as an independent time series from news flow:
1. **Political will** — polling on war support, funding-bill friction, draft/mobilization resistance.
2. **Manufacturing capacity** — munitions depletion vs. production rates, replacement timelines for high-end platforms.
3. **Casualty tolerance** — transparency vs. suppression of casualty reporting (suppression signals *low* tolerance and political fragility).
An actor failing two of three cannot sustain; predicted behavior is either an exit-seeking pivot or a high-risk gamble to shorten the war.

### 10.3 Expensive-Platform Fragility (GT#16) — Tier B
Corollary to the cost pyramid: prestige platforms (top-end fighters, supercarriers, layered missile defense) are attrition liabilities against cheap drones/missiles — each loss is strategically cheap for the attacker and narratively catastrophic for the owner. Watch for asymmetric cost-exchange events (a $10K drone forcing a $1B asset's withdrawal); they mark inflection points in perceived power and in defense-sector market narratives.

### 10.4 The Audition Model (GT#16) — Tier B
When a hegemon visibly falters, model subordinate powers as *auditioning* to inherit its role. Audition criteria: demonstrated unity, capacity, and determination. The VC logic: backers rationally prefer the desperate, existentially committed candidate over the rich, diffident incumbent, because desperation aligns incentives. Predictive use: a junior partner conspicuously absorbing costs, showcasing cheap/asymmetric capabilities, and tolerating reputational damage may be signaling for succession rather than merely supporting the patron. Related **mercenary-succession pattern**: history's enforcer-clients repeatedly displace decadent patrons — treat as an analogy-prior only.

### 10.5 Three Rules of Coalition Dynamics (GT#16) — Tier B, high predictive value
1. **Strong respect strong, prey on weak.** Two players who both *prove* strength through conflict have a rational path to condominium — even sworn ideological enemies. Watch for tacit cooperation signals between "enemies" who have both demonstrated resilience.
2. **Ideology yields to structure.** Declared enmity is not a stable predictor; demonstrated capability distribution is.
3. **The weak don't coalition — they attach.** Weak players won't pool sovereignty even when arithmetic favors it; they individually seek strong patrons, then split when the patron falters. Predict partition of weak blocs, not consolidation.

### 10.6 Incentive Audit / Sanctions Paradox (GT#16) — Tier A
For every ongoing conflict, run a *cui bono* on continuation vs. termination for each player. Wars can produce perverse incentive flips — e.g., emergency de-sanctioning giving the targeted state windfall revenue and thus a rational preference for prolonging the conflict. When a player's economic position improves *because of* the war, discount all "seeking off-ramp" narratives about that player.

### 10.7 The Steelman Inversion (GT#18) — Tier A as hypothesis generator, never as conclusion
When a capable actor's behavior appears self-defeating, generate the inversion hypothesis: assume the "catastrophic outcome" is the *objective*, and re-derive the strategy that outcome would serve. Then test which hypothesis (incompetence vs. intent) better predicts subsequent moves. This is revealed-preference reasoning applied to states. Guardrails: incompetence, principal–agent conflict, and domestic-audience theater usually explain "irrational" moves better than grand design; the inversion earns weight only when it makes *differential predictions* that then verify.

### 10.8 Dependency Cascade Mapping (GT#18) — Tier A, highest market value
Chokepoint disruptions must be traced past energy into second-order input markets:
- **Food chain:** phosphate, ammonia, urea, sulfur → fertilizer → food prices → import-dependent-state instability.
- **Tech chain:** helium, sulfuric acid → semiconductor fabrication → AI capex narratives.
- **Redirection effect:** cutting one supply region doesn't destroy demand, it *redirects dependence* — identify the residual suppliers (who gain pricing power and political leverage) and the captive buyers (whose trade balances and currencies suffer). Overlay with sovereign-debt holdings: captive resource buyers who also finance the supplier's debt lose exit options — supporting continued demand for the supplier's bonds/currency.
- **Stability overlay:** freshwater and food self-sufficiency maps identify which regions absorb shocks vs. destabilize; upstream/downstream river control (e.g., Himalayan headwaters) is a standing flashpoint list.

### 10.9 Fortress/Autarky Doctrine Recognition (GT#18) — Tier B
Rival grand strategies now on the board are variants of "the system is collapsing; consolidate a self-sufficient bloc and let rivals absorb the collapse" (continental-heartland variant; hemispheric resource-bloc variant). Recognition markers in news flow: annexation/absorption pressure on resource-rich neighbors, reshoring mandates, war-economy conversion, autarky rhetoric. Under this doctrine, *chaos elsewhere is a benefit*, so de-escalation predictions premised on "chaos hurts everyone" fail — check whose doctrine treats disorder as tailwind.

### 10.10 War-Economy Conversion Index (GT#18) — Tier A
Track industrial composition: military vs. civilian output share, domestic production ramp of attritable systems (drones/munitions) vs. import substitution, reserves call-ups, multi-year procurement. A state converting to a war economy is structurally committed to *continued* conflict (the economy now requires it) — a strong prior against near-term peace-deal predictions for that state, and a durable tailwind thesis for its defense-adjacent supply chains.

### 10.11 Unconventional OSINT Signals (GT#18) — Tier B, corroborating only
Signals Jiang treats as leading indicators of imminent military action: late-night activity proxies around defense HQs (the "Pentagon pizza index"), abrupt social-behavior shifts in defense-workforce cities, and — most tradeable — prediction-market whales (large, conviction-sized bets suggesting informed positioning). Use only as corroboration on top of structural analysis, never standalone; all three are noisy and gameable.

### 10.12 Convergence Rule (GT#18, his stated meta-method) — Tier B
Jiang raises confidence when independent analytical vectors (structural/geopolitical, historical-pattern, and ideological/eschatological narratives held by the actors) all point to the same outcome. Agent translation: model actors' *own belief systems* as real decision inputs (what leaders believe matters even if false), and treat multi-lens agreement as a confidence multiplier — while logging that lens correlation can be spurious.

### 10.13 Batch 2 Tier C quarantine additions
GT#16/#18 embed: intelligence-agency authorship claims for terror groups asserted from coincidence patterns; "global elite/game masters/occult" system architecture; biblical-territorial project as literal state strategy; civilizational-values collapse determinism; and crude ideological characterizations. Recognize as narratives; never adopt as causal priors. Note also that both lectures analyze a US–Iran war scenario as ongoing fact — verify all empirical claims against primary reporting before the agent treats any of it as world-state.

## 11. BATCH 3 ADDITIONS (from full GT#15 and GT#13)

### 11.1 Pillar-Decay Model, fully specified (GT#15) — Tier B, macro backbone
The unipolar order rests on three pillars, each with a *characteristic decay mechanism*. Track all three as independent slow variables:
1. **Security guarantee (Pax Americana)** → decays through **hubris**: the hegemon starts violating its own rules-based order (unauthorized interventions, ignoring allied consensus). Indicator: unilateral action frequency.
2. **Ideological/scientific prestige** → decays into **orthodoxy**: the legitimating knowledge system shifts from innovation engine to suppression engine (credential gatekeeping, heresy policing, stagnant frontier innovation vs. mere scaling-out). Indicator: institutional-trust polling, dissent-suppression episodes.
3. **Reserve currency** → decays through **debasement**: unlimited issuance funds corruption, drives inequality, and produces mass exit behavior — the young rationally abandon wage accumulation for lottery-like speculation (crypto, sports betting, 0DTE). Indicator: retail gambling/speculation volumes as a *systemic-legitimacy gauge*, not just sentiment.
Regime-change signal = correlated decay across all three, not any single shock. Wars at the end of the cycle are symptoms (credibility defense), not causes.

### 11.2 Efficiency→Resilience Regime Shift (GT#15) — Tier B, portfolio-relevant
Definition pair: efficiency = optimize for the best case; resilience = survive the worst case. His claim: the operating objective of states is flipping from the first to the second. Downstream predictions if true: reshoring/stockpiling over just-in-time, mercantilist regional trade blocs (more expensive but functional trade, not autarky), de-urbanization pressure, nationalism/remilitarization as youth-mobilization technology, and religious revival as a governance tool for managing scarcity (spirituality substituting for material legitimacy). Market translation: persistent premium for redundancy plays (domestic capacity, logistics, defense, agriculture inputs) over efficiency plays (globalized JIT supply chains).

### 11.3 National Resilience Scorecard (GT#15) — Tier A as a screening tool
Score each state on: food self-sufficiency (fertilizer-import dependence is the hidden variable — ammonia/phosphate flows from North to South are what sustain 8B people), water security (weights heavier than food), energy import share, freedom/accountability (his mechanism: transparency determines whether populations accept crisis sacrifices; opacity → elite self-dealing → non-compliance), demographic structure (gerontocracy = decision sclerosis + immigration dependence → cultural-conflict feedback loop), and megacity concentration (specialization hubs = fragility under trade disruption). Low scores across food+water+freedom predict conflict, revolution, and out-migration; use the scorecard to rank sovereign risk and to anticipate migration-driven political shocks in destination countries.

### 11.4 Infrastructure Single-Points-of-Failure list (GT#15) — Tier A
Standing watch list where physical events have system-wide transmission: Strait of Hormuz (energy), undersea cables (20–30% of global internet; cloud-dependent finance is the real exposure, not streaming), fertilizer input chains, and civil aviation (a Pax-dependent luxury that reprices fast under conflict). Any newsfeed event touching these gets automatic priority escalation in the agent.

### 11.5 Constitutive vs. Distributive Conflict (GT#13, salvaged and reframed) — Tier A, high value
Distinguish two conflict types with different termination logic:
- **Distributive:** players fight over the division of surplus *within* an accepted system. Bargaining ranges exist; ceasefires and compromises are stable; standard Fearon logic applies.
- **Constitutive:** players fight over the *architecture of the system itself* (who sets rules, whose currency, whose credibility). Here interim ceasefires are inherently unstable because any settlement short of architectural resolution leaves the core dispute intact.
Diagnostic: if a challenger's attacks target system nodes (chokepoints, settlement rails, reserve-currency mechanisms) rather than territory, classify constitutive and *heavily discount peace-deal predictions and truce rallies*. This is the agent's rule for fading ceasefire headlines.

**11.5b Symmetric war-premium fade (added from eval Case 5 — Iran-Israel Apr-2024).** §11.5 fades *peace/ceasefire* optimism; its mirror fades *war-fear* spikes. Rule: **fade a telegraphed or pre-spiked WAR premium when no supply node is physically hit.** Diagnostics that force the fade (all as-of-date observable): the attack was *telegraphed* (days of warning — a state maximizing damage maximizes surprise; telegraphing maximizes the *signal* while minimizing *damage* → choreography for audience costs, escalation control not dominance, formal §10 item 8), it was *largely intercepted*, and oil/gold *pre-spiked on fear* with no facility struck (market-transmission → GENERIC/Branch A, not SUPPLY; §8 priced-in; §12.3 publicity multiplier amplifies the drama beyond the tiny real state-change). ACH diagnosticity (contrarian #1): defiance optics and "300 missiles!" are *non-diagnostic*; telegraphing + interception + adversary foreknowledge are the *diagnostic* items, and they're inconsistent with a genuine damage-maximizing first strike. → predict no rung-skip to broad war, calibrated/symbolic response, and **fade the oil/gold/vol spike** (buy-the-invasion, sell-the-retaliation). Do NOT confuse with a real SUPPLY disruption (Hormuz closure, Aramco hit) where the premium is physical and persists.

### 11.6 Credibility-Maintenance War Model (GT#13, stripped of metaphysics) — Tier B
A hegemon whose order rests on perceived invincibility must (a) periodically demonstrate punishment capacity, and (b) refuse to concede visibly lost conflicts, because the asset being defended is systemic credibility, not the local stake. Explains wars persisting far past local rationality. Corollaries: challengers "renegotiate" their position in the system by cheaply demonstrating the hegemon's fallibility; and expect maximum resistance to formal admissions of defeat (watch for reframing, "peace with honor" packaging, burden-shifting to allies) rather than clean exits.

### 11.7 Price-Hierarchy / System-Renegotiation Motive (GT#13) — Tier B
Model the global economy as a tiered pricing structure (resources priced cheapest → manufacturing → knowledge → finance priced richest). Resource exporters locked into the bottom tier have a standing structural motive to force renegotiation when the enforcement layer weakens — this predicts *which* states challenge and *when* (at hegemonic-credibility lows), independent of ideology. Pairs with 10.5's coalition rules.

### 11.8 Reconstruction/Asset-Positioning Tell (GT#13) — Tier B, tradeable
When politically connected actors visibly position for post-conflict spoils (frozen-asset recovery mandates, reconstruction concessions, redevelopment ventures) they reveal both expected outcomes and expected timelines of insiders. Track such positioning news as a revealed-expectations signal — same family as the Polymarket-whale indicator (10.11), and equally in need of corroboration.

## 12. BATCH 4 ADDITIONS (from full GT#21 — his most complete worked example)

### 12.1 Signal-vs-Strategy Rule (Tier A, core noise filter)
Principal-level daily rhetoric (a leader saying "peace soon" and "more bombs" within hours) is *deliberately or structurally noisy* — discard it as a prediction input. Anchor instead on the slow, expensive, hard-to-fake layer: published doctrine documents, budget trajectories, industrial contracts, procurement, force posture. Agent rule: rhetoric moves markets intraday but must never move the *thesis*; only capital-committed actions update the model. This is the formal version of "watch what the system does, not what the leader says."
