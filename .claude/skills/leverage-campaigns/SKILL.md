# LEVERAGE CAMPAIGNS - forecasting a decision someone stronger is trying to move
Built 23 Nov 2026. formal-game-theory wins on validity. Doctrine §14 binds: direction and mechanism, never sizing.
The war-price ban and the ai-trade price no-call are untouched by anything here.

## 0. WHY THIS EXISTS (the miss)
Nvidia's chief executive resigned on 12 Nov 2026 and was reinstated on 23 Nov, eleven days later. We had no branch
for his return. The only node that named him read "first full quarter without Huang". Four presidential statements
calling for his return were filed as noise under the rhetoric filter, and the board's "door shut" of 18 Nov was
taken as final. Every step was in our own feed, with timestamps (section 9).
The error was structural, not a bad number. A body's "no" is only as firm as the constraint behind it. When another
player owns that constraint, the forecast has to be about that player.

## 1. WHEN TO RUN IT
Run the map in section 2 when BOTH hold:
- a named body has taken, or faces, a discrete decision: remove or reinstate, approve or block, license or ban, sue
  or settle, list or delist, air or pull; and
- a stronger player has stated the outcome it wants, or plainly gains or loses from it.
Also run it on every shock a report names, to answer one question: who can reverse this, with what, and what would
we see first. A shock with no reversal branch is a wiring gap (item 43).
Do not run it on a quote with no decision attached.
The map and the ladder run whenever both conditions hold, whether or not any statement passes the gate in section 3.
The gate only decides whether a statement is itself an event. A statement that fails it still counts as R0.

## 2. THE LEVERAGE MAP (write it as a block in the analysis)
1. **Who decides.** The body, its voting rule, who sits on it, and the reason it has given for where it stands now.
2. **Who owns the reason.** Is the stated reason something another player determines: a security finding, a
   licence, a legal exposure, funding, a rating? If the principal owns the reason, the body's position is
   conditional on the principal, whatever the body says. This is the single most useful question in the module.
3. **The levers.** List each one the principal holds over the body and mark it used, threatened or idle:
   - *permission*: licences, approvals, clearances, contracts, access to a market;
   - *punishment*: investigations, enforcement, tariffs, litigation, loss of a contract;
   - *cover*: an official statement that removes the body's liability or its reputational cost for reversing;
   - *access*: direct contact, a seat or an observer, the power to convene;
   - *audience*: public statements that move the body's own shareholders, staff, customers or voters (formal §12.3).
   For each lever note what using it costs the principal. A lever that hurts the principal more than the body is weak.
   Mark each lever's DIRECTION too: does it push the body toward the principal's outcome, or raise the body's cost
   of reversing? A probe that deepens the subject's legal exposure cuts against reinstatement even though the
   principal's own agencies run it. When the principal's agencies pull both ways the principal is divided: say so
   and stay in the lower half of the band.
4. **The principal's stake.** Policy, political (an election, the level of the market, a negotiation abroad) or
   personal. Stake size sets persistence. Reported financial interests are cited as an incentive, with source and
   date, never as a proven motive.
5. **The body's payoffs.** The cost of holding out (lever pain, plus revolt by staff, shareholders or customers)
   against the cost of reversing (legal exposure, loss of face, other regulators). Then look for the
   **face-saving path**: a new committee, an apology, a finding of "no risk", conditions. A body reverses when it
   has a story it can tell. If there is no story yet, watch for one being built. Note how settled the alternative
   is: an interim in the seat is easy to undo, a named and seated successor is not.
6. **Veto players.** Who could block the outcome: courts, a legislature, a state regulator, a foreign government.
   Mark each as a binding veto or as voice only. Voice makes noise after the fact; only a binding veto stops it.
7. **Is the body one player.** Boards and cabinets split. Reports of a faction, a divided workforce or a leaked
   letter mean the body's "no" is a current majority, not a fixed preference (formal §10 item 1).

## 3. WHEN A LEADER'S STATEMENT IS NOT CHEAP TALK
Doctrine §12.1 and the Truth-Social rule stay the default: principal rhetoric is sentiment, not a thesis input.
A statement graduates to an analyzable event when ALL three hold:
- it names a specific body and a specific decision;
- the speaker holds at least one permission or punishment lever over that body, or owns its stated reason; and
- one of: it is repeated on two or more separate days; an official with authority over a lever echoes it or gives
  cover; or direct contact with the body is reported.
Then analyze the lever, not the words. The formal ground is already in the skills: a public demand ties the
speaker's hands (audience costs, formal §3), re-prices the body's payoffs, and gives a split body a focal point to
coordinate on. It is still coarse (formal §12.4): read "wants X and holds Y over them", never a date or a detail.
A U-turn by the principal is data. If the speaker accepted the outcome last week and demands its reversal this
week, ask what changed in the stake (section 2.4) and date it.

## 4. THE CAMPAIGN LADDER (observable rungs; no rung-skipping without a stated mechanism)
- **R0** the principal states a preference once.
- **R1** the demand is repeated or sharpened on a later day.
- **R2** an official whose office runs any lever in line 3 gives cover or makes the threat concrete, on the record.
  Cover that removes the body's stated reason is the strongest single rung.
- **R3** a face-saving path appears: the principal names a condition, or the body builds a structure that could
  clear the stated reason. A structure that could not clear it may entrench the decision; say which it is.
- **R4** direct contact is confirmed on the record or by a top-tier wire, or the body takes a formal step: a meeting
  notice, a filing, counsel retained. A press report of a faction is evidence for line 7 of the map, not R4.
- **R5** the decision.
Rungs arrive out of order. Record each with its date. A rung is SUPPORTED only when the ones it rests on have been
seen: R3 or R4 without R2 is read in the R1 band, at its upper end.
**Counter-rungs:** a formal refusal issued AFTER R2 (a refusal before R2 is not terminal); a veto player acts with
binding force; the principal's stake flips to another target; new facts raise the body's cost of reversing. A formal
refusal is on the record from the body or its chair. A report from unnamed sources is a reported refusal and weighs
less.
**Odds.** Start from the outside view (item 40). The bands below set the TIMELINE conditional for the reversal
branch. They are STARTING PRIORS from one case and general reasoning, and they are UNCALIBRATED. Replace them with
the scored record after five resolved campaigns.

| Highest SUPPORTED rung, levers present | Reversal branch |
|---|---|
| R0 | open the branch at 0.10 or more; do not leave it unwired |
| R1 | 0.20-0.35 |
| R2 | 0.35-0.50 |
| R3 on top of R2 | 0.50-0.65 |
| R4 on top of R2 | above 0.65 only with the execution signal cited |

- **Ledger.** A `decision` row must sit inside 0.35-0.65 unless an execution signal is cited. Below 0.35 the branch
  is wired on the timeline and no ledger row is written.
- **Execution signal** (the only thing that lifts the cap): on-record cover from the office that rules on the
  body's stated reason, or R4 as defined above.
- **First cycle:** start at the lower edge of the band for the highest supported rung. **Later cycles** (one
  analysis session each) move at most 0.10 toward the band.
- **Subtract** 0.10 for each binding veto player. For a protected body (section 7) use half the band. Floor 0.10.
- **Horizon.** The bands assume about 60 days from the latest rung. For a shorter question scale down and say so.

## 5. TIMING
Campaigns resolve at forcing dates, not at random. Look for the body's next scheduled meeting, a hearing, an
earnings date, a deadline the principal has named, and above all the principal's audience clock: a falling market,
an election, a summit. Decisions that move markets are often taken while markets are closed, so a weekend inside a
selloff is a window. State a window with its reason. Do not state a date the map cannot support.

## 6. WHAT ELSE THE MAP PREDICTS
- **Terms.** Reversals arrive with a structure attached: a committee, undertakings, conditions, sometimes a deal
  that pays the principal. Predict the terms as well as the outcome.
- **Residue.** The reversal is not the end state. Probes continue and players with voice keep the issue alive. Add
  the next branches the same cycle: the hearing, the state regulator, the investigation's conclusion.
- **Markets.** The reversal of a shock is itself a shock. Hand it to market-transmission and technical-stack for
  the channel and the positioning. No price call comes from this module.

## 7. GUARDS
- **This is not a plot model.** The mechanism library's rule on converging incentives stands. This module needs an
  overt principal, named levers and observable rungs. Claims of hidden coordination stay under Tier C hygiene.
- **Facts and motives are written differently.** A documented action ("said he spoke to the board directly", with
  source and time) is a fact. Intent is an inference and is written as one. Words such as "manipulated",
  "ordered" or "bought" are never stated as fact in a report without a primary source that says so.
- **Protected bodies resist.** Courts, a central bank with for-cause protection and agencies with statutory
  independence have a narrow win-set. Check formal §10 item 13 before applying the bands.
- **The principal can lose.** Pressure can harden a body that pays an audience cost for folding. Check who the
  body answers to before assuming it bends.
- **Stale items are archival.** The timeliness rule applies to every rung.

## 8. CHECKLIST (items 39-44; run after the mechanism sweep)
39. **Leverage gate.** Before any principal statement is filed as noise, were the three conditions in section 3
    tested and the result written down?
40. **Outside view first.** Reference class named with its count (how often this kind of body reverses under this
    kind of pressure) before the inside view. If we have no count, say "no base rate" and stay inside 0.35-0.65.
41. **Map complete.** All seven lines of section 2 filled, each lever marked used, threatened or idle, veto players
    marked binding or voice.
42. **Rung and counter-rung dated.** The highest rung seen and any counter-rung, each with the reported timestamp
    of the item that shows it.
43. **Reversal branch wired.** Every removal, ban, refusal or shock on the timeline has a sibling "reversed by
    <date>" branch with p of 0.10 or more and a shift edge to what its reversal would move. No dead ends.
44. **Wording check.** Documented actions stated with source and time; motives marked as inference; no intent
    claim stated as fact.
Ledger rows from this module carry `--kind decision` and the cluster tag `leverage-campaign` so the record can be
read on its own.

## 9. WORKED CASE: NVIDIA, 12-23 NOV 2026 (times UTC, from our feed)
Written after the outcome. It shows how to fill the map. It is not a test and does not count toward the record.
- **Who decides:** Nvidia's board. Stated reason for refusing a return: national-security exposure.
- **Who owns the reason:** the executive branch. It defines the security risk, runs the export licences and runs
  the criminal investigation into the Singapore reseller. The board's "no" was conditional on Washington.
- **Levers:** permission (export licences, the remote-access rule and its 90-day licensing clock), punishment (the
  criminal probe, the reported SEC review), cover (a statement that the security question is separate from the
  personal one), access (direct contact with directors), audience (public statements into a falling stock).
- **Stake:** an 8.6% week in the S&P 500 after the midterms, a Fed that was hiking, and a national-champion
  company without its founder. A report of 17 Nov said accounts linked to the President had traded Nvidia; that is
  cited as a reported incentive only.
- **Body's payoffs:** holding out cost staff unrest (exodus warnings on 17 Nov, pay rises promised on 18 Nov) and
  a falling stock. Reversing cost legal exposure and criticism in Congress. The face-saving path was the national
  security and export control committee the board set up on 17 Nov.
- **Veto players:** Congress and a state attorney general, both voice only. No court order. No binding veto.
- **One player?** No. A board faction was reported working for his return on 18 Nov.

| Reported | Item | Rung |
|---|---|---|
| 12 Nov 15:29 | The President names a different successor | preference is the opposite |
| 16 Nov 18:24 | The President calls for reinstatement | U-turn; R0 in the new direction |
| 17 Nov 11:21 | Board sets up a security and export control committee | structure for R3 exists |
| 17 Nov 16:33 | The former chief says he would be honored to return | the outcome is available |
| 18 Nov 05:11 | Board "shuts the door" | refusal BEFORE R2: not terminal |
| 18 Nov 13:30 | Board faction reported working for his return | body is split |
| 20 Nov 19:45 | The President urges him to apologize | R3: a condition is named |
| 21 Nov 01:49 | The President calls for reinstatement again | R1 |
| 22 Nov 19:07 | Treasury Secretary: export violations are separate from his private life | R2: cover on the stated reason |
| 23 Nov 01:54 | Report: the other party was in contact with a foreign spy agency | counter-evidence; raises the cost |
| 23 Nov 02:54 | The President posts that he hopes the board rehires him | R1 again, into the weekend window |
| 23 Nov 14:03 | Board reinstates him, unanimously, "citing no national security risk" | R5 |
| 23 Nov 15:14 | The President says he spoke to the board directly | R4, reported after the fact |

**What the map would have said.** By 18 Nov: open the branch, 0.20-0.35, because the principal owned the reason
and had reversed himself. By 22 Nov: 0.50-0.65, on R2 plus R3, with the weekend before Monday's open as the
window. It would not have given the day. **Residue to wire now:** the board's investigation, the Senate and House
hearings, the state attorney general, the SEC review and the criminal probe of the reseller.
