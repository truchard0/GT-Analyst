# MECHANISM LIBRARY - cause-and-effect rules, swept before ledgering

Built 18 Nov 2026 from outside commentary, restated in our own words. Origins live only in `PROVENANCE.md` and are
never cited. A rule is judged on its mechanism, indicator and record, not its origin. Doctrine §14 binds.

## 1. RUNNING THE SWEEP (checklist items 31-35)

31. **Tag** the item's ai-trade layer (L1-L5) and its domains below (A, S, X, W, R, M, G). Domain P (pressure on decision bodies, added 23 Nov 2026) is tagged whenever leverage-campaigns runs.
32. **Bite list:** every rule in those domains, plus any rule whose *Applies* line names the item's company, catalyst
    or date. A rule bites when a current reading of its indicator moves it.
33. **Read the indicators with dates.** Anything older than about 48h is standing context, not tape.
34. **Status gate.** ADOPT rules may move a timeline conditional or a conviction component, at most 0.10 per rule per
    cycle. SHADOW rules move nothing: write their implied direction and falsifier under "shadow rules" in the desk
    appendix so they can be scored. Two rules are two lenses only if their indicators are different data (contrarian
    #5).
35. **Conflict check (section 3), then write** each change as a proposed delta with a number, horizon and falsifier.
    No rule licenses an L5 price call or a war-driven price call.

**Promotion and demotion.** SHADOW becomes ADOPT after three resolved instances, two or more hits and no miss of the
rule's own shape; ADOPT drops to SHADOW after two same-shape misses. Log changes in `STATE.md`. Reports state the
mechanism in plain words, never rule IDs.

## 2. THE RULES

### A. AI demand and financing

**MX-A1 Lab revenue is a print** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* frontier labs' revenue run-rates fund the next round of compute, so a miss hits AI semis like a
  supplier's earnings miss.
- *Check:* run-rate against the street estimate; gross or net of partner revenue share (one definition switch cut a
  lab's perceived base 25-30%, Oct 2026); early signs (distillation losses, share shifting between labs, buyers
  building their own models). Next: one lab's December run-rate, estimated $86-90bn, due about January 2027.
- *Falsifier:* the five-day chip-index move takes the opposite sign to the surprise on two straight disclosures.
- *Record:* n=1 (Sept 2026 run-rate about $70bn against high-$70s expected; AI semis fell). Input only: no ANT call.

**MX-A2 Revenue has to catch the capex** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* AI infrastructure at about 3% of US GDP exceeds what the buyers' profits fund, so next year's capex
  depends on AI revenue growing into it. Expect slower spending growth, not a break; the supplier risk is a falling
  price per unit of compute, not volume. AI spend that replaces labour can rise while consumers slow, so a consumer
  slowdown alone is not digestion.
- *Check:* identifiable AI revenue against what the capex needs (about $150bn against $2.5tn); capex near 100% of
  operating cash flow (40% norm); usage outrunning software shipped; compute rents.
- *Falsifier:* all four big buyers guide 2027 capex up 30%+ and AI revenue keeps pace.
- *Record:* slowdown call open; the price warning was two years early (2024); the software leg hit in early 2026.

**MX-A3 The funding class sets the punishment** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* capex paid from free cash flow is tolerated; capex that turns it negative is bond-funded and punished
  in credit and equity whatever the cloud growth.
- *Check:* FCF after capex; CDS of cash-poor builders (36bp to about 120bp to Oct 2026) against cash-rich (29 to 48bp).
- *Falsifier:* a negative-FCF builder raises capex and its CDS tightens over the next month.
- *Record:* 2 of 2 in Q3 2026 (one negative-FCF builder lost about $500bn in a day; the positive-FCF one was received
  cleanly); fits ai-trade law 2. A funding-class classifier and L3 input; the equity reaction stays a no-call.

**MX-A4 Read neocloud stress at the contract, not the parent bond** - ADOPT (thresholds SHADOW)
- *Mechanism:* new neocloud loans are tied to investment-grade customer contracts and paid from them, and customers
  prepay; parent CDS prices old unsecured bonds and can overstate asset-level stress. The collateral test is GPU rent.
- *Check (Nov 2026):* new secured loans at 7-7.5%; prepayments 100% of capex on Nebius's Q3 deals; interest at 2.2x
  adjusted operating income (from 25x in Q1; healthy below 1.5x); connected against contracted MW; H100 rent
  $3.10/hr, +58% y/y (13 Nov); B300 index -7% from its Sept high (22 Oct).
- *Triggers:* distress if new loans price above 10% or prepayments fall below 50% of capex. Dark GPUs if the B300
  index is over 15% below its October high AND H100 rents fall below a six-year straight-line schedule.
- *Falsifier:* a neocloud defaults or restructures while all three contract gauges read healthy.
- *Record:* untested; measured states that fit ai-trade L3.

**MX-A5 Vendor-backed capacity ends on a rule or sponsor change** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* capacity built on a supplier's lease guarantees, or a subsidising rule, drifts from end demand and
  stops when the guarantor pulls back or the rule changes, not when demand fades.
- *Check:* Nvidia's guarantee and credit-support lines each filing ($108.5bn July 10-Q; about $118bn October, press);
  flat or lower means withdrawal. Rating, accounting or export changes touching guaranteed assets.
- *Falsifier:* support lines fall in two straight filings and the guaranteed builds stay on schedule.
- *Record:* extends ai-trade law 4 (one vendor-finance unwind took about 15 months); the rule-change trigger has one
  analogue outside AI.

**MX-A6 A founder's exit is an execution and sponsorship shock** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* a CEO who personally ran allocation, customer finance and Washington leaves; the supplier loses
  priority in its supply chain, sponsors weak customers less, and big buyers qualify alternatives.
- *Applies:* Nvidia for 12 months after the 12-13 Nov 2026 resignation.
- *Check:* senior departures; next-platform ramp timing; support lines (MX-A5); rival-accelerator wins; the 1-10 Dec
  hearings.
- *Falsifier:* support lines flat or higher and the next platform on schedule at the fiscal Q1 FY28 report (about
  May 2027).
- *Record:* the exit was anticipated a day or two early (n=1); consequences untested.

**MX-A7 Displacement losses travel through credit; gates before defaults** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* AI cuts the value of seat-priced software; software buyouts strain; private-credit funds gate
  withdrawals and pay interest in kind long before defaults; losses can reach the insurers holding the paper.
- *Check:* three gauges together: software price to FCF, high-yield spreads, private-credit net flows; gates;
  payment-in-kind share; insurer outlooks.
- *Falsifier:* by end-Feb 2027 the three gauges move together and private-credit lending does not contract.
- *Record:* one large fund gated (Feb 2026); open to Feb 2027; the software leg hit.

### S. Supply bottlenecks and power

**MX-S1 Bottleneck parts decouple from aggregate capex** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* content per system can rise 10-15x while capacity grows 2-3x a year, and scarce parts sell under
  take-or-pay with deposits, so a 25-30% capex cut can coexist with shortages in memory, lasers, capacitors,
  substrates and indium phosphide.
- *Check:* lead times (capacitors about 40 weeks); 2027 HBM and DRAM contract prices; share of output under
  long-term agreements; prepayments.
- *Falsifier:* a major memory or optics maker renegotiates an agreement or cuts a contract price within two quarters
  of a hyperscaler capex cut.
- *Record:* memory prints held through Q3 2026 while shares fell. It protects volume, not the pace of price gains:
  DRAM contract gains slowed from +80% to +40% to about +20% q/q, so the Micron lean-below stands.

**MX-S2 Delay costs more than dear power** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* in a one-MW project model, power +50% costs about 0.7 IRR points; a $2m/MW grid bill about 2; a 20%
  lower contract price about 9.5; a year's delay with idle GPUs over 10. Score policy and grid events in delay-years
  times idle capital; near-term capacity goes to sites with fast interconnects and on-site gas.
- *Check:* connected against contracted MW (under 20% at one neocloud); queue cuts (one state from 30 to 13 GW);
  transformer lead times (80-210 weeks).
- *Falsifier:* a funded project slips a year or more and its financing and contract are unchanged a quarter later.
- *Record:* model-based; consistent with Nebius's slip into 2027 and Microsoft's trim on third-party capacity.

**MX-S3 Local permission is a supply shock** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* rising household power bills and land fights make data centres an electoral issue; moratoria and
  freezes cut the supply of powered sites. That moves when capex lands, not demand, and makes powered capacity
  scarcer (rents up).
- *Check:* local measures (46 of 47 on 3 Nov 2026 favoured restriction); moratoria pending in about 14 states,
  statewide in New York; the Texas approval freeze, kept by the governor-elect sworn in 19 Jan; utility rate cases.
- *Falsifier:* fewer than 2 of Virginia, Texas, Georgia and Ohio restrict or reprice large data-centre approvals by
  31 Jul 2027.
- *Record:* leaning hit; a large campus abandoned Jul 2026; three independent readings converge.

**MX-S4 War reaches chips through inputs, with a lag** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* Gulf disruption reaches chips months later through fab helium and Taiwan's LNG-fired power.
- *Check:* Qatar LNG and helium (17% of LNG capacity lost); Taiwan gas stocks (10-11 days; gas about 48% of power);
  fab helium allocations.
- *Falsifier:* no helium- or power-linked allocation at a fab or packager by 30 Jun 2027 while disruption lasts.
- *Record:* none. Indicators only, never an energy or oil call.

### X. Export controls and China's levers

**MX-X1 Fast-biting measures draw retaliation; slow ones draw lobbying** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* Beijing answers US executive measures that bite within a quarter within about 60 days, usually with a
  mineral lever; statutes, phased rules and licence regimes get lobbying and leader-channel blunting.
- *Check:* lag to first bite; executive or statute; Chinese moves within 60 days.
- *Falsifier:* retaliation explicitly tied to a slow licence-based rule within 60 days.
- *Record:* consistent 2022-25 (no retaliation to phased rules; minerals after the acute 2025 tariffs); one dissent.
  The licence-based 16 Nov rule predicts low odds of tied retaliation before about 15 Jan.

**MX-X2 The counter-lever sits one layer down, and customs data show it first** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* China answers a US restriction in a lower layer where it holds 60-90%, near a negotiating cliff;
  informal throttling by firm and destination shows in customs data 1-3 months before any written rule. The cost
  lands on US build timing; the US answers with equity stakes and price floors.
- *Check:* indium phosphide (+250% since its Feb 2025 control); Chinese shares of isolators (90%), optical
  transceivers (two-thirds), rare-earth processing (90%); control-list additions; customs flows by destination.
- *Falsifier:* a US restriction round with no lower-layer move within 90 days.
- *Record:* 2025 mineral controls forced a tariff reversal; two customs-first cases (gallium to a neighbour went to
  zero; solar-tool flows through one hub fell 99% in a month).

**MX-X3 Controls tax the middlemen while the leader is supply-short** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* while the chip leader sells all it makes, China-access rules hit intermediaries (Southeast Asian
  neoclouds, server makers, brokers) and US labs bid for the freed capacity.
- *Check:* China-tenant share of neocloud books; their funding; whether the leader's guide names the rule.
- *Falsifier:* Nvidia cuts guidance citing the rule.
- *Record:* consistent: China data-centre revenue near zero, guide excludes China, beat on 17 Nov.

**MX-X4 Score enforcement, not text** - ADOPT (release-rush clause SHADOW)
- *Mechanism:* a control is what is enforced in the next six months (fines, informing letters, indictments); drafts
  narrow after comment; a draft exempting what is already public sets off a rush to publish.
- *Check:* fines against past multi-billion sanctions penalties (largest chip-tool fine $500m); final against draft.
- *Falsifier:* a rule with no enforcement in six months still measurably cuts the targeted flow.
- *Record:* one rule sat unenforced; one foundry route closed, reopened and closed; Chinese finals narrowed after
  comment (2022-23). Release-rush test: a Chinese open-weight model of 2T+ parameters before China's AI export rule
  is final (comments closed 15 Nov).

**MX-X5 Controls speed the substitute; tools cap it** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* a denied market pays for a domestic system that merely works, funding the next generation; but
  China's output is capped by its immersion lithography stock, so benchmark parity is not compute parity.
- *Check:* domestic accelerator repeat customers; Chinese token share on open routers (above 30%); immersion tools
  (about 340 installed, 90 a year, each supporting 100k+ accelerators a year); compute (US 30M+ H100-equivalents
  against about 2M in China incl. rented, Oct 2026).
- *Falsifier:* repeat orders flat and token share below 30% two to three quarters after a control round.
- *Record:* benchmark lag held at 1-6 months while compute stayed 10-30x behind; no clean test. Mixed sources.

**MX-X6 Truces are bought, and stay armed** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* a US-China truce rolls when one side pays, and the US has paid in Taiwan arms timing and control
  slack. Both keep weapons loaded: China's mineral dominance is written plan; US controls are moving into statute,
  harder to trade away. The side whose concessions are carried out first gave more.
- *Check:* control-list additions; rare-earth licence speed; the four chip-control bills (one in the defence-bill
  conference); Taiwan arms notifications; order of concessions.
- *Ledger:* rollover and US slack or arms delay are positively correlated; never independent rows.
- *Falsifier:* a rollover with no visible US concession within 30 days.
- *Record:* held through three 2026 summits; arms delayed around May (hit); a front-loaded Gulf deal collapsed in
  three weeks. The 16 Nov rule is a tightening counter-signal.

**MX-X7 Summits trade symbols; agencies set direction** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* summits deliver purchases and ceremony; direction is in agency rules issued around them and in
  movement on managed-dependence goods (rare earths, minerals, AI models).
- *Check:* agency actions within 15 days either side; whether minerals or AI guardrails appear in either readout.
- *Falsifier:* a readout overrides contrary agency action within 60 days.
- *Record:* May 2026 purchases with no mineral concession; September thin; the 16 Nov rule days before Shenzhen.
  Pairs with ai-trade rule 8.

### W. War, coercion and truces

**MX-W1 Count costly moves; the ground rung needs supplies** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* strike rungs need force concentration (carriers, squadrons). A ground rung needs fuel and munitions
  stocks, heavy airlift, field hospitals, engineers and a theatre command; troops alone earn no credit. Each host-state
  basing or overflight denial lowers the odds of escalation needing its geography.
- *Falsifier:* a ground operation starts without those markers, or they complete with no operation in 90 days.
- *Record:* deployments preceded the Feb, July and September strike rounds (3 hits); eight months of Marines and
  airborne units in theatre brought no ground war (a 75% call missed); one operation paused within 36 hours of host
  denials.

**MX-W2 Force alone does not bring capitulation; indivisible truces are pauses** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* bombing, blockade and sanctions rally a cohesive regime; elites defect only when the ruler cannot
  protect them and a successor can; decapitation yields a harder successor. A truce leaving an indivisible issue open
  (nuclear threshold, sovereignty over the strait) is a pause.
- *Caps:* "no capitulation" 0.85+ per quarter while cohesion holds; "truce holds 60 days" 0.35 or less.
- *Falsifier:* enriched uranium leaves the country, or a deal dividing those issues holds 60 days.
- *Record:* no capitulation in 2026; the April ceasefire broke in about two weeks and the June deal in about three
  (2 of 2). Grounded in formal §2.

**MX-W3 Post-election windows cut both ways** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* before a new legislature sits, a defeated executive may escalate for a legacy win, more so if an ally
  is in caretaker mode; or the shorter horizon lets the target wait, and the war ends by purchase (cash, unfrozen
  assets) plus declared victory. Carry both as siblings.
- *Check:* force clock (third carrier, expeditionary unit, evacuations); the target's currency z-score; its
  floating-storage cash; mediation tracks.
- *Falsifier:* escalation: no multi-day US strike campaign or ground operation in Iran, 18 Nov 2026 to 3 Jan 2027.
  Purchase: no money-for-access deal by 31 Mar 2027.
- *Record:* open. A US strike on an Iranian island (2 Nov) and an Iranian drone strike on a US base in Bahrain
  (15 Nov) sit in the window; the June deal bought the strait open, then collapsed. Escalation has been called
  imminent about ten times since March (MX-G1).

**MX-W4 A chokepoint's state is its transit count; the bypass is next** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* a strait is controlled by unreliable passage plus insurance cost, not declared closure. Contested
  past 60 days, escalation moves to the bypass (Gulf of Oman terminals, Saudi east-west pipeline, Red Sea).
- *Check:* daily transits (normal 100-140; below 30 is constrained whatever communiqués say); war-risk premiums.
- *Falsifier:* 30 days above 60 transits with no settlement; or 90 contested days with no bypass strike.
- *Record:* bypass call hit (a terminal struck in May; Saudi exports curtailed 10-11 Sep, 6-14 weeks after). Matches
  market-transmission §7.1. State measures only.

**MX-W5 Pressure on a target hits its biggest buyer** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* to bite, pressure on an exporter must sanction its great-power buyer's refiners or banks, which lands
  on that bilateral relationship. Cutting an inelastic exporter's volume can raise its revenue, so tanker-war
  headlines alone do not mean less money for the target.
- *Check:* US designations of Chinese refiners, terminals or banks over Iranian oil (China takes 80%+ of Iran's
  shipped barrels).
- *Falsifier:* a major Chinese refiner or bank is designated and the truce rolls with no Chinese response.
- *Record:* none; no secondary sanctions on Chinese firms since Sept 2026.

**MX-W6 Taiwan: three outcomes, moved by constraints** - ADOPT (method)
- *Mechanism:* carry invasion, quarantine or blockade, and limited kinetic gray-zone action separately; their AI
  impacts differ by an order of magnitude. Move them on constraints, not rhetoric: US munitions, interceptors and
  ships spent in the Gulf; PLA readiness (2027); the US fear of self-harm shrinking as Arizona fabs ramp (about 30%
  of the foundry's 2nm-and-below capacity at six fabs). Coercing third countries is a gray-zone pace gauge.
- *Check:* inspection or exclusion zones; unannounced drills; missile stocks (near zero); about 40% of the underway
  US Navy in the Middle East; the frozen $14bn arms package; purges (ambiguous); the January 2028 election.
- *Falsifier (method):* a sub-outcome resolves and the split adds nothing over one number.
- *Record:* structural; regional-body splits called twice (2 hits); risk argued to peak after mid-2027.

### R. Rates, liquidity and the dollar

**MX-R1 Attribute before you explain** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* (a) if the 10y10y forward holds on an AI drawdown and companies are net savers, the long-end premium is
  fiscal: never ledger "AI capex raises yields", and do not expect an AI bust to pull the long end much. (b) Before
  crediting a move to AI or the Fed, check 30-day correlations of the S&P, gold and the dollar with Brent; above 0.5
  in absolute value the read is confounded by the war.
- *Falsifier:* (a) the forward falls 15bp+ on an AI-only sell-off day; (b) correlations stay high in a quiet war.
- *Record:* (a) passed its first test on 7 Nov 2026; (b) descriptive since 27 Feb, untested out of sample.

**MX-R2 Capex is demand** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* a capex digestion is also a GDP shock with a Fed leg: cutting 25% of spending worth about 3% of GDP
  removes about 0.75pp of growth, roughly 0.75m jobs (1pp for 1m jobs, rule of thumb). The decline branch partly
  cushions itself through rates.
- *Check:* information-processing investment in GDP; payrolls after the late-January capex guides.
- *Falsifier:* a year of falling AI capex with no drag in GDP's investment line.
- *Record:* accounting identity; in 2026 the build offset tariff uncertainty. Multiplier size untested.

**MX-R3 Follow the pricing, not the "should"** - ADOPT
- *Mechanism:* for the next meeting, futures plus Fed speakers beat any model of what the Fed should do; forecasters
  whose positions need lower rates are the worst guides. Split pricing into modal path plus tail premium. Real-rate
  stress (real yields at 20-year highs, breakevens falling) is a recession input, not a Fed call.
- *Check:* futures by meeting; 3-month annualised core (3.7%); speakers.
- *Falsifier:* two straight meetings where a "should" view beats pricing of 90%+.
- *Record:* the "should not hike" view missed at least seven times in 2026 across four forecasters. Strongest record
  here.

**MX-R4 Liquidity stress comes in order** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* systemic stress runs repo spikes, then a MOVE jump (wider haircuts), then falling term premia, then
  wider credit spreads. A single-name CDS blowout without the earlier steps is idiosyncratic.
- *Check:* SOFR-EFFR; MOVE (above 100 tightening ahead; above 135 sustained, slowdown risk); term premia; spreads.
- *Falsifier:* a systemic credit event with no repo or MOVE warning in the prior 60 days.
- *Record:* Oct 2025 repo stress preceded the end of QT (hit); MOVE broke 100 in Aug-Oct 2026 with no event yet.

**MX-R5 Energy shocks fade faster than headlines** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* inelastic energy markets overshoot into shortage, then glut as capacity lands. Use only for how long
  an energy-driven inflation or rates impulse lasts.
- *Check:* LNG additions (North America 11.4 to 24.4 bcf/d by 2028); core against headline inflation.
- *Falsifier:* an energy impulse still lifting core inflation 12 months after its peak.
- *Record:* one earlier glut call hit (2023-24). Never an oil, energy or tanker call.

### M. Market plumbing and positioning

**MX-M1 A crash with intact prints is positioning; the low needs the pressure gone** - ADOPT (low-gate clause SHADOW)
- *Mechanism:* falls without company news are forced selling (retail, then long/short funds cutting gross, then
  leveraged and volatility-targeting products and dealers) and say nothing about the next print. A capitulation day
  is the low only if the source of pressure goes (policy rescue, earnings holding).
- *Check:* prints against price; leveraged single-stock ETF assets; margin debt; for the low: a follow-through day,
  leaders refusing new lows, the 10-year falling.
- *Falsifier:* twice, a crash with intact prints is followed by a print miss; or a V-shaped recovery while rate
  pressure is still priced.
- *Record:* Korea's main index fell from about 9,000 to 5,500 (Jun-Jul 2026) with memory earnings intact; low gate 2
  of 2 in 2026 (July V without rate pressure; no V in Oct-Nov with hikes priced). The low gate moves the scenario
  tree only.

**MX-M2 New share supply drains the theme** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* a mega-listing or issuance wave pulls money from the nearest proxies. A speculative top needs high
  multiples plus an issuance surge (IPOs, at-the-market sales, new share classes, novel financing) plus credit
  diverging from equity.
- *Live test:* Anthropic lists 19 Nov 2026; day 47 is 5 Jan 2027; the rule says SMH lags QQQ over the window.
  Falsifier: it leads.
- *Record:* n=1 (a 2026 mega-listing: QQQ -8%, semis -19% in 47 days). Tracked, never ledgered.

**MX-M3 Size the theme before the move** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* compute a theme's real supply or spend effect; a move far beyond it fades within about two weeks.
- *Check:* e.g. one product at 25% adoption uses about 1.3% of a year's DRAM.
- *Falsifier:* three outsized moves hold past two weeks.
- *Record:* n=2 (a 14% jump given back; an 8% fall).

### G. Reading signals

**MX-G1 Weigh a source by its stake and by call type** - ADOPT
- An on-record denial outweighs unsourced or interested claims, but a company's own product schedule is discounted.
  A record on direction calls does not license rung or timing calls; repeated "imminent" calls are a calibration
  defect; scorecards that drop misses are re-scored; a source is discounted on answers its position needs.
  *Record:* two denials held in Nov 2026; one supplier's optics dates had slipped before; one escalation source scored
  about 9 of 13, strong on direction and weak on timing, with three timing misses left out of its own tally.

**MX-G2 Common target, no common command** - ADOPT
- When several camps push one way, model converging independent incentives, not a plot: a plot predicts coordinated
  timing, institutions keep their own calendars. *Record:* the Nvidia sequence (news report 29 Oct, committee letter
  4 Nov, resignation 12-13 Nov) followed each body's own process. A Tier C guard. An overt principal with named levers is not a plot and is not excluded by this rule: run leverage-campaigns.

**MX-G3 Calm erodes the premium; rule changes are not dips** - SHADOW
- After long quiet, markets under-price a costly, explicit statement of intent; events that change the system's
  rules (trade access, export or currency regimes) bring long volatility and a discount on long-dated capex.
  *Check:* tail-hedge cost against house odds. *Falsifier:* volatility normalises within a month and long-dated
  capex guides hold. *Record:* one 2025 tariff episode consistent.

### P. Pressure on decision bodies (added 23 Nov 2026; procedure in `leverage-campaigns`)

**MX-P1 Who owns the reason owns the decision** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* a body that refuses on a ground another player determines (a security finding, a licence, a legal exposure) holds its position only while that player lets it.
  *Check:* the body's stated reason; who rules on it; whether that player has stated a preferred outcome. *Falsifier:* the owner of the reason gives public cover and the body still
  holds its position for 30 days. *Record:* n=1, in-sample (a chipmaker's board, Nov 2026: refused on security grounds on the 18th, reversed on the 23rd "citing no national security risk").

**MX-P2 A refusal before the principal commits is not final** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* a body's first "no" is given before it knows what holding out will cost. The informative refusal is the one issued after an official with a lever has given cover or made
  the threat concrete. *Check:* the date of the refusal against the date of the first such statement. *Falsifier:* two cases where an early refusal held through later cover or threat.
  *Record:* n=1, in-sample (door "shut" 18 Nov, cover 22 Nov, reversal 23 Nov).

**MX-P3 Reversals come with a structure and leave residue** - SHADOW
- *Mechanism:* a body reverses when it has a story it can tell, so a committee, a review or a set of conditions appears before or with the reversal, and the players with voice but no veto
  keep the issue alive afterwards. *Check:* whether the defensible structure exists yet; who has voice. *Falsifier:* a reversal with no structure attached, or no follow-on action by any
  voice player within 60 days. *Record:* n=1, in-sample (oversight committee formed 17 Nov; lawmakers and a state attorney general objected on the 23rd).

## 3. CONFLICTS WITH EXISTING SKILLS (who wins)

1. **ai-trade L5 no-call vs MX-A3, M1 (low gate), M2:** ai-trade wins; these classify, gate the tree or run as shadow tests.
2. **ai-trade own-guidance bands vs MX-S1:** decoupling protects volume, not price gains; the guide and the Micron
   lean-below stand.
3. **ai-trade rule 7 vs MX-A1, M2:** chip and capex inputs only; no ANT call; disclose that the analyst is an
   Anthropic model.
4. **War-price ban vs MX-W4, W5, S4, R5:** state measures and impulse length only. The ban wins.
