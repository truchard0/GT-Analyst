## 1. Europe: ECB path, 10/29 meeting, December, inflation and energy, growth, German fiscal and defence, BTP-Bund, French deficit politics

### Takeaway
The ECB is hiking into an energy shock. It raised the deposit rate to **2.50%** on 2026-09-10 (effective 09-16). The 10/29 meeting is almost certainly a hold (about 96-97% by market pricing in mid-October). The live question is a **December 17 hike to 2.75%**, possibly with more to follow (markets have priced about 75bp of total tightening). The pressure points on the euro side of the board are France and gas. The 10-year OAT went above 5% on 10/22 for the first time since 2002, and the OAT-Bund spread is near 170bp, the widest since 2011. TTF gas is at a post-2022 high of €92/MWh, with storage only about 75% full going into winter. Germany is borrowing heavily for defence: a €109.7bn defence chapter in 2027, of which €85.4bn sits outside the debt brake.

### Inferences
- The ECB faces a stagflationary trade-off: headline inflation near 3.7% against weak gas-hit growth and a French spread blow-out. It is unlikely to hike into a sovereign-spread crisis without signalling backstop tools. That is why December, not October, is the hike candidate. A Moody's downgrade of France or a failed budget vote could push the ECB toward a pause.
- With France at roughly 170bp over Bunds and Italy at roughly 110bp (10-10), the "periphery" has moved to Paris. In the game, France is the euro's weak square, not Italy.
- German defence and infrastructure borrowing plus ECB QT keep Bund supply heavy (one estimate puts the term-premium cost at 10-15bp — [Grosswald](https://www.grosswald.org/germany-2027-budget-defence-109-7-billion-procurement-55-8-billion-85-4-billion-outside-debt-brake/)). A 3.3% Bund narrows the yield gap with US Treasuries (US 10-year about 4.97%, the week before 10/30, per [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/bank-of-japan-rate-decision-october-2026/)). Over time that competes for global duration demand.
- European defence names benefit from the €109.7bn German defence line. Energy-intensive industry is hurt by TTF at €90+.

The policy mix is a weak-currency intervention defence plus gradual hikes plus fiscal expansion. That keeps the long end (30-year above 4%) the weakest point.
- Carry-unwind risk is two-sided and event-driven. Triggers would be a December BoJ hike, a renewed joint US-Japan intervention near 160, or a fall in US yields (already visible in late October). A fast yen rally forces deleveraging in yen-funded positions, as in August 2024, and hits high-beta US equities and Nikkei exporters. A slow grind keeps carry alive.
- Joint US Treasury participation means intervention is now partly a US policy tool. That makes 160 a more credible line than in past cycles.
- Japanese reserve sales to fund intervention show up in US Treasury holdings (see section 5).

## 3. United Kingdom: BoE, gilts, fiscal

### Takeaway
The BoE is on hold at **3.75%** with a hawkish 6-3 split: three members voted to **hike** to 4.00% on 2026-09-17. UK CPI rose to **3.5% in September** and the BoE projects about 3.75% in Q4 and above 4% in early 2027. The 10-year gilt is about **5.4%**, the highest borrowing cost in the G7. A new Burnham-led government with Chancellor John Healey faces its first autumn budget. The next BoE decision is 11/05, and a cut is very unlikely.

### Cited Findings
- On 2026-09-17 (meeting ending 09-16) Bank Rate was held at 3.75% by a 6-3 vote. **Greene, Mann and Pill voted to hike to 4.00%**; Bailey, Breeden, Dhingra, Lombardelli, Ramsden and Taylor voted to hold. The MPC unanimously agreed to reduce the APF gilt stock (financed by reserves) to zero. CPI was 3.1% in August, and the Bank expects about 3.75% in Q4 2026 and slightly above 4% in early 2027. Next decision 2026-11-05 — [Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy-summary-and-minutes/2026/september-2026); [MTA](https://www.mta.org.uk/resources/monetary-policy-committee-interest-rate-decision-september-2026/)
- UK CPI rose to **3.5% in September 2026** (forecast 3.4%), with core and services also above expectations. On about 10-21 the 10-year gilt was about **5.4%**, near multi-year highs. These come from an October 21 market update cited in search results; the primary ONS figure was not checked — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-kingdom/government-bond-yield)
- The autumn budget is the first from a **Burnham-led administration with Chancellor John Healey**, who seeks a fiscal "buffer against uncertainty". Britain has the highest government borrowing costs in the G7. Tax rises have not been ruled out, and bank windfall taxes have been reported. A former BoE chief economist says Britain is on "thin ice" and must "appease financial markets" (2026-10-06) — [CNBC 10-06](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/06/burnham-healey-budget-tax-bonds-gilts.html)

- A prediction-market note (about 10-19) implies a low chance of a November cut. The source's reliability is uncertain.

### Inferences
- The BoE tilt is hawkish. With three hike votes and CPI above forecast, a November hike is a real tail risk, not a cut. Ending the APF stock means active gilt sales pressure the long end just as a new government sets fiscal policy. That makes the UK the purest "bond vigilante" square on the board.
- The budget date was not found. If it lands near 11/05, the gilt reaction around the BoE meeting is a key event for global duration.

## 4. Emerging markets: oil importers vs exporters at $110+ Brent, Brazil, Gulf, EM FX and debt

### Takeaway
The oil shock splits EMs by geography and position. The **Gulf exporters are contracting** because Hormuz is blocked: the IMF sees the GCC at **−4.8%** in 2026 and Saudi Arabia at −3.3%, so physical volume losses outweigh the price gain. **India** is the most visible importer under stress: the rupee is near **94.8/USD** (10/23), down more than 7% this year, with RBI dollar windows and capital-flow curbs. **Turkey** is easing anyway, cutting to **36% on 10/22** as inflation fell below 30%. **Brazil**, a net oil exporter with high real rates, keeps cutting: the Selic is **13.75%** after 9/16, with the next decision 11/04. Data on Pakistan is thin.

## 5. Global liquidity and cross-border flows: foreign demand for Treasuries, the dollar, yen carry unwind

### Takeaway
Official foreign demand for Treasuries is falling. China's holdings dropped to **$590.7bn in August 2026**, the lowest since 2008, and Japan's fell to **$1.068tn** as Tokyo spent reserves defending the yen. Total foreign holdings still rose to about **$9.33tn** in August, implying private and hedge-fund buyers are filling the gap. US 10-year yields near 4.97% and a Fed range of about 3.75-4.00% keep the dollar carry advantage over the yen (1.25%) and the euro (2.50%). The main unwind risk sits in the yen.

### Cited Findings
- TIC August 2026 (released about 10-19): **China $590.7bn** (from $618bn in July; −4.4% m/m, about −15.6% y/y, the lowest since August 2008). **Japan $1,068.3bn** (from $1,103.9bn). **Total foreign holdings $9,331.7bn** (from $9,248.4bn). China now ranks third behind Japan and the UK — [Trading Economics China](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/foreign-treasury-holdings-china); [Economy.ac](https://economy.ac/news/2026/10/202610295902)

### Inferences
- **Treasury plumbing:** each yen intervention means Japan sells dollar reserves, mostly Treasuries or deposits, so intervention near 160 is mechanically bearish for US duration. Gulf contraction reduces petrodollar recycling, and India's FX defence drains reserves too. Marginal Treasury buyers are becoming price-sensitive private and leveraged money, such as hedge-fund basis trades, which raises term-premium and basis-unwind risk.
- **Global yield competition:** OAT at 5.05%, gilt at about 5.4%, 30-year JGB above 4% and Bund at 3.33% all offer domestic investors alternatives at home. Japanese life insurers in particular have less reason to buy unhedged Treasuries when 30-year JGBs exceed 4%. That is a slow structural drain on foreign Treasury demand.
- **Yen carry unwind path to US equities:** a December BoJ hike, intervention, or a sharp fall in US yields compresses the rate gap. A rapid USD/JPY drop, like the 163.87 to 158 move since July, forces deleveraging of yen-funded long positions in US tech and high-beta names. Watch the 10/30 BoJ meeting (hold expected) for hawkish forward guidance as a December signal.
- **Dollar role:** the dollar is supported by the US rate advantage and by safe-haven flows during the Hormuz escalation (the rupee "flight to safety"). It is undermined by official reserve diversification (China at an 18-year low). Net effect: probably a firm dollar against importer EMs and the yen, but the euro is supported by ECB hikes.
