## 1. Why are SMH (~21% off its June high) and QQQ (~7% off its 10/6 high) down? What happened in the October semis selloff?

### Takeaway
The semis drawdown is a rates-plus-financing scare layered on a crowded position, not a demand miss: hot September CPI (10/14) and a ~5% 10-year raised the cost of debt-funded AI buildout just as neocloud credit (CoreWeave) cracked and hyperscaler free cash flow turned negative (Meta, 10/21). The 10/22 leg was set off by a Lam Research supply-constraint warning plus Jensen Huang's remark that neoclouds lack capital, so the market sold both "too much spending" and "can't fund the spending."

## 2. Hyperscaler capex guidance (2026/2027), financing of AI capex, capex-cut signals, OpenAI/Anthropic funding

### Takeaway
2026 hyperscaler capex is running at roughly $700B+, and no hyperscaler has cut. Meta narrowed its 2026 range upward to $135-145B on 10/21 and posted its first negative free cash flow since 2012. Funding has shifted to debt, JV and off-balance-sheet structures, and new equity: Goldman estimates ~$500B of AI-related debt in 2026, and Meta announced non-voting stock. Credit markets are now pricing that risk (CoreWeave distressed, Oracle CDS doubled, Hyperion bonds at 91). Formal 2027 guides mostly arrive in the 10/28-10/29 reports.

Meta's negative FCF, non-voting equity class, Alphabet's negative Q2 FCF and a ~$500B AI debt wave mean marginal AI capex now depends on credit markets and rates, which links the AI cycle directly to the Fed and inflation (and so to oil and geopolitics).
