## 2. Redistricting, DOJ election activity, contested-result risk, certification calendar

### Takeaway
Mid-decade redistricting nets Republicans an estimated +3 to +8 House seats, which raises the bar for Democrats. That cushion now looks insufficient given Cook's ratings. Callais (2026-04-29) let Louisiana and others redraw majority-minority seats. DOJ plans a record ~1,000 poll monitors, has filed 31 voter-roll suits, and says it may challenge mail-ballot states or try to slow certification. That points to a noisy post-election period rather than an overturned result, since certification is legally ministerial.

### Cited Findings
- *Louisiana v. Callais* (decided 2026-04-29, 6-3, Alito): Louisiana's second majority-Black district is an unconstitutional racial gerrymander. Section 2 of the VRA was narrowed, and challengers must now show evidence supporting a "strong inference" of intentional discrimination. The Court fast-tracked its judgment so Louisiana could redraw mid-cycle. The Georgia governor called a special session for 2026-05-13 to redraw maps, and the Florida Senate approved a new map 21-17. — [Ballotpedia](https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/04/29/u-s-supreme-court-decides-prominent-redistricting-case-with-implications-for-future-map-changes/); [CRS LSB11431](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11431); [Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/supreme-court-clears-path-for-louisiana-to-gerrymander-mid-election/); [NAACP LDF](https://www.naacpldf.org/case-issue/louisiana-v-callais-faq/)
- Net redistricting effect, two tallies that disagree:
  - Tally 1 (2026-10-18): TX +5R, NC +1R, FL +1-3R, MO +1R vs CA +5D (Prop 50); Virginia 0 because its Democratic map was voided. Net about +3 to +5 R. — [predictionmarketspicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/midterms-2026/house)
  - Tally 2: TX +5, FL +4, OH +2, MO +1, LA +1 vs CA +5. Net about +8 R. — [Midterm Project tracker](https://themidtermproject.org/redistricting-2026)
  - Both are non-authoritative. Florida's map is under live litigation. — [Stateline 2026-05-15](https://stateline.org/2026/05/15/the-redistricting-frenzy-is-scrambling-the-midterm-elections-heres-where-things-stand-now/)
- DOJ will send a record ~1,000 monitors to polling places. Department attorneys, including prosecutors, will serve as monitors. Monitors have no statutory right to enter polling places. Wyoming Gov. Mark Gordon called DOJ monitors at the August Laramie County primary "aggressive, unexpected, [and] disruptive." — [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/justice-department-election-monitors-midterms/)
- DOJ voter-roll litigation:
  - The Civil Rights Division has filed 31 suits for unredacted state voter data, with judges blocking requests in 23 cases. This count is about 2 months old. — [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/judge-blocks-doj-state-voter-roll-policy-civil-rights/)
  - Judge Sooknanan struck down the plan to check rolls against immigration databases. — [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/judge-blocks-doj-state-voter-roll-policy-civil-rights/)
  - DOJ is weighing suits against more states, especially universal-mail-ballot states, before or after 2026-11-03. It would not rule out seeking to delay certification in California. It has sued California and LA County to set aside ballots of flagged likely noncitizens, and claims it can act for 30 days after Election Day. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/justice-department/doj-weighs-election-litigation-states-rcna604872)
  - 2026-10-21: Democratic leaders and election groups sued Trump and DOJ in D.C. federal court over directing states to purge suspected noncitizens. — [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/10/21/democrats-sue-trump-doj-over-effort-remove-voters-state-rolls/); [The Hill](https://thehill.com/regulation/court-battles/6164426-democrats-sue-trump-doj-voter-rolls/)
- Certification calendar and process:
  - Results take days to weeks. Example: California county certification is due 2026-12-03 and statewide 2026-12-11, the same day as the funding deadline. Georgia runoffs could push results into December. — [Bipartisan Policy Center](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/article/the-2026-midterms-key-dates-and-events/); [Kalshi explainer](https://news.kalshi.com/p/midterm-election-results-2026)
  - Certification is a mandatory, ministerial duty. Refusing officials can be compelled or bypassed; in Michigan, the Board of State Canvassers takes over. At least 17 interference attempts in 2024 all ended in certification. — [Protect Democracy](https://protectdemocracy.org/work/what-to-expect-during-the-2026-election-season/); [Beck Dispatch](https://beckdispatch.com/explainer-how-swing-state-election-certification-works-in-2026/)
  - In a midterm there is no national certification date and no Electoral Count Act clock, so disputes are local. — [Beck Dispatch](https://beckdispatch.com/explainer-how-swing-state-election-certification-works-in-2026/)
  - The new Congress is sworn in 2027-01-03. This is a constitutional date, not from a fetched source.

### Inferences
- Contested-result risk is more about headline volatility through mid-December than an outcome reversal. California's slow count is the obvious flashpoint, since many CA House seats decide the margin and DOJ has already sued there. If House control hinges on CA seats, the winner may be unknown for 1-3 weeks.
- Close Senate races (GA runoff possibility, ME ranked-choice tabulation) could leave Senate control unresolved into December.

## 3. Iran war, gasoline and inflation in the campaign

The Iran war (started February 2026; fragile ceasefire announced 2026-04-08) is a net liability for Republicans. Gas is about $4.27-4.30 vs about $2.93-3.04 a year earlier and pre-war. September CPI was 3.7% y/y vs 2.4% in February. Majorities say the war was not worth the gasoline cost. Polls suggest even late relief (cheaper gas, war ending) would move few votes.

## 4. Fiscal: H.R. 6500 stopgap to 12/11, lame duck, shutdown risk, deficit, Treasury issuance, OBBBA

### Takeaway
Government is funded through 2026-12-11 under H.R. 6500, signed 2026-09-02. None of the 12 FY2027 bills had cleared both chambers as of mid-October. The lame duck must pass an omnibus, minibus or another CR, with the election result shaping each side's leverage. The FY2026 deficit is about $2.0-2.1T, worse than CBO's February $1.9T because of IEEPA tariff refunds. Treasury is holding coupon sizes and leaning on bills: 22.2% of marketable debt (July). The next refunding is 2026-11-02 (financing estimates) / 11-04 (statement).

### Cited Findings
- H.R. 6500, the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act 2027: House passed 370-48 on 2026-09-01, signed 2026-09-02. It funds agencies at FY2026 levels with some exceptions through 2026-12-11. ### Inferences
- **December 11 game theory:**
  - If Republicans keep both chambers, they can pass a CR or omnibus on their own terms. Shutdown risk is low, though a 60-vote Senate still needs Democrats.
  - If Democrats win the House, lame-duck Republicans (still in the majority until 2027-01-03) may push a long CR or full-year bill to lock in FY2027 levels before losing leverage. Democrats may prefer a short CR into January to negotiate with their new majority.
  - That makes "another short CR into January/February 2027" a likely base case and pushes a shutdown risk window into early 2027. This is my inference, not a cited forecast.
- The 11-02/11-04 QRA straddles the election. Holding coupon sizes plus a rising bill share is mildly supportive for the long end. A guidance shift to "changes" (increases) in 2027 would be a term-premium catalyst.
- Tariff refunds widened the FY2026 deficit. The end of Section 122 and its replacement by Section 301 reduces refund liability going forward, unless the Section 122 CIT ruling is upheld (see Section 5).

## 5. Tariffs and the Supreme Court

### Takeaway
IEEPA tariffs were struck down 2026-02-20 (*Learning Resources v. Trump*, 6-3), and refunds are flowing. The stopgap 10% Section 122 tariff expired 2026-07-24 and was replaced the same moment by Section 301 tariffs of 10-12.5% on most countries. Section 232 sectoral tariffs remain the durable core, including 100% on patented pharma phasing in from 2026-07-31/09-29 and 25% on advanced chips. Effective tariff rate: about 7.1% (Wharton, August data) vs Yale's statutory average of 11.1%. The Supreme Court's June rulings expanded presidential removal power over agencies but protected the Fed.

### Cited Findings
- *Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* (2026-02-20, 6-3): IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. Refunds are not automatic but are available through an automated CBP system. — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/02/the-supreme-court-ends-ieepa-tariffs); [Flexport](https://www.flexport.com/blog/the-supreme-courts-ieepa-tariff-ruling-next-steps-potential-refunds-and/); [Norton Rose](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/20f2de87/potential-refunds-us-supreme-court-overturns-ieepa-tariffs)
- Section 122:
  - The 10% replacement tariff expired at 12:01 a.m. EDT 2026-07-24 under its 150-day sunset, and Congress did not extend it. — [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/public_contract_law/resources/procurement-lawyer/2026-fall/ieepa-tariffs-struck-down-replacement-tariffs-expired/); [Industrial Sage](https://www.industrialsage.com/section-122-tariff-expires-july-2026/)
  - The CIT ruled it unlawful on 2026-05-07 for three named plaintiffs. The Federal Circuit stayed the ruling pending appeal. If upheld, broader refunds are possible. — [Skadden May 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs)
- Section 301 (USTR final action 2026-07-23, effective 2026-07-24): flat 10% and 12.5% add-ons for most countries, and 10% net of MFN for the EU, Taiwan and others. Yale: average statutory rate 11.1% after a 0.3pp fall. — [Yale Budget Lab 2026-07-24](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/page_to_pdf/1568/publication_1568.pdf)
- Effective rate (duties collected / imports): 7.1% as of August 2026 (Wharton, updated 2026-10-14). Tax Foundation projects 7.2% for calendar 2026. A Yale mirror citing 2026-08-24 shows 10.9% pre-substitution and 9.0% post-substitution. The gap is methodological. — [Penn Wharton Budget Model](https://budgetmodel.wharton.upenn.edu/p/2026-10-14-effective-tariff-rates-and-revenues-updated-october-14-2026/); [Tax Foundation](https://taxfoundation.org/research/federal-tax/trump-tariffs-trade-war/)
- Pharma Section 232:
  - The 2026-04-02 proclamation set 100% on patented drugs and APIs, excluding generics and biosimilars. 20% applies to firms with a Commerce-approved onshoring plan. Deal rates: 15% for Japan, EU, South Korea, Switzerland and Liechtenstein; 10% for the UK. Firms with MFN-pricing agreements are exempt.
  - Effective 2026-07-31 for one group and 2026-09-29 for others. Sources conflict on which group (Annex III) starts when. — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/trump-administration-imposes-section-232-tariffs-on-patented-pharmaceutical-imports-tiered-rate-structure-takes-effect-beginning-july-31-2026)
- Semiconductors Section 232: 25% on a narrow set of advanced chips/HBM from 2026-01-15. Broader chip tariffs were threatened, but none confirmed as of October. — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/president-trump-orders-narrowly-targeted-25-section-232-tariff-certain-advanced); [STR](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/tariff-actions-resources/section-232-investigation-semiconductors)
- NY Fed: tariffs added about 2.9pp to inflation through February. — [Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/oct/13/checks-galore-trump-seeks-quick-fixes-affordability-issue-heart/)
- Supreme Court, 2026-06-29:
  - *Trump v. Slaughter* (6-3) overruled *Humphrey's Executor*, so most independent-agency heads (FTC etc.) are removable at will. — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2026/07/supreme-court-overrules-humphreys-executor-upholds-federal-reserve-independence); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/06/us-supreme-court-reshapes-independent-agency-removals-carves-out-fed)
  - *Trump v. Cook* (5-4, interim): Fed governors' for-cause protection is constitutional and requires notice and a hearing. Cook stays while the case proceeds; the facts are not resolved. — [CRS LSB11449](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11449); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/06/29/politics/takeaways-supreme-court-cook-slaughter-carroll-ballots)
- OT2026 (term opened October 2026):
  - Vladeck: none of the first 24 merits cases are high-profile Trump disputes, but "at least three sets" are coming quickly. — [Vladeck](https://www.stevevladeck.com/p/bonus-237-looking-toward-ot2026)
  - Business-relevant cases: *Suncor v. Boulder County* (climate torts) and *RNC v. Mi Familia Vota* (voter citizenship). — [MLex](https://www.mlex.com/pulse/articles/2526682); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/news-and-events/events/2026/09/practices-supreme-court-2026-2027-term-preview)
  - No tariff or Section 232 case found on the docket.
- Fed leadership: Warsh was confirmed as governor 51-45 (2026-05-12, taking Miran's seat) and as chair 54-45 (2026-05-13), succeeding Powell. Powell stays as a governor. — [Mortgage Point](https://themortgagepoint.com/2026/05/13/senate-confirms-kevin-warsh-as-next-fed-chair/); [Scotsman Guide](https://www.scotsmanguide.com/news/senate-confirms-kevin-warsh-to-fed-board-with-chair-vote-on-deck/)

### Inferences
- The tariff regime now rests on Section 301 and Section 232, which are statutorily sturdier than IEEPA. Congress can't easily undo them without veto-proof majorities, so a Democratic House changes tariff *rhetoric* more than tariff *law*.
- Two court tails remain for markets:
  1. The Federal Circuit Section 122 appeal could trigger more refunds, which is fiscal-negative and importer-positive.
  2. *Cook* factual proceedings: an attempted Fed-governor removal remains a tail risk to Fed independence.
- Pharma: the 100% headline rate is mostly avoided via MFN deals, onshoring plans and 15% deal rates. The effective hit concentrates on non-deal firms.

## 8. Historical market behaviour around midterms

### Takeaway
History favours weakness or chop into midterms and strong 12-month returns afterward: about +12% to +18% on average, positive in every case since 1946 per one source. Academic work says part of this is risk compensation rather than a free lunch. The "Republican president + divided Congress" configuration has the weakest historical returns in one dataset, but another source says split Congress is good. The data are fragile.

### Cited Findings
- U.S. Bank: +12.4% average equity return in the 12 months after a midterm (31 cycles, 1900-2025). In a 60-year sample of 15 midterms, the S&P 500 averaged +16.3%. The 12 months *before* midterms averaged +2.9% vs +8.9% in non-midterm years. — [Capital Group](https://www.capitalgroup.com/advisor/insights/articles/midterm-elections-markets-5-charts.html); [Sequoia Financial](https://www.sequoia-financial.com/insights/the-midterm-year-roadmap-what-history-tells-us-about-2026/)
- Since 1946, all 20 midterms were followed by positive 12-month S&P returns, averaging +18.1%. — [Landmark Wealth](https://landmarkwealthmgmt.com/articles/sp-500-midterm-election-performance-why-stocks-have-historically-rallied-after-midterms/)
- A century-long daily study finds about three-quarters of the midterm-year outperformance comes *before* Election Day, i.e., the pre-vote rally into the election from the typical late-summer/early-fall low. — [QuantSeeker](https://www.quantseeker.com/p/do-stocks-rally-into-midterm-elections)
- Academic: higher post-midterm returns "appear to merely compensate investors for bearing systematic risk." — [Finance Research Letters (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612323001988)
- Retirement Researcher (1926-2023), via Motley Fool 2026-10-22: Democratic president + divided Congress 16.63%; unified Republican 14.52%; unified Democratic 14.01%; **Republican president + divided Congress 7.33%** (the weakest, 34 years). The Fool frames 2026 as a "worst-case" setup that "isn't the full story". — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/22/midterm-elections-shaping-up-as-worst-case-scenario-for-stock-market-isnt-full-story/)
- Contrary data point: a strategist cites +13.7% average S&P since 1933 for a Democratic House with a Republican president and Senate. — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/a-split-congress-could-spell-big-stock-gains-into-2027)
- Sources disagree on whether the midterm year itself is weak (some say weakest and most volatile, others strong). — [Interactive Brokers](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/technical-analysis/sp-500-seasonality-and-presidential-cycles-what-historical-patterns-suggest-for-2026/); [Certuity](https://certuity.com/insights/midterm-elections-and-the-stock-market/)

### Inferences
- With about 7 trading days to the vote, the seasonal pattern argues for a pre-election and post-election relief rally (uncertainty removal). The 12-month-forward base rate is strongly positive but rests on a small sample (n=15-31) and an overlapping risk premium.
- The 7.33% vs 13.7% conflict for the likely outcome (Republican president, Democratic House) shows how sensitive configuration studies are to definitions and sample. Don't anchor on either.
- Transmission channels specific to 2026:
  - **Oil/Iran:** gas and CPI to Fed path.
  - **12/11 funding:** shutdown headline risk; a lame-duck CR is likely.
  - **11-02/11-04 QRA:** bill share and coupon guidance to term premium.
  - **Tariff court tails:** refunds to deficit.
  - **Senate control:** Fed and judicial confirmations, regulatory direction (banks, crypto, prediction markets).
  - **Certification noise:** CA count and DOJ suits into mid-December.

- LPL and Carson midterm-seasonality studies not directly retrieved; LPL views are second-hand.
- No 2026-specific options-implied election-day volatility (VIX term structure around 11-03) gathered; outside this scope.
