## 1. Trade truce: Busan terms, the 9/20 New York talks, tariffs, rare earths, farm and energy purchases

### Takeaway
The Busan truce (2025-10-30) was due to expire on 2026-11-10. On 2026-09-23 it was extended only two months, to **2027-01-10**. That makes the APEC meeting (11/18-19) and the G20 in Miami (December) checkpoints, with a cliff in early January. Washington runs the truce as a "compliance" regime: China has to keep delivering soybeans, farm goods and rare-earth access in two-month windows. Export controls are explicitly kept out of the trade track. The tariff stack itself has changed a lot because the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Truce timeline**
- 2025-10-30: Busan Summit. Trump invited Xi to visit the US. — [Wikipedia: 2026 Xi state visit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_state_visit_by_Xi_Jinping_to_the_United_States)
- Busan truce terms: US tariffs on Chinese goods were capped at about 20%, with expiry on 2026-11-10. — [Al Jazeera, 2026-09-20](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/20/us-china-open-high-level-talks-ahead-of-trump-xi-summit)
- After Busan, the IEEPA fentanyl tariff was cut from 20% to 10%, effective 2025-11-10. — [Bloomberg, 2025-11-04](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-04/us-formally-cuts-fentanyl-tariff-on-china-to-10-after-xi-deal); [China Briefing](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/us-china-tariff-rates-2025/)
- Busan farm pledge: China committed to buy at least 25 million metric tons of US soybeans a year over 2026-2028. — [NDSU Ag Hub](https://www.ndsu.edu/agriculture/ag-hub/understanding-us-china-trade-agreements) (via search summary)
- May 2026: Trump made a state visit to Beijing. It added at least $17bn a year of other US farm products (soybeans excluded) and agreed a process to identify "non-strategic goods" for lower tariffs. — [Farm Policy News, May 2026](https://farmpolicynews.illinois.edu/2026/05/china-to-buy-17-billion-in-us-farm-goods-annually/); [Al Jazeera, 2026-09-20](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/20/us-china-open-high-level-talks-ahead-of-trump-xi-summit)
- 2026-09-20, New York (at JPMorgan's Manhattan headquarters): Bessent and Greer met He Lifeng and Li Chenggang for about 8 hours.
  - Bessent called the talks "very successful."
  - The US proposed an AI dialogue with an **incident-notification mechanism** for national-security-grade AI incidents.
  - Greer said chip and semiconductor-equipment export controls are **not** part of that mechanism.
  - The sides discussed putting the May tariff-reduction process into effect: Chinese consumer and low-tech goods in exchange for US energy, agriculture and medical devices.
  - **No new progress on rare earths or critical minerals was announced.**
  — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/20/us-china-open-high-level-talks-ahead-of-trump-xi-summit); [CNBC 9/20](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/20/bessent-he-lifeng-trump-xi-summit.html) (via search summary)

- 2026-09-23: Bessent announced the truce would run to **2027-01-10**. Many had expected an extension of six months or more. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_state_visit_by_Xi_Jinping_to_the_United_States); [CNBC 9/24](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/24/us-china-trade-truce-bessent-trump-xi.html) (via search summary)

**Washington summit and the Board of Trade**

  - On 9/24 they held a 90-minute Oval Office meeting covering trade, Taiwan, the Middle East, Ukraine and Korea.

**Tariff levels (secondary sources only; verify)**
- **2026-02-20**: the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs. The IEEPA fentanyl and reciprocal tariffs on China ended 2026-02-24. — [TariffsTool](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/china-tariff-rate); [Airlift USA](https://airliftusa.com/tariff-updates/china) (via search summary)
- They were replaced by a global **Section 122** surcharge, effective 2026-02-24. Sources disagree on the rate (10%, or 10% rising to 15%). It **expired 2026-07-24** at its 150-day limit. — same sources (via search summary)
- From 2026-07-24, China reportedly falls in a **12.5% tier of a Section 301 "forced-labor" tariff**. That stacks on legacy Section 301 product duties and on any Section 232 duties. — [TariffsTool](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/china-tariff-rate)
  - TariffsTool estimates a typical effective rate of about 37.5% on many electronics and machinery lines. This is a single unofficial estimate.

**Rare earths**
- The **April 2025** licensing regime on seven heavy rare earths was never suspended and was still biting as of May 2026, after the Beijing summit. — [TechTimes, 2026-05-26](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317208/20260526/china-rare-earth-export-controls-april-curbs-still-bite-after-beijing-summit.htm)
- The **October 2025** package (five more elements plus the extraterritorial "0.1% rule") was suspended to 2026-11-10. — [Carra Globe](https://carraglobe.com/china-rare-earth-export-controls-2026/); [Rare Earth Directory](https://rareearthdirectory.com/policy/china-export-controls-timeline/)
- Sources conflict on whether that suspension now runs to January 2027:
  - One source said no extension covering rare earths had been confirmed as of 2026-10-11, and that MOFCOM's 9/28 statement did not mention rare earths. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/commodities/articles/china-rare-earth-export-pause-123614106.html)
  - A 10/16 source says the suspension runs into January 2027, based on Bessent's 9/24 announcement. — [AI Horizon timeline](https://www.aihorizonnetwork.com/blog/china-rare-earth-export-controls-timeline) (via search summary)
- The US counterpart, the BIS **Affiliates (50%) Rule**, was suspended for one year after Busan. That suspension ends **2026-11-09** unless BIS extends it. — [Kharon / Silicon Analysts](https://siliconanalysts.com/analysis/us-china-export-controls-policy-2026-framework-update) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- **November 9-10 is a live event-risk window.** China's rare-earth suspension and the US Affiliates Rule suspension are both formally dated around 11/9-10. The overall truce now says 1/10/2027, but I found no official Chinese text extending the rare-earth suspension. A quiet paired roll-over on about 11/9-10 is the base case. If either side fails to roll its piece, rare-earth miners (MP, Lynas) and chip-equipment names would get headline risk.
- The short two-month roll plus two-month "compliance periods" mean Washington is holding back leverage until Beijing delivers. That keeps the soybean, farm and rare-earth flow as the scorecard markets will watch into APEC and the 1/10 cliff.
- With the IEEPA tariffs gone, the President's quick tariff lever has weakened. New China tariffs now have to come through Sections 301, 232 or 122, which are slower and procedural. That lowers tail risk of a sudden tariff shock on China versus 2025. Section 232 sector tariffs (semiconductors, among others) remain the main equity-relevant tool. I did not verify their current status.

### Gaps
- Exact current all-in US tariff rate on China from USTR or CBP primary notices. Secondary sources conflict.
- The official text of the Board of Trade and the $30bn-for-$30bn framework (the WSGR fetch was blocked), and whether any reductions are implemented or only announced.
- An official MOFCOM statement extending the October-2025 rare-earth suspension past 11/10.
- Energy purchases (LNG/crude) by China under the truce. None found.
- The fentanyl track after the February 2026 SCOTUS ruling. None found.

## 3. Chip export controls, Nvidia/AMD China sales, China's semiconductor push, Huawei

### Takeaway
In 2026 US policy moved from blanket denial to **case-by-case licensing of H200-class chips** under conditions. Licences were granted, but the volumes were "trivial," because Beijing itself is steering buyers to domestic chips. Nvidia now effectively guides China data-centre revenue to zero. Huawei Ascend is closing the share gap quickly. Washington has kept export controls explicitly outside the trade negotiations.

### Cited Findings
**H200 licensing**
- **2026-01-15**: BIS moved license review for H200-class chips (and AMD MI325X-class) to China and Macau from presumption of denial to **case-by-case**. — [BIS press release](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-revises-license-review-policy-semiconductors-exported-china); [Introl](https://introl.com/blog/bis-export-policy-h200-mi325x-china-case-by-case-2026)
- Conditions attached to the January 2026 rule: third-party lab testing, and China volume capped at 50% of the amount sold to US customers. — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/asia/us-approves-nvidia-h200-chip-exports-china-conditions-rcna253948); [Thomasnet](https://www.thomasnet.com/insights/nvidia-h200-chip-china-exports-approved/) (via search summary)
- **2026-02-26**: Nvidia received a US licence for a small amount of H200 exports to specific China customers. Nvidia's FY2026 10-K said there was no revenue yet under the programme. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-26/nvidia-gets-us-license-for-small-amount-of-h200-exports-to-china); [Nvidia 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)
- Nvidia later shipped its first H200s to China but forecast no China data-centre compute revenue.
  - Beijing initially restricted purchases, then allowed selected firms limited quantities.
  - A Commerce official called the volume "trivial."
  — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3365383/nvidia-ships-first-h200s-china-forecasts-no-data-centre-computing-revenue); [Yahoo/Bloomberg](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/small-amount-nvidia-ai-chips-152915216.html)

**Nvidia financials and comments**

**Huawei and China's semiconductor push**
- Huawei shipped about 572,000 Ascend chips in H1 2026, a 28% share of China's AI-accelerator market, 6 points behind Nvidia (IDC via Digitimes). Digitimes says Nvidia's lead has shrunk 80%. — [Digitimes, 2026-10-22](https://digitimes.com/news/a20261022PD226/nvidia-huawei-ai-chip-market-revenue.html)

**Pending rules and Chinese retaliation**
- The BIS Affiliates Rule suspension ends 2026-11-09. As of 2026-10-19, no replacement for the rescinded (May 2025) AI Diffusion Rule had been announced. — [Silicon Analysts](https://siliconanalysts.com/analysis/us-china-export-controls-policy-2026-framework-update); [AsiaAI tracker](https://asiaai.fyi/guides/chip-export-controls-tracker/) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- The binding constraint on Nvidia/AMD China sales is now **Beijing's procurement policy as much as Washington's licensing**. Any "chip deal" at APEC that loosens US licensing may matter less to NVDA/AMD numbers than headlines suggest, unless China also lifts its informal buy-domestic steering. Read-through for Huawei suppliers, SMIC and Cambricon (China A/H shares) is the opposite sign.
- Keeping export controls off the Board-of-Trade table is a deliberate US firewall. Watch for any breach of it, such as chips traded for rare earths, as a regime change.

## 4. US-China AI talks; China's position on the Iran war and Hormuz

### Takeaway
An AI channel was **agreed in principle** (US proposal on 9/20, Xi's endorsement on 9/24), but I found no evidence a formal first or second round has taken place. China is the biggest economic loser from the Hormuz disruption among major importers, and it presents itself as mediator. It is backing a US-Iran return to the "Islamabad Memorandum" while pushing yuan settlement and shielding its refiners from US sanctions.

### Cited Findings
**AI dialogue**
- On 2026-09-20 the US proposed an AI dialogue with incident notification; chip controls are excluded. — [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/20/us-china-open-high-level-talks-ahead-of-trump-xi-summit)
- Wikipedia records that on 9/20 the sides "agreed to set up an AI dialogue." On 9/24 Xi said AI should be "kept under human control." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_state_visit_by_Xi_Jinping_to_the_United_States)

**Iran war and Hormuz**
- The Iran war ("Operation Epic Fury") cut China's Hormuz-routed crude from 5.35 mb/d to about 1.22 mb/d, all of it Iranian.
  - China's March 2026 crude imports fell 2.8% y/y overall and about 25% from the Gulf.
  — [Foreign Policy, 2026-03-17](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/03/17/iran-middle-east-war-china-strait-hormuz-oil-energy-exports/); [USCC timeline](https://www.uscc.gov/research/enabling-iran-timeline-chinas-role-during-and-after-operation-epic-fury)
- Iran floated allowing tanker transit if cargoes are paid in yuan (March 2026, to CNN). Foreign Policy judged it implausible in wartime. **No evidence it is operating.** — [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2026/03/17/iran-middle-east-war-china-strait-hormuz-oil-energy-exports/); [Asia Times](https://asiatimes.com/2026/03/irans-hormuz-pain-is-chinas-yuan-gain/) (opinion)
- CIPS daily volumes rose from $85-105bn to over $130bn around the war's start (Atlantic Council GeoEconomics Center). — [Asia Times](https://asiatimes.com/2026/03/irans-hormuz-pain-is-chinas-yuan-gain/) (via search summary)
- China bought about 80% of Iran's shipped oil in 2025. Xi has signalled readiness to mediate, and Beijing sent envoys. — [ORF](https://www.orfonline.org/research/xi-signals-china-s-readiness-to-mediate-in-the-us-iran-war); [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/beijings-approach-to-the-conflict-in-iran-and-its-implications-for-china/)
- On 2026-09-24 in the Oval Office, Xi said he supports the US and Iran returning to the "Islamabad Memorandum." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_state_visit_by_Xi_Jinping_to_the_United_States)
- USCC frames China as "enabling Iran" during and after the war. This is a US congressional-commission view. — [USCC](https://www.uscc.gov/research/enabling-iran-timeline-chinas-role-during-and-after-operation-epic-fury)

### Inferences
- Beijing's interest is lower oil prices and reopened Gulf flows. It gains diplomatic standing as a broker, but US secondary sanctions on Chinese refiners remain a flashpoint that could spill into the trade track.
- China's support for the Islamabad framework suggests some ceasefire or memorandum exists and has frayed. Its current status should come from the Middle East research stream.

### Gaps
- Whether any formal AI-dialogue meeting has occurred since 9/24. None found.
- Current status of Hormuz flows and the Iran ceasefire as of October 2026. Most sources found are March-June 2026.
- Any operating yuan-for-oil or yuan-for-passage arrangement. None confirmed.

## 5. Taiwan: PLA activity, the blockade assessment, the ~$14bn arms package, TSMC

### Takeaway
There has been **no large PLA encirclement exercise in 2026 so far**, unlike every year from 2023 to 2025. However, Taiwan's MND says Beijing is steadily rehearsing a "joint blockade and control" of air and sea routes. November-December is the PLA's peak drill season. The record **~$14bn US arms package is frozen**: Trump openly calls it a "negotiating chip" with Xi. TSMC is printing records and guided Q4 higher.

### Cited Findings
**PLA activity**
- December 2025 "Justice Mission 2025": large blockade-simulation drills, a response to the $11.1bn US arms sale announced 2025-12-18. These were the largest since 2022. — [USNI News, 2025-12-29](https://news.usni.org/2025/12/29/china-launches-blockade-drills-around-taiwan-in-protest-of-u-s-arms-sales); [Defense News, 2026-01-08](https://www.defensenews.com/global/asia-pacific/2026/01/08/chinese-drill-near-taiwan-seen-as-test-run-for-blockade-message-to-us/)
- **Early September 2026**: MND's "2026 China Military Power Report" went to the Legislative Yuan alongside the five-year force plan and the 2027 budget.
  - It found PLA drills increasingly focused on "joint blockade and control" of external air and sea routes, quarantine scenarios, joint firepower strikes and amphibious landings.
  - A full-scale landing is still seen as beyond PLA reach.
  — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3366011/mainland-china-stepping-preparations-taiwan-blockade-report-says); [Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6433078); [Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-09-03](https://en.sedaily.com/international/2026/09/03/taiwan-warns-china-is-preparing-fully-for-blockade)
- Han Kuang 2026 (August) tested countering a maritime blockade in the western Pacific for the first time. — [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3362965/how-taiwan-preparing-pla-sea-blockade-its-biggest-war-games)
- 2026-10-12: Defense Minister Wellington Koo said the latest PLA drills were held off China's coast, not near Taiwan. — [Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/politics/202610120006)

## 6. China's economy: growth, deflation, property, PBoC, yuan

### Takeaway
Q3 2026 GDP grew **4.5% y/y**, a modest acceleration driven by exports and industry. Consumption, investment and property are weak. One source reports PPI swinging to strongly positive while CPI stays under 1%, which, if accurate, reflects an oil and commodity shock rather than healthy reflation. That narrows room for PBoC easing. The yuan is reported firm. Analysts are split on Q4 stimulus.
