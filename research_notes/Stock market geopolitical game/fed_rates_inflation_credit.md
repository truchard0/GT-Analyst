## 1. Fed path in 2026: rates, the 9/16 hike, July dissents, Warsh's remarks, market pricing, forecasts, balance sheet

### Takeaway
The Fed held at 3.50-3.75% for five straight meetings through July 2026. It then hiked 25 bp to 3.75-4.00% on 2026-09-16 by a unanimous vote, its first hike since 2023. As of about 2026-10-21, markets price a hold on 10/28 (about 86%) and a likely hike on 12/9. Goldman and JPMorgan both expect one more hike in December and then a stop. Warsh refuses forward guidance ("I don't prejudge meetings").

### Inferences
- What Warsh appears to want: credibility on inflation without guidance, and room to move "smaller and more frequent" (Hammack's framing) or skip. What he appears to fear: the energy/AI demand shock de-anchoring expectations (Michigan 5-10y at 3.5%, see section 2), and the look of politics, with a hike six days before the midterms.
- The base case among sell-side banks and in market pricing as of ~10/21 is a hold on 10/28 and a hike on 12/9 to 4.00-4.25%, which would likely be the last. The October CPI (due ~11/12) and the October payrolls report (due ~11/6) will decide December.
- Balance-sheet policy is talk, not action. No concrete QT acceleration or accord had been announced as of the sources found.

## 2. Inflation: CPI/PCE (August, September 2026), energy pass-through, core trend, expectations, October CPI date

### Takeaway
Headline inflation is re-accelerating on energy: September CPI was 3.7% y/y (0.6% m/m), with gasoline up 34.2% y/y. Core CPI is only 2.6% y/y but rising, running at a 3.7% annualized pace in September. PCE (August) was 3.4% headline and 3.0% core. Consumer inflation expectations are elevated: Michigan's October reading is 4.7% for 1-year and 3.5% for 5-10-year. Market breakevens (~2.4%) are far calmer than the surveys.

### Cited Findings
- **September CPI (released 2026-10-14):** headline +0.6% m/m and 3.7% y/y (August 3.4%; consensus 3.6% y/y, 0.6% m/m). Core +0.3% m/m and 2.6% y/y (August 2.4%; consensus 0.2% and 2.5%). Energy +5.2% m/m and gasoline +9.1% m/m, about half the headline gain. Gasoline +34.2% y/y. — [CNBC, 2026-10-14](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/cpi-inflation-report-september-2026.html); [CNBC breakdown](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/cpi-inflation-breakdown-september-2026.html)
- Shelter rose 0.4% in September. — [Yahoo Finance (Benzinga)](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/traders-warsh-42-chance-doing-082651946.html)
- A Carson Group strategist said core CPI ran at a 3.7% annualized pace in September. Another source says September leaves the 6-month upward trend in core intact. — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/economy/cpi-report-september-2026-what-to-expect) (search summary)
- Purdue argued that energy is driving energy prices, not grocery prices, in the September CPI/PPI. — [Purdue Center for Commercial Agriculture](https://ag.purdue.edu/commercialag/home/paer-article/the-september-2026-cpi-and-ppi-reports-energy-drives-energy-not-grocery-prices)
- One aggregator cites 6.3% wholesale (PPI) inflation; not verified against BLS. — [0xzx headline](https://0xzx.com/en/post/pst_cbd67831b48ce1a9)
- **August PCE (released 2026-09-30):** headline about 3.4% y/y and core about 3.0% y/y, below forecasts of ~3.7% and ~3.3%. Core rose about 0.2% m/m. The miss came mostly from a downward revision to July in the BEA annual update, which lowered inflation in every month of 2026. — [Advisor Perspectives, 2026-09-30](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/dshort/updates/2026/09/30/two-measures-of-inflation-august-2026); [Portfolio Terminal](https://portfolio-terminal.com/blog/pce-inflation-august-2026)
- **Note the gap between gauges:** core PCE (3.0%, August) is running above core CPI (2.4% August, 2.6% September). That is unusual and probably reflects weights and the annual revision. Reconcile from the BEA tables before relying on it. — same sources as above
- **Energy:** the December 2026 WTI crude contract was quoted at 103.27 (+3.8%) on an undated ticker capture around mid-October. Oil reached a five-month high in the week of ~10/20. The Iran war has pushed energy prices sharply higher. — [Yahoo Finance (Benzinga) ticker](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/traders-warsh-42-chance-doing-082651946.html); [SMM, ~2026-10-21](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104173809-macro-front-bearish-factors-regain-dominance-market-focuses-on-month-end-fed-rate-meeting-smm-precious-metals-macro-analysis); [Bangkok Post](https://www.bangkokpost.com/world/3338205/feds-warsh-surprised-by-world-economys-resilience)
- **Michigan, October final (released 2026-10-23):** 1-year expectations 4.7% (from 4.6%), 5-10-year 3.5% (from 3.4%), both the highest since May. Sentiment was 48.0 (preliminary 46.3), the second-lowest reading on record. — [Newsquawk 1y](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/us-michigan-inflation-expectations-final-oct-47-vs-exp-47-prev-46); [Newsquawk 5y](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/us-michigan-5-year-inflation-expectations-final-oct-35-vs-exp-35-prev-34); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/michigan-inflation-expectations/news/500356)
- **Market breakevens:** the implied 10-year breakeven was about 2.37% on 2026-10-21 (single tracker, see section 4). — [TreasuryRateWatch](https://treasuryratewatch.com/daily/tips-real-yields)

### Inferences
- Energy pass-through is showing up in headline inflation and consumer expectations faster than in core. Warsh's "AI demand shock" framing gives him a reason to worry about core even if oil fades.
- Surveys are far above markets: Michigan 5-10y at 3.5% against a ~2.4% 10y breakeven. Markets do not yet price de-anchoring, and the high real yields (section 4) do the tightening.

## 3. Labour market: payrolls, unemployment, wages; the 11/6 October jobs report

### Takeaway
"Low-hire, low-fire." September payrolls rose only 29k (released 2026-10-02), with 60k of downward revisions. Unemployment rose to 4.2% and wage growth slowed to 3.0% y/y, the slowest since May 2021. Initial jobless claims meanwhile sit near record lows.

### Cited Findings
- **September jobs report (released 2026-10-02):** NFP +29k against consensus of +84k (another poll had +90k per LSEG); the prior 12-month average was +45k. July and August were revised down by a combined 60k. Unemployment was 4.2% (expected 4.1%), with the rise largely from a jump in participation. Health care added 17k jobs and financial activities lost 7k. — [CNBC, 2026-10-02](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/jobs-report-september-2026.html); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/september-2026-jobs-report-payrolls-123334753.html)
- Average hourly earnings rose 0.1% m/m and 3.0% y/y, the lowest since May 2021. — [CNBC, 2026-10-02](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/jobs-report-september-2026.html)
- **Conflict:** the average workweek is reported as 34.4 hours by one source and 34.6 by another. — search summary of [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/economy/us-jobs-report-september-2026) / [ForexFactory](https://www.forexfactory.com/news/1429690-the-us-employment-situation-september-2026)
- Initial jobless claims are near the lowest levels in records going back almost six decades (CNBC, 2026-10-22). Claims fell 6,000 to about 194k, while continuing claims rose to a six-week high. — [CNBC, 2026-10-22](https://cnbc.com/2026/10/22/jobless-claims-record-low-weekly.html); [JobsnHire, 2026-10-22](https://www.jobsnhire.com/articles/60223/20261022/us-jobless-claims-fall-194000-layoffs-stay-rare-while-continuing-claims-hint-slow-hiring.htm)
- Soft September payrolls moved hike expectations from October to December. — [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/fed-october-2026-rate-hold-odds-polymarket-swing/)

### Inferences
- With wages at 3.0%, there is no wage-price spiral. That supports the dovish Goldman/Williams view that the September hike plus high real yields may be enough. Weak hiring alongside 3.7% headline CPI is a stagflation-lite mix that puts both sides of the Fed's mandate in tension.

### Gaps
- The October jobs report date (~2026-11-06) was not verified on the BLS schedule. No consensus was found.
- No JOLTS, ECI or labour-force participation level was found.

## 4. Treasury yields: 10-year, 2s10s, term premium, real yields, refunding and bill share, foreign demand

### Takeaway
The 10-year Treasury yield peaked around 5.31-5.35% in early October (highest since 2002 per Forbes, **not** 2007) and was at about 4.96-5.00% on 10/22-10/23. Term premium is meaningfully positive (ACM about 0.80%, Kim-Wright about 0.99%). 10-year real (TIPS) yields are about 2.65-2.76%. The curve is positively sloped (2s10s about +23 to +42 bp, depending on the source). Treasury kept coupon auction sizes unchanged in August and is using bills to adjust funding; the bill share is about 22.2%.

### Cited Findings
- **Peak, with a framing conflict:** Forbes (2026-10-07) says the 10-year rose above 5.35% on Oct 7, its highest since 2002, with the 30-year at 5.70%. It attributes the move to inflation, Fed policy and heavy borrowing. A separate Forbes page lists the 52-week high as 5.31% on 2026-10-05. **The brief's "5.31%, highest since 2007" framing conflicts with Forbes' "highest since 2002."** Note that 5.31-5.35% exceeds the 2023 peak of ~5.0% and the 2007 peak of ~5.3%. — [Forbes, 2026-10-07 (search summary)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/garthfriesen/2026/10/07/why-the-10-year-treasury-yield-just-hit-a-24-year-high/); [Forbes Advisor, 2026-10-21](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/investing/treasury-rates/)
- **Latest:** 5.00% on 2026-10-23 (Trading Economics) and 4.96% on 2026-10-22, down 17 bp that day (StockMarketWatch). A mid-October ticker showed 4.988%. — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/government-bond-yield); [StockMarketWatch](https://stockmarketwatch.com/bonds/10-year-treasury-yield); [ETF Trends, 2026-10-23 snapshot](https://www.etftrends.com/fixed-income-content-hub/treasury-yields-snapshot-october-23-2026/)
- **2-year:** 4.81% par yield on 2026-10-21. — [search summary citing TreasuryRateWatch/curve trackers](https://stockmarketwatch.com/bonds/2-year-treasury-yield)
- **2s10s (conflicting):** +23 bp (Oct 20, one tracker), +38 bp (Oct 16), +42 bp (FRED via a tracker, Oct 13-14). The mid-September low was about +25 bp. — [RecessionPulse](https://recessionpulse.com/indicators/yield-curve-2s10s); [Convextrade](https://convextrade.com/tools/yield-curve); [Meaterm](https://meaterm.com/blog/how-to-read-the-yield-curve-2s10s-spread)
- **Term premium:** NY Fed ACM 10-year about 0.80% on 2026-10-19. Kim-Wright (FRED THREEFYTP10) 0.9857% on 2026-10-16. — [MacroMicro (ACM)](https://en.macromicro.me/series/20480/us-10-treasury-term-premium); [FRED THREEFYTP10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/THREEFYTP10)
- **Real yields:** 10-year TIPS at 2.76% on 2026-10-21 per one page, whose headline figure is 2.65% (internal conflict), with an implied 10-year breakeven of 2.37%. The 2026-09-17 10-year TIPS reopening cleared at a 2.653% high yield. — [TreasuryRateWatch](https://treasuryratewatch.com/daily/tips-real-yields)
- **August 2026 refunding (announced ~2026-08-05):** $125B of coupons ($58B 3-year, $42B 10-year, $25B 30-year) refunded ~$96.3B of maturing debt and raised ~$28.7B. Treasury expects to keep nominal coupon and FRN sizes unchanged "for at least the next several quarters." TIPS: $8B 30-year reopening in August, $19B 10-year reopening in September, $26B new 5-year in October. Bills are the adjustment tool: cash-management bills, smaller short bill auctions in September, larger sizes across the bill curve in October. — [Treasury refunding statement](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0590); [Curvo Journal](https://www.curvojournal.com/treasury-auction-august-refunding/)
- **Bill share:** the TBAC presentation shows the T-bill share held constant at 22.2% as of 2026-07-31 under a prorated framework. — [Treasury TBAC presentation, Q3 2026](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/221/CombinedChargesforArchivesQ32026.pdf)

### Inferences
- With a 10-year real yield of ~2.7% and a ~0.8-1.0% term premium, much of the rise in long yields is real rates and compensation for supply and fiscal risk, not inflation expectations (breakeven only ~2.4%). That fits "heavy borrowing" plus a hawkish Fed rather than a de-anchoring story.
- The next quarterly refunding (likely early November, around the 11/3 midterms) is a catalyst. Unchanged coupon guidance would confirm reliance on bills.

### Gaps
- No data were found on foreign demand (TIC holdings, indirect bidder shares at recent auctions).
- No primary Treasury daily par or real yield curve data were fetched. 2s10s and TIPS levels are from unverified trackers and conflict.
- The date and expectations for the November 2026 refunding were not found.

## 5. Credit: IG/HY spreads, private credit stress, CMBS office delinquency, SLOOS

### Takeaway
Public credit spreads remain tight despite the move in rates: IG OAS 89 bp and HY OAS 340 bp on 2026-10-22, with HY widening from ~315 bp on Oct 8. Stress is concentrated in private credit and CRE. Non-traded BDC redemption requests are running ~13% a quarter against 5% gates. Trepp office CMBS delinquency was ~12.2% in September.

### Cited Findings
- **IG:** ICE BofA US Corporate OAS 0.89% on 2026-10-22, 0.88% on Oct 21, 0.85% on Oct 16, 0.86% on Oct 1. BBB 1.09% (Oct 21), AAA 0.42% (Oct 22). — [FRED BAMLC0A0CM](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLC0A0CM); [FRED BBB](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLC0A4CBBB); [FRED AAA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLC0A1CAAA)
- **HY:** ICE BofA US HY OAS 3.40% on 2026-10-22, 3.27% on Oct 21, 3.29% on Oct 19, and 3.15% on Oct 8 (one tracker described 3.15% as "above average but not extreme"). — [FRED BAMLH0A0HYM2](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLH0A0HYM2); [MacroRadar](https://www.macroradar.io/high-yield-spread)
- Muzinich's October snapshot says spreads widened across the board into month-end, partly on French political risk. The timing may refer to late September. — [Muzinich](https://www.muzinich.com/opinions/corporate-credit-snapshot)
- **Private credit / BDCs:** average redemption requests at 11 of the 14 largest non-traded BDCs were 13.9% in Q2 and 13.0% in Q3 (data through 2026-10-02), against ~5% quarterly limits.
