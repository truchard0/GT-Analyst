# Energy, Commodities and Russia: fact base as of 2026-10-24 (pre/post 2026-11-03 US midterms)

Notes compiled 2026-10-24. Every figure carries its date. Many sources are secondary aggregators (Trading Economics, Discovery Alert, Political.org, Substack, Energy News Beat). These are flagged where used. Reuters, Bloomberg and FT pages were mostly paywalled or returned 403, so much of their reporting arrives second-hand through syndication (World Oil, StreetInsider, US News, CNBC citations). No investment advice. These are facts and mechanisms only.

Background framing, needed for every section below: the 2026 oil shock comes from the **US–Israel war on Iran**, which began 2026-02-28, and the resulting closure and disruption of the Strait of Hormuz. The Russia-Ukraine war is the second front. US energy policy toward Russia (the Graham Act waiver and the diesel deal) is explicitly tied to the Hormuz-driven fuel shortage.

## 1. Brent/WTI path in 2026, US gasoline and its political salience, SPR and IEA stock actions

### Takeaway
Brent spiked past $100 on 2026-03-08 after Hormuz closed. Sources disagree on the 2026 peak: about $120.88 intraday on 2026-04-30 (Forbes), or about $126 (Wikipedia). Brent eased after a June US–Iran memorandum, then re-escalated in October on Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and tanker strikes. It closed about $116.7–117.0 on 2026-10-23 (WTI about $103.3), well above the user's "~111–113" premise, which matches the 10-20 to 10-22 range. US gasoline is about $4.27–4.30/gal (AAA, 10-22 to 10-24). That is the first October ever above $4, and voters overwhelmingly blame Trump. The US SPR is at about 284 mb, its lowest since the early 1980s, and is still being drawn down.

### Inferences
- The late-October leg up (about $107 → about $117 in two weeks) is driven by Saudi/Houthi and Hormuz risk, not by Russia. Russia sanctions relief was a brief bearish offset around 10-19.
- Pump prices lag crude. Brent's +$10 move after 10-13 had not yet reached AAA averages, which were still drifting lower on the winter-blend switch as of 10-24. A crude rally that lasts into early November would hit pumps around or just after election day.
- The SPR is near commonly cited operational floors and has Nov–Dec exchange deliveries already scheduled, so the administration has limited extra SPR ammunition before or after the vote.

## 2. OPEC+: 10/4 decision, 11/1 meeting, Gulf output below quota, capacity review, Saudi/UAE strategy

### Takeaway
On 2026-10-04 a **seven-member** core group (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan, Oman) held November quotas steady. The group is seven, not eight, because the **UAE left OPEC and OPEC+ effective 2026-05-01**. Quotas are largely notional. Core members pumped about 25.0 mb/d in August, about 5 mb/d below February pre-war levels, because the war constrains Gulf output. The group meets online on 2026-11-01 for December, and the full ministerial and JMMC meet on 2026-11-29 for 2027 policy. Quota changes are seen as unlikely before 2027, pending a capacity audit.

### Cited Findings
- 2026-10-04: the seven core members held November targets in a brief online meeting, as widely expected. The seven pumped 25 mb/d in August (+630 kb/d vs July), about 5 mb/d below February pre-war levels. Output is "well below quota" despite rising Hormuz flows. Changes are unlikely before 2027 because the group needs the capacity review to distribute increases. The next meeting is 11-01, likely to finalise December — [Reuters via StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/OPEC++agrees+to+keep+November+oil+output+targets+steady/27144369.html)
- Bloomberg/World Oil (10-04): OPEC+ had signalled a pause in quota hikes through end-2026 after modest increases in the six months through August. Those increases were "largely symbolic" because Gulf output is cut by the Iran war, but they give room to boost output once the conflict abates. The JMMC reiterated "concern regarding attacks on energy infrastructure". Next steps: 11-01 sub-group meeting; full ministerial plus JMMC on 2026-11-29 to set 2027 policy; a decision pending on restoring another layer of the cuts halted in 2022, shaped by the capacity audit — [World Oil / Bloomberg 2026-10-04](https://www.worldoil.com/news/2026/10/4/opec-holds-november-oil-production-targets-steady-as-supply-remains-constrained/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-04/opec-has-deal-outline-for-steady-november-quotas-delegates-say)
- UAE exit: announced 2026-04-28, effective 2026-05-01. The stated rationale was national interest and a long dispute over its 3.5 mb/d quota versus expanded capacity (about $150bn investment). The target is 5 mb/d capacity by 2027. ADNOC's Al Jaber said the move is "not directed against anyone". Hormuz limits the UAE's near-term ability to raise exports — [Enerdata](https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/uae-announces-exit-opec-effective-1-may-2026-after-59-years.html), [JPT/SPE](https://jpt.spe.org/uae-to-exit-opec-after-60-years-citing-new-market-reality), [Annahar](https://en.annahar.com/en/306432/sultan-al-jaber-uaes-decision-to-withdraw-from-opec-not-directed-against-anyone), [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/business%2Fenergy%2Fwhy-did-uae-decide-to-exit-opecgovernment-officials-industry-experts-reveal-reasons-behind-move-1.500522343)
- Gulf flows: Kpler says Gulf oil flows excluding Iran recovered to over 81% of pre-war levels in September — [Al Jazeera 2026-10-06](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/6/hormuz-ship-attacks-surge-are-increased-oil-exports-sustainable). - Saudi bypass: Aramco CEO Nasser (10-22) said East-West pipeline capacity has grown from about 2 to 7 mb/d (7 mb/d reached in March, terminating at Yanbu), with 10 mb/d under study. - Houthi pressure on Saudi Arabia: UKMTO has logged at least one attack a day in Hormuz or the Gulf of Aden on Saudi-linked shipping since 10-02 — [search summary of multiple outlets incl. The Houthi claims on Yanbu are unconfirmed by Riyadh — [Energy News Beat](https://energynewsbeat.co/crude-oil/brent-tops-114-as-houthi-attacks-reach-saudi-oil-and-aramco-clears-its-headquarters/)

### Inferences
- The 11-01 meeting looks low-signal. With Gulf members unable to fill existing quotas, a hold for December is the default, and the substantive decision moves to the 11-29 ministerial and the capacity audit. That timing lands after the US vote.
- The UAE's exit removes OPEC's largest swing-capacity member outside Saudi Arabia. Once Hormuz normalises, the UAE can pump without quota, which pushes toward lower post-war prices and a weaker Saudi-led price defence.
- Saudi Arabia's binding constraint is physical (Houthi attacks, East-West pipeline security), not policy. Its strategy is visible mainly as maximising the Yanbu bypass.

## 3. Russia-Ukraine: war status, talks, the Graham Act, the 10/16 waiver and Rubio's 60-day review, Russian flows, Europe, energy truce

### Takeaway
The Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act (H.R. 5334) became law on 2026-09-18 and required implementation by about 10-18. On 2026-10-16 Trump signed an EO waiving its key tariffs and sanctions, which included penalties on buyers of Russian oil and gas. Rubio (10-17) framed it as an up-to-60-day "analytical pause" driven by energy markets, which puts the decision around mid-December, after the midterms. The waiver followed a Trump–Putin diesel deal (around 10-09): lifting US sanctions on Russian fuel in exchange for Russian diesel volumes. Trump also announced an "energy ceasefire" on 10-11, which Moscow did not confirm and then rejected. The EU condemned the waiver and kept and expanded its own sanctions. Talks may resume after 11-10 (single-source).

### Cited Findings
**Graham Act**
- Signed 2026-09-18. It allows tariffs of up to 100% on goods from countries that knowingly buy Russian crude or gas or rank among the top five importers, with USTR re-evaluation every 180 days. Potential targets based on 2025 volumes include China, India, France, Spain and Turkey. There is a carve-out for countries showing reduced Russian gas imports (EU phase-out). It has a broad national-interest waiver for tariffs and sanctions and a 5-year sunset. Implementation was required within 30 days (by 2026-10-18) — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/09/the-lindsey-o-graham-sanctioning-russia-and-iran-a), [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/newly-enacted-law-targeting-russia-creates-novel-tariff-authority-over-third-country-imports-while-reinforcing-existing-us-sanctions-framework), [CRS LSB11474](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/LSB11474.html), [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/september/president-trump-signs-graham-sanctions-act-expanding-russia-sanctions-and-tariff-authorities)

### Inferences
- The 60-day clock from 10-16/17 runs to about 2026-12-15/16. That puts the US sanctions decision after the midterms, after the reported post-11-10 talks window, and after the 11-29 OPEC+ ministerial. It is a deliberate option held in reserve.
- The diesel deal is the clearest link between Russia policy and US pump and diesel prices. It and the Graham waiver are energy-supply policy aimed at the US distillate shortage (US distillate stocks 32% below normal).
