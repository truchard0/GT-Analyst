# Historical capex booms and semiconductor cycles: base rates and leading indicators for the AI capex cycle

Research date: 18 November 2026. Provenance flags used on every finding: **[F]** = page or PDF opened and read; **[S]** = figure taken from a search-engine summary of the linked page, not opened by me; **[2nd]** = secondary or low-tier source (blog, newsletter, aggregator) — treat as unverified until checked against a primary. Where two sources disagree, both are shown.

## 1. Past capex booms: peak intensity, duration, size of the capex decline, capex peak vs equity peak, and trigger

### Takeaway
In every boom with usable dates, equities peaked before or in the same year as capex and never after the capex decline began: UK railway shares peaked about two years before railway spending did, US telecom equities peaked in March 2000 while carrier capex was still rising to its 2000 peak, and in the 2018-19 and 2022-23 cloud digestions semis bottomed before capex growth troughed. Capex declines after debt-financed booms were 50-80% over two years (telecom carriers -60%, CLECs -80%, US rail track-laying -79%, shale upstream -51% in three quarters); declines in cash-funded hyperscaler digestions were shallow (-2% y/y for two quarters in 2019).

**Telecom and fiber, 1996-2002**
- Capital investment by publicly traded US telecom service providers rose from $47B in 1995 to a peak of $121B in 2000, then fell by over half to $49B by 2002; government data show a 206% increase in telecom capital spending between 1994 and 2000 (189% for the public companies). [F] — [Doms, FRBSF Economic Review 2004](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/er19-34bk.pdf)
- All five carrier segments (ILEC, CLEC, long-haul, long-distance, wireless) raised capex 1997-2000 and cut through 2002; CLEC capex fell nearly 80% from its 2000 peak by 2002; long-haul had both the largest rise and the largest fall. [F] — [Doms, FRBSF](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/er19-34bk.pdf)
- In 2001 telecom companies' IT spending dropped by an estimated $22B, more than two-thirds of the total US decline in IT spending; in 2002 telecom accounted for nearly all of the further decline. [F] — [Doms, FRBSF](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/er19-34bk.pdf)
- Supply side: US fiber route miles rose from 143,000 (1996) to 320,000 (2000); firms with nationwide fiber networks went from four (1990) to ten (2000); DWDM raised capacity per fiber twenty-fold between 1996 and 2000, cutting the fiber actually needed. [F] — [Doms, FRBSF](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/er19-34bk.pdf)
- Carrier capex-to-revenue norm was about 20%; in the 1999-2000 optical boom some carriers spent 30-40% of revenue, and Level 3 spent about 400% of revenue (attributed to an RHK director). [S] — [Lightwave](https://www.lightwaveonline.com/business/earnings-statements/article/16648149/surviving-the-crunch-in-capital-expenditures)
- Telecom-equipment spending grew about 17% a year 1995-2000, then fell 24% in 2001 (Economy.com). [S] — [Seattle Times, Jan 2002](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/20020113/darkfiber130/fiber-optic-lines-languish-under-scarred-city-streets)
- Carrier capex was projected in November 2001 to fall 16% in 2001 with a further large drop in 2002. [S] — [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/optical-networking/optical-oracle-more-carrier-cutbacks)
- Industry spending exceeded $500B in the five years after the 1996 Telecom Act. [S] — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-telecommunications-crash-what-to-do-now); same $500B figure [F] — [Sparkline Capital, Oct 2025](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)
- Peak annual telecom capex of about $213B in 2000 (inflation-adjusted) and 1.0-1.2% of US GDP. [S][2nd] — [7GC](https://www.7gc.co/insights/ai-capex-and-the-telecom-bubble-a-comparative-analysis)
- Financing: telecom companies raised $1.6 trillion on Wall Street and floated $600B in bonds; FCC Chairman Powell said in July 2002 the industry owed about $1 trillion, much of which would never be repaid. [S] — [Quartz](https://qz.com/ai-debt-dot-com-telecom-borrowing-comparison-050526)
- Utilisation after the bust: estimates of fiber lit range from 2.7% to 25% depending on date and definition — 5% lit (2001-02 trade press), 22% of dark fiber lit on 22 routes (late 2001), 25% of US backbone fiber lit (TIA, Nov 2002). [S] — [Lightwave](https://www.lightwaveonline.com/business/earnings-statements/article/16648148/is-too-much-fiber-not-enough); [Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/2001/6/18/19591977/economy-s-slump-idles-95-of-fiber-optic-lines/); [Lightwave/TIA](https://lightwaveonline.com/optical-tech/article/16647915/tia-calls-for-more-us-optical-capacity)
- Sparkline's version: 80 million miles of fiber laid, 85% unused, bandwidth cost fell 90%. [F] — [Sparkline Capital](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)
- Equity outcome: the telecom stock index fell 92% after 2000 and was still about 60% below its high in September 2025. [F] — [Sparkline Capital](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)
- Equity timing: Cisco's record close was about $80.06 on 27 March 2000 (market cap about $550B, over 200x net income); it regained that level only in December 2025. [S] — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/cisco-stock-still-isnt-back-to-its-2000-high-chart-of-the-week-123018268.html)
- Lucent rose from a split-adjusted IPO price of $6.38 (April 1996) to a peak of $71.25 in March 2000; it issued a profit warning on 6-7 January 2000. [S] — [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/a-stumbling-lucent-tries-to-get-back-on-track/); [CNN Money, 7 Jan 2000](https://money.cnn.com/2000/01/07/companies/lucent/index.htm)
- Nortel's peak market cap was $283B in July 2000. [S] — [Fortune/CNN](https://money.cnn.com/galleries/2010/fortune/1002/gallery.biggest_losers.fortune/5.html)

**Dot-com valuation leg**
- Internet stocks with price/sales above 25 on 1 Jan 2000 (average P/S 33.3) compounded sales at 12% a year for 20 years, yet lost 80% by 2002 as P/S fell to 5.2; it took 18 years to regain peak multiples. [F] — [Sparkline Capital](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)

**UK Railway Mania, 1840s**
- An all-railway share index rose 106.2% from 1,000 (Jan 1843) to 2,062 on 6 Oct 1845, then fell 64.1% to 741 by April 1850; established dividend-paying railways fell from 1,706 to 616. [S] — [Campbell, VoxEU/CEPR](https://cepr.org/article/railway-mania-not-so-great-expectations)
- Alternative index: 167.9 (summer 1845) to 60.5 (end 1849), -64%; another source gives -66% from August 1845 to April 1850. [S] — [Odlyzko](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/mania06.pdf)
- Railway investment ran at about 7% of GDP in 1847 (annual flow); cumulative Mania investment about £200M, about 40% of GDP. [S] — [Odlyzko, mania18](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/mania18.pdf); [Odlyzko talk slides](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/talks/gmu2008.pdf)
- Odlyzko's verdict: an investment disaster for shareholders that left a national network; by contrast the 1830s mania saw a price collapse but investment continued and prices recovered. [S] — [Odlyzko](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/mania18.pdf)

**US railroads, 1868-1873**
- Over 33,000 miles of track were laid 1868-1873; the Panics of 1873 and 1893 bankrupted hundreds of railroads. [F] — [Sparkline Capital](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)
- Jay Cooke & Co., financier of the Northern Pacific, could not sell its bonds and closed on 18 September 1873. [S] — [American-Rails](https://american-rails.com/1870s.html)
- New track fell from about 7,500 miles in 1872 to about 1,600 miles in 1875. [S][2nd] — [Encyclopedia Dubuque](https://encyclopediadubuque.org/index.php/PANIC_OF_1873)
- 89 of 364 railroads went bankrupt. [S] — [PBS American Experience](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/grant-panic); a second source says about 55 were insolvent within two months and about half within three years. [S][2nd] — [Mises Wire](https://mises.org/mises-wire/not-worth-transcontinental-railroading-nation-prosperity)

**US shale, 2010-2016**
- Quarterly capex for a shale peer group peaked at $31B in Q4 2014 and fell from $27B in Q1 2015 to $17B in Q3 2015 (Rystad); cash flow before financing was -$3B in Q3 2015. [S] — [Oil & Gas Journal](https://www.ogj.com/home/article/17295269/shales-response-to-low-oil-price-environment)
- Upstream capex fell 51% from Q4 2014 to Q3 2015 (attributed to Dallas Fed work). [S] — [Natural Gas Intelligence](https://www.naturalgasintel.com/?p=235228)
- 2016 E&P capex was surveyed at 25% below 2015 globally and 40-50% lower in North America. [S] — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/oil-and-gas/our-insights/tough-first-quarter-ends-with-signs-of-finding-a-bottom)
- 34 shale-focused companies spent $189B more on capex than they generated from operations over 2010-2019. [S] — [IEEFA](https://ieefa.org/resources/shale-producers-spilled-21-billion-red-ink-last-year)
- Haynes Boone counted 114 North American producer bankruptcies from the start of 2015 to mid-December 2016; 2015 filings carried about $17.4B of debt and 2016 E&P filings about $56.8B. [S] — [Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/getcontentasset/588047ed-0b66-4c80-85ab-555932d9270c/141d77fc-2e06-49eb-b14c-2ff58f5ce730/oil_patch_bankruptcy_20160106.pdf?language=en-US)
- The US rig count reached an all-time low of 404 on 20 May 2016 after a dive that began from a 5 December 2014 peak. [S] — [World Oil](https://worldoil.com/magazine/2016/august-2016/features/one-for-the-record-books/)

**Bitcoin mining, 2021-2022**
- Core Scientific, the largest miner by computing power, filed Chapter 11 on 21 December 2022 with its stock down 98% that year; much of its debt was taken in H2 2021 to finance machines, and operating cash flow stayed positive but could not service equipment debt. [S] — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/196925/core-scientific-files-chapter-11-bankruptcy)
- Public miners ended 2022 with over $4B of debt; in H1 2022 about 70% of their funding was equity and 30% debt; hashprice fell 34% q/q in Q3 2022. [S] — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/197819/bitcoin-mining-companies-close-out-year-4-billion-in-debt-report); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/181448/bankruptcy-top-of-mind-as-miners-confront-market-downturn)

**Scale of the AI boom against these (as of late 2025)**
- AI capex relative to GDP already exceeded the internet-boom peak and was below the railroad peak; adjusted for a 5-year chip life versus 30 years for rail, it is the largest of the three. [F] — [Sparkline Capital](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)
- Meta, Microsoft and Alphabet were each set to spend 21-35% of revenue on capex (consensus NTM, Sept 2025), above AT&T at the height of the telecom bubble and above the average global utility; Magnificent-7 capex rose from 4% to 15% of revenue since 2012. [F] — [Sparkline Capital](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)

### Inferences
- Ordering rule supported by all four episodes with usable dates (UK rail, US telecom, 2018-19 and 2022-23 cloud digestions; shale, 1870s rail and mining lack verified equity dates): equity peak comes first, capex peak second, capex decline third. The lag from equity peak to the first annual capex decline was about 9-21 months in telecom (March 2000 to calendar 2001) and about 24 months in UK rail. Capex data is therefore a lagging confirmation, not a leading sell signal.
- The depth of the capex decline scales with external financing. Debt- and equity-market-funded builders (CLECs, long-haul, 1870s railroads, shale, miners) cut 50-80%; cash-funded hyperscalers in 2019 and 2023 cut by low single digits or paused. The current cycle's shift from cash funding toward debt (section 5) moves it along that axis.
- Peak carrier intensity of 30-40% of revenue is the same range as Meta (44%) and Microsoft (39%) in 2025 (section 4), so intensity alone is already at telecom-peak levels; what differs is the funding source and customer credit quality.
- Demand shortfall against forecast, not a rate shock, started the telecom turn; financing closure then converted a slowdown into a collapse. In 1873, shale and mining the trigger was the financing or price channel directly.
- Technology-driven capacity gains (DWDM, 20x per fiber in four years) were a supply shock that worsened the glut; the analogue to monitor is compute-efficiency gains per GPU or per token.

### Gaps
- No clean capex/revenue time series for US carriers 1996-2003 was found; the 30-40% and 20% figures come from one trade-press quote.
- UK railway capex by year, and the size of the post-1847 spending decline, were not retrieved (Odlyzko's PDF returned HTTP 503).
- XOP or E&P equity peak date (commonly cited as June 2014) and high-yield energy issuance could not be verified; the equity-vs-capex ordering for shale is therefore unconfirmed.
- No verified figure for the ASIC price collapse or miner capex totals in 2021-22.
- The widely quoted "$2 trillion of telecom market value lost" and the WorldCom (July 2002) and Global Crossing (January 2002) bankruptcy dates were not confirmed by any page I opened.
- Lucent's peak is commonly dated December 1999; the only source found says March 2000 at $71.25 (possibly spin-off adjusted). Unresolved.
- JDS Uniphase and Corning peak dates: one source gives Corning's peak as October 2000; JDSU's date was not found.

## 2. Vendor financing episodes (Lucent, Nortel, Cisco, 1999-2001)

### Takeaway
Vendor financing at the peak was large relative to revenue only at Lucent (about $7-8B of commitments, roughly a quarter of revenue) and unwound in under 18 months through customer bankruptcies and $3.5B of provisions; the early warnings were receivables growing faster than sales, concentration in unprofitable start-up carriers, and revenue recognition problems. Nvidia's 2025 customer and supplier investments are larger in relation to revenue, but on a far stronger cash position.

### Cited Findings
- Lucent had $8.1B of vendor financing commitments in 1999-2000, about 24% of $33.6B revenue. [F][2nd] — [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/); other reports give about $7B or $7.5B for 2000. [S] — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/vendor-financing-backfires/); [IT World Canada](https://itworldcanada.com/?p=24821)
- Lucent's receivables grew $5.4B in 1998-1999. [F][2nd] — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/)
- Lucent committed $2B to Winstar; Winstar filed for bankruptcy in April 2001 owing Lucent $700M, after Lucent refused a final $90M draw, and then sued Lucent for $10B. [F][2nd] — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/); [S] — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/vendor-financing-backfires/)
- Lucent booked customer-financing provisions of $2.2B (2001) and $1.3B (2002), $3.5B in total; an earlier $340M reserve preceded these. [F][2nd] — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/); [S] — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/vendor-financing-backfires/)
- Lucent's revenue peaked at $37.92B in 1999 and fell 69% to $11.80B by 2002; the SEC found $1.148B of revenue improperly recognised in FY2000; its top two customers (AT&T 10%, Verizon 13%) were 23% of revenue. [F][2nd] — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/) (the page itself cites three different Lucent revenue figures: $33.6B, $37.92B and $41.4B)
- Lucent extended a $1.35B credit line to Leap Wireless. [S] — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/vendor-financing-backfires/)
- Nortel's customer credit commitments fell from $4.1B at end-2000 to $2.8B at end-March 2001; a second source gives financed agreements of $5.2B in 2000 falling to $2.1B in 2001; a third gives $3.1B extended with $1.4B outstanding. [S] — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/vendor-financing-backfires/); [IT World Canada](https://itworldcanada.com/?p=24821); [F][2nd] — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/)
- Cisco had about $2B of vendor financing available and had extended $1.1B, mostly operating and equipment leases; another source says $2.4B promised. [S] — [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/ethernet-ip/vendor-financing/d/d-id/571917); [F][2nd] — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/)
- Cisco took a $2.77B inventory provision in 2001. [S] — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/cisco-stock-still-isnt-back-to-its-2000-high-chart-of-the-week-123018268.html)
- Motorola's customer Telsim missed a $728M payment on $2B of vendor financing. [S] — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/vendor-financing-backfires/)
- 47 CLECs failed between 2000 and 2003; 33-80% of vendor loan portfolios went uncollected. [F][2nd] — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/)
- AI-cycle comparison (2025): Nvidia's direct investments total about $110B, 67% of $165B LTM revenue, including up to $100B to OpenAI in ten $10B tranches; top two customers 39% of revenue and top four 46%; CoreWeave carries $10.45B of GPU-collateralised debt; Nvidia operating cash flow $15.4B in Q2 FY26 against Lucent's $304M in FY2000. [F][2nd] — [Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/nvidia_nortel_vendor_financing_comparison/)
- Sparkline flags the Nvidia-OpenAI $100B investment and the OpenAI-AMD equity-for-chips deal a week later as a return of circular financing, and notes Meta's $27B off-balance-sheet Hyperion financing as the largest private-credit deal on record. [F] — [Sparkline Capital](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)

### Inferences
- Unwind speed: from Lucent's January 2000 warning to Winstar's April 2001 bankruptcy was 15 months; Nortel cut commitments by about one-third in a single quarter (Q1 2001). Vendor financing withdraws fastest exactly when customers need it, which makes it pro-cyclical.
- Early-warning checklist derived from the Lucent record: (1) receivables or customer-financing commitments growing faster than revenue, (2) top-customer share rising, (3) customers that are cash-flow negative and dependent on the vendor's credit, (4) guarantees and off-balance-sheet structures, (5) first reserve build, however small ($340M came before $3.5B).
- On the two measurable ratios, Nvidia in 2025 is above Lucent in 2000 (financing/revenue 67% vs 24%; top-two customers 39% vs 23%), while its cash generation is roughly 50x larger. The exposure is to counterparties (OpenAI, neoclouds), not to Nvidia's own solvency.

### Gaps
- Lucent, Nortel and Cisco 10-K figures were not checked directly; all vendor-financing numbers are from trade press or a secondary blog and conflict by up to $1B.
- Days-sales-outstanding series for the three vendors were not found.
- No academic case study of telecom vendor finance was located.
- Cisco's inventory charge is widely quoted as $2.25B; the one source found says $2.77B. Unresolved.

## 3. Semiconductor cycle statistics: drawdowns, cycle lengths, memory peaks, the second-derivative rule

### Takeaway
A 20%+ SOX drawdown has occurred about once every three years since 2004, with a median depth of 35% and a median of nine months from peak to trough. Memory stocks have peaked one to two quarters ahead of memory fundamentals and fallen 50-60% each cycle. The "stocks peak when growth peaks" rule is contested: Truist's October 2026 study finds semis have peaked about one quarter after revenue growth peaks, not before.

**Second-derivative rule and lead/lag**
- Truist (William Stein, note reported 20 October 2026): semiconductor stocks have historically topped about one quarter after revenue growth peaks, not two quarters before; demand growth typically peaks near 29% y/y in the seventh quarter of a cycle; the current cycle is in its 15th straight quarter of improvement with consensus growth peaking at 74% in Q4 2026; the 1998-2001 cycle peaked near 50% growth and broke by its 14th quarter. [F][2nd] — [StockWireX summary of Truist](https://stockwirex.com/?p=51733)
- The same summary lists 2000, 2018 and 2021-22 as cases where stocks led fundamentals, and 2011, 2015 and 2018 as 20%+ drawdowns that were corrections inside ongoing up-cycles. [F][2nd] — [StockWireX](https://stockwirex.com/?p=51733)
- Opposing practitioner view: the SOX peaks and troughs two to three months before semiconductor sales. [S][2nd] — [techstock01 Substack](https://techstock01.substack.com/p/semi-revenue-growth-near-peak)
- UBS hyperscaler capex growth path cited alongside: about +76% (2026), +25% (2027), +6% (2028). [F][2nd] — [StockWireX](https://stockwirex.com/?p=51733)

**Memory cycles** [F][2nd] — [UncoverAlpha](https://www.uncoveralpha.com/p/every-memory-cycle-ends-the-same)
- 1993-96: DRAM prices peaked late 1995, then fell 51% in 1996 and 65% in 1997; capex exceeded 30% of semiconductor production value and about 50 fab plans were announced in 1995-96; memory stocks fell 60-80%.
- 2016-19: Micron revenue $12.4B (2016), $20.3B (2017, +64%), $30.4B (2018, +50%), $23.4B (2019, -23%); gross margin 20.2% to 58.9% (2018 peak) to 27% at the Q1 FY2020 low; industry inventory fell to 3-4 weeks against a normal 8 weeks at the top.
- Stock timing 2018: Micron peaked near $64 in May 2018, about two quarters before the fundamental peak (Q4 2018), and was down 56% by December 2018 while reporting near-peak earnings on a forward P/E of 4-5x.
- 2020-23: up-cycle about 14 months; Micron fell from about $98 (early 2022) to about $49 (late 2022); SK Hynix 2023 net margin about -28%.
- Cross-cycle pattern: boom 4-7 quarters; bust 4-8 quarters; revenue decline 25-40%; gross margin from above 50% to the low 20s or negative; stock decline 50-60%, leading fundamentals by one to two quarters.
- Late 2025 readings: DRAM supplier inventory 3.3 weeks at end-Q3 2025 (equal to the 2018 low); DRAM spot nearly 3x y/y by Q4 2025; HBM sold out through 2026.
- SEMI's Memory Inventory Cycle Index (sales growth minus inventory growth) has shown memory ASPs weakening right after the index peaks. [S] — [SEMI](https://www.semi.org/en/taxonomy/term/61646)
- Contract DRAM prices lag spot at both peaks and troughs. [S][2nd] — [Silicon Analysts](https://siliconanalysts.com/analysis/dram-spot-price-signals-2026)
- Creative Strategies (Feb 2026): three preconditions for a memory peak — trough inventory, peak y/y pricing, stock-price inflection — were not all met at that date. [S] — [Creative Strategies](https://creativestrategies.com/research/memorys-200b-inflection/)

### Inferences
- Base rate for a new 20%+ SOX drawdown starting in any given year is roughly one in three (seven completed in 22 years, plus the open 2026 episode). Six of seven recovered to a new high within 14 months of the trough; the exception was the one tied to a systemic credit event.
- Distinguishing a correction from a cycle top: in 2006, 2015, 2018 and 2024-25 industry revenue growth re-accelerated within two to three quarters; in 2000, 2007 and 2021-22 an inventory or credit problem was already visible. The discriminating variables are inventories and customer financing, not the price decline itself.
- Memory gives the earliest reading: equity peak, then spot price peak, then contract price peak, then inventory rebuild, then margin peak. Very low valuation multiples at the top (4-5x forward earnings in 2018) are a peak marker for memory, not a value signal.
- The Truist finding and the Micron 2018 record conflict on sign. A defensible reading is that memory and commodity semis lead their fundamentals while the broad index has lagged the growth peak; the framework should treat the growth-rate peak as opening a risk window of roughly minus two to plus one quarter, not as a point signal.

### Gaps
- No rigorous back-test of SOX against WSTS/SIA year-over-year sales growth was found; the Truist note is known only from a secondary summary.
- Book-to-bill lead times were not found (the SEMI North American book-to-bill series is, to my recollection, discontinued — unverified).
- Pre-2004 SOX drawdowns (1995-96, 1997-98, 2000-02, 2002-03) lack a verified source; the -82% figure is from a blog.

- Micron inventory-days data from filings was not retrieved.

## 5. Evidence on predictability: what can and cannot be forecast

### Takeaway
The evidence supports forecasting crash risk and multi-year relative returns, and does not support timing the peak. A 100% two-year industry run-up raises the probability of a 40% crash to about half without lowering expected returns; high asset or capex growth predicts underperformance of several percentage points a year; valuation explains under a third of ten-year returns and almost none of one-year returns. Earnings beats by mega-cap tech are near-certain and therefore carry little information, and Nvidia's post-earnings reaction has been close to a coin flip.

### Cited Findings

**Bubbles and crash probability**
- Greenwood, Shleifer and You (JFE 2019), US industries 1926-2014 plus international sectors: a sharp industry run-up does not on average predict low subsequent returns, but the probability of a crash (40%+ drawdown within two years) rises from about 20% after a 50% net-of-market two-year return to 53% after 100% and about 80% after 150%. Volatility, turnover and issuance during the run-up help predict which run-ups crash. [S] — [NBER w23191](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w23191/w23191.pdf); [Shleifer publications page](https://shleifer.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/bubbles-fama)
- Goldman Sachs (Oppenheimer, October 2025): global tech was not yet in a bubble; two of three hallmarks were present (rapid price rises, extreme valuations) and the third (systemic leverage) was only starting; capex-to-sales was below prior bubble levels and mostly cash-funded; 2000 comparators were Cisco at 101.7x and Intel at 42.1x forward earnings. [S] — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/why-global-stocks-are-not-yet-in-a-bubble); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/10/09/ai-bubble-stock-market)
- A Goldman note reported in August 2026 reframed the main risk as an earnings bubble, not a valuation bubble. [S] — [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/goldman-the-real-ai-risk-is-an-earnings-bubble-not-valuations-4830442)

**Capital cycle and asset growth**
- Cooper, Gulen and Schill (Journal of Finance 2008, 63:1609-1651): the lowest asset-growth decile earns about 9.1% risk-adjusted in the following year while the highest decile earns a negative return. [S] — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-asset-growth-effect-in-stock-returns/)
- Sparkline replication: top-quintile asset-growth firms underperformed bottom-quintile firms by 8.4% a year, June 1963 to August 2025 (equal-weighted, Ken French data); top-quintile capex-growth firms also underperformed in all ten GICS sectors and in all three major regions from 1995 to 2025, with the effect roughly monotonic across quintiles and present outside the dot-com bust. [F] — [Sparkline Capital](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)
- Titman, Wei and Xie (JFQA 2004): firms that substantially increase capital investment earn negative benchmark-adjusted returns, more so for firms with high cash flow and low debt (greater discretion). [S] — [NBER w9951](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9951)
- Critiques: the anomaly is partly explained by delisting bias and the external-financing effect, and is absent among low-idiosyncratic-volatility firms. [S] — [JFQA](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/on-the-scope-and-drivers-of-the-asset-growth-effect/B8CFCF68A82E273E979221EF26A77096)
- Magnificent-7 context: 27.5% compound annual return since 2015 on a 22.5% return on invested capital against 6.2% for the other 493; Sparkline estimates their depreciation rising from $150B to $400B a year over five years; Oracle's capex rose 249% in the year to September 2025. [F] — [Sparkline Capital](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)

**Issuance and IPO signals**
- Baker and Wurgler (Journal of Finance 2000, 55:2219-2257): the equity share of total new issuance strongly predicted US market returns over 1928-1997, in both halves of the sample, and sometimes predicted negative returns. [S] — [Baker and Wurgler](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jwurgler/papers/equityshare.pdf)
- Ritter data: average first-day IPO return of 65.0% on 803 offerings in 1999-2000 (64.6% on 849 in another table) against about 14.8% in the 1990s; an average of 310 IPOs a year over 1980-2000. [S] — [Ritter, University of Florida](https://site.warrington.ufl.edu/ritter/files/IPOs-Underpricing.pdf)
- Loughran and Ritter (Journal of Finance 1995, 50:23-52) documented long-run underperformance of equity issuers. [S] — [NBER w11459](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11459.pdf)

**Financing-stage (Minsky) indicators for the AI cycle**
- Hyperscaler bond issuance: about $121B in 2025 and $159B year-to-date in 2026 against a BofA full-year forecast of $175B (raised from $140B); Morgan Stanley's early-2026 forecast was $400B. [S][2nd] — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/ai-hyperscalers-159b-debt-issuance/); [Rallies](https://rallies.ai/news/amazon-raises-54-billion-in-bonds-bofa-sees-hyperscaler-debt-at-175-billion-in-2026)
- Broader AI-linked debt issuance of about $450-500B in the first nine months of 2026; more than $120B of data-center spending moved into SPVs and leases; Oracle's five-year CDS more than tripled after its September 2025 deal. [S][2nd] — [Quartz](https://qz.com/tech-hyperscaler-bond-issuance-investment-grade-index-050526); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/big-tech-ai-debt-competes-governments-capital/)
- JPMorgan (via Sparkline): AI stocks produced 75% of S&P 500 returns, 80% of earnings growth and 90% of capex growth since ChatGPT's release; Bain: data centers need $2 trillion of annual revenue by 2030 to justify cost against about $20B of AI revenue. [F] — [Sparkline Capital](https://blog.sparklinecapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/sparkline-ai-capex.pdf)

**Valuation timing**
- Regressing ten-year real returns on CAPE over 1871-2020 gives an R-squared of 29%; Dimensional concludes valuation ratios are not useful for allocation timing. [S] — [Dimensional](https://www.dimensional.com/insights/cape-fear-should-investors-be-concerned-with-market-valuations)
- Asness et al., "Market Timing: Sin a Little" (2017): the valuation-return link is stronger at 5-10 years than at one quarter. [S] — [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/419567707/Market-Timing-Sin-a-Little)
- One-year R-squared of CAPE of about 0.05. [S][2nd] — [eco3min](https://eco3min.fr/en/qa/valuations-matter-long-term/)

**Concentration**
- Top-ten weight in the S&P 500 was about 40.7% at end-2025 against an 18-23% range over 1990-2015 and about 26-27% at the 2000 peak. [S] — [RBC Wealth Management](https://www.rbcwealthmanagement.com/en-us/insights/the-great-narrowing-sp-500-concentration)
- Concentration level has no relationship with 6- or 12-month forward index returns (R-squared 0.04 and 0.07). [S] — [State Street](https://ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/insights/market-concentration-risk-and-how-to-diversify-now)

[S] — [MFS](https://mfs.com/en-au/investment-professional/insights/market-insights/diversification-disappearance.html); [S&P Global/Vanguard](https://www.spglobal.com/en/research-insights/special-reports/look-forward/partner-perspectives/unlocking-potential-ahead-with-vanguard/in-the-shadows-of-giants)

**Earnings beats, drift and reactions**
- S&P 500 EPS beat rate: 75% ten-year average, 77% five-year average, 78% in Q1 2025. [S] — [Benzinga citing FactSet](https://www.benzinga.com/analyst-stock-ratings/analyst-color/25/06/45758803/sp-500-q1-earnings-stun-again-the-street-with-broad-based-beats-double-digit-growth)
- Magnificent 7: six of seven beat in Q1 2025 (average surprise 14.9%); seven of seven beat in Q1 2026 against 84% for the index; in Q2 2026 their aggregate surprise was 66.2% against 26.5% for the index (five-year average 7.0%, ten-year 7.4%). [S] — [FactSet, 21 May 2026](https://advantage.factset.com/hubfs/Website/Resources%20Section/Research%20Desk/Earnings%20Insight/EarningsInsight_052126.pdf); [FactSet, 28 Aug 2026](https://advantage.factset.com/hubfs/Website/Resources%20Section/Research%20Desk/Earnings%20Insight/EarningsInsight_082826.pdf)
- Nvidia beat EPS consensus in 10 of its last 12 reports (another tracker: 8 of 8); average absolute next-day move about 7.6% (median 5.2%), positive in 8 of 12. [S][2nd] — [Invezz, May 2026](https://invezz.com/news/2026/05/20/nvidia-earnings-history-reveals-a-pattern-investors-may-be-overlooking/)
- Over the last eight reports to May 2026 the average next-day move was -0.9% with only 25% positive; February 2026 was the fourth straight decline after a beat-and-raise (-5.5% on a +5.3% EPS surprise). Since 2016 the stock closed higher the day after earnings 55% of the time. Extremes: +26.1% (May 2023), -6.4% (August 2024). [S][2nd] — [Motley Fool, May 2026](https://fool.com/investing/2026/05/22/nvidia-just-crushed-earnings-estimates-but-the-sto); [Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/229873/nvidias-earnings-on-nov-19-what-history-tells-us-about-nvidia-stocks-moves-following-earnings-releases)
- Post-earnings-announcement drift has declined significantly over four decades and may have disappeared outside hard-to-arbitrage stocks; explanations are more arbitrage or less informative earnings news. [S] — [Columbia working paper](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/CEASA/Events%20Page/PEAD_Declined_over_time.pdf); [University of Colorado](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_511043)

### Inferences
- Forecastable with evidence: (a) crash probability conditional on run-up size and on issuance, volatility and turnover; (b) multi-year relative underperformance of the fastest asset and capex growers, on the order of several points a year; (c) that mega-cap tech will beat consensus (base rate 85-100% for the Magnificent 7).
- Not forecastable with evidence: (a) the date of the peak — the Greenwood-Shleifer-You result is precisely that returns are not lower on average because some run-ups continue; (b) one-year index returns from CAPE or concentration; (c) the sign of Nvidia's post-earnings move, where the unconditional base rate is 55% up and the recent conditional rate after beat-and-raise is well below half.
- A beat is the expected outcome, so calls should be framed on the size of the beat relative to the whisper and on guidance, and probability for "stock up after a beat" should sit near 50%, not near the beat rate.
- Practical bubble-stage scoring from these sources: run-up magnitude (the 100% and 150% thresholds), issuance (equity share, IPO first-day returns against the 65% of 1999-2000), financing mix (capex/OCF near 100% against a 40% norm; debt issuance run-rate; SPV usage), and counterparty credit (CDS). By late 2026 the financing indicators have moved decisively toward the leveraged stage that Goldman said was only beginning in October 2025.
- Asset-growth evidence predicts relative, not absolute, returns and has operated over one-to-five-year horizons; it argues for preferring asset-light AI adopters over builders, not for a dated short call.

### Gaps
- Marathon/Chancellor "Capital Returns" was not read directly; only Sparkline's use of it.
- GMO and AQR bubble studies, and the track record of prominent bubble calls (for example the lag from Greenspan's December 1996 remark to the March 2000 peak), were not retrieved.
- Baker-Wurgler coefficient sizes and Loughran-Ritter five-year underperformance magnitudes were not confirmed.
- Bernard and Thomas (1989) drift magnitudes and analyst-revision momentum returns were not found.
- FactSet's "reward for beats" price-reaction statistic was not found.
- No guide-above-consensus base rate for Nvidia or mega-cap tech was found; only EPS beat rates.
- Current equity share in new issues and 2025-26 IPO first-day returns were not retrieved, so the issuance signal cannot be scored today.
- The 26.5% aggregate S&P 500 surprise for Q2 2026 is far outside the 7% norm and should be checked against the FactSet PDF before use.

## 6. Long-duration tech in Fed hiking cycles and when the 10-year yield breaks above prior ranges

### Takeaway
Tech has risen through most hiking cycles: of five cycles since 1994, the Nasdaq-100 or Composite was up or flat over four, and the one large loss (2022, about -33%) came when hikes began from peak valuations with rates rising at the fastest pace. The typical pattern is an early drawdown of about 10-15% and recovery while hikes continue, with the damage arriving after the last hike in 1999-2000.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Hikes alone have a poor record as a sell signal for tech: four of five cycles were flat to strongly positive. The discriminating conditions in 2022 were starting valuation and the speed of the move in real yields, not the fact of tightening.
- 1999-2000 is the relevant analogue for a capex boom under tightening: tech gained 59% while the Fed hiked, and the collapse followed the end of the hikes (the Nasdaq peak of 10 March 2000 preceding a final hike in May 2000 is from memory — unverified). Tightening coincided with the last and steepest leg, then with the financing closure that hit telecom customers.
- For semis specifically, the rate-driven 2022 drawdown (-46.5%) was deeper than the two digestion-driven ones (-26% in 2018, -25% in 2015-16), so a rates shock on top of a capex deceleration would stack two historically separate drivers.

### Gaps

- No academic equity-duration estimate (sensitivity of growth stocks to real yields) was retrieved.
- Returns for each cycle are from secondary pages using different indices and windows; exact first-hike-to-last-hike Nasdaq-100 returns should be recomputed from index data.
- The 1994 hike count and range are from a newsletter; the cycle ran into February 1995 (to 6.0%, from memory — unverified).
