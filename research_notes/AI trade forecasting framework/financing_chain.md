## 1. Neoclouds: debt, interest cost, financings, covenants, concentration, market moves; what was the "neocloud credit stress" of October 2026?

### Takeaway
The October 2026 "neocloud credit stress" was a CoreWeave-centred bond and CDS rout (unsecured yields 15-16%, 5-year CDS above 1,100bp, bonds below 80 cents) that began in the week of 12-16 Oct, spread to Nebius and IREN equity (each down well over 30% in October), shut the high-yield data-center primary market (zero deals since late September) and coincided with pulled IPOs. It re-intensified after the 3 Nov midterms (CoreWeave CDS peak 1,289bp on 5 Nov) and after Huang's 12 Nov exit; no neocloud has defaulted, and CoreWeave's response has been to stop issuing unsecured debt and fund through contract-matched secured loans and customer prepayments.

### Inferences
- The October event was a repricing of *unsecured, parent-level* neocloud credit, not a failure of contract-backed secured lending: CoreWeave still expects DDTL and prepayment funding, and Nebius closed a $3.5B secured facility in October while reportedly abandoning a high-yield bond. The chain is tightening from the bottom of the capital structure upward.
- Prepayments (CoreWeave ~70% of deals; Nebius >100% of capex on Q3 deals) shift the funding burden from lenders to customers. That makes the AI labs' and hyperscalers' own access to capital the next link, and makes the Anthropic IPO proceeds and OpenAI's round directly relevant to neocloud solvency.
- Dated pressure points visible in the sources: CoreWeave's equity-cure window ended 28 Oct 2026; DSCR first tested 31 Oct 2027; 130k GPUs re-contract in 2027 and 170k in 2028; Q4 2026 interest of ~$1.1B implies a ~$4.4B annualised run-rate against FY26 adjusted operating income guidance of ~$1.3B.
- Backlog quality is the contested variable. Lambda, Nscale and Volta backlogs all lean on single Anthropic commitments, some conditional; Nscale's active contract value is ~2.5% of headline TCV.
- Equity is no longer a cheap escape valve: CoreWeave and Nebius are down 45-57% since 6 Oct, and Nebius converts at 70 cents signal the convertible window is impaired for this cohort.

## 2. Oracle: debt load, OpenAI/Stargate commitments, spreads/CDS, rating actions

### Takeaway
Oracle is the investment-grade pressure point: S&P cut it to BBB- (stable) in July 2026, Moody's affirmed Baa2 with a negative outlook and Fitch BBB stable in early November, while its 5-year CDS went from ~58bp (Oct 2025) to a record 343bp (week of 19-23 Oct 2026) and ~300bp now, a single-B-equivalent level. It has responded by leaning on equity ($20B ATM completed June-August plus a $10B share sale at an 11.2% discount in early November) alongside a further $10B bond.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Oracle is one notch from junk at S&P with a negative outlook at Moody's. A fallen-angel event would be the largest of the cycle (IFR's framing references Ford/GM 2005) and is the cleanest binary rating indicator in the chain.
- Oracle's shift from "no more debt in calendar 2026" (June) to a $10B bond plus a deeply discounted $10B equity sale (November) shows the IG market is open to it only at a price, and that equity dilution is now the marginal funding source.
- With OpenAI payments reportedly starting in 2027, Oracle's credit is a levered claim on OpenAI's ability to fund itself; OpenAI's November round coming in below target (see section 5) is therefore a direct Oracle credit input.

## 3. Hyperscaler bond issuance, off-balance-sheet structures, data-center ABS/CMBS, private credit and bank exposure

### Takeaway
Hyperscalers have sold roughly $296B of bonds in 2026 YTD (Goldman, early November; other tallies run $244B-$371B on different scopes and dates), pushing US IG issuance to a record $1.88T, and collectively turned free-cash-flow negative in Q3. - The correlation problem is the real exposure: Nvidia's take-or-pay floors and residual-value guarantees pay out in exactly the state where its own sales slow. The projected growth of residual guarantees to $373B by FY31 makes this the fastest-growing contingent liability in the chain.
- Huang's exit converts Nvidia's willingness to extend its balance sheet from an assumed constant into a variable. - GPU residual values are currently *not* the weak point (rental prices recovered; older chips repriced up). The financing chain is being priced on offtaker credit, which redirects attention to OpenAI and Anthropic.
- The AMD warrants are a vendor subsidy that only vests with deliveries and a share price near record highs; with AMD at ~$553 the final $600 tranche is out of the money on the price test.

## 8. Effect of the Fed hiking cycle and a 10-year Treasury above 5% on refinancing costs

The Fed raised rates 25bp to 3.75-4.00% on 16 Sept 2026 (first hike since 2023), markets price an 88.5% chance of another 25bp on 8-9 Dec, and the 10-year reached ~5.13-5.15% on 13 Nov after a hot October CPI. ### Inferences
- The rate shock and the spread shock compound. With the 10-year near 5.15%, an IG data-center borrower at +245bp pays ~7.6%; the same structure a year earlier (Hyperion) cleared at 6.58%. For project economics underwritten on 15-20 year leases at fixed rents, a ~100bp rise in funding cost comes straight out of developer equity returns.
- Floating-rate exposure is the faster channel: DDTLs and bank loans reprice with each hike, so a December hike (and any 50bp surprise) feeds into CoreWeave-type interest lines within a quarter, while fixed-rate hyperscaler bonds are only affected at issuance.
- Convertibles were the workaround for high coupons (CoreWeave 2.875%, Nebius 0.5%/4.5%), but that depends on equity levels; with shares down 45-57% since 6 Oct the workaround is impaired just as rates rise.
- Hyperscaler AI issuance at "nearly half of long-dated IG supply" and "comparable to gilt issuance" means the chain is now large enough to be a term-premium factor itself, a feedback loop between AI supply and the 10-year that the IMF flags.
- The hiking cycle is young (one hike so far, a second priced). Refinancing costs have risen mostly through term premium and spreads rather than the policy rate, so there is more to come if the Fed continues.
