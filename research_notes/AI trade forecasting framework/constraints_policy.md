## 1. Power: is electricity the binding constraint on 2027 deployments, and by how much?

### Takeaway
Power delivery (grid capacity, interconnection, turbines and transformers) is the most consistently cited physical limit on 2026-2027 US data-center deployment: PJM has cleared at its price cap three auctions running and is 6.8 GW short of its reliability requirement for 2028/29, and Texas has frozen new large-load approvals on a 474 GW queue. The size of the 2027 slip is contested: project trackers imply roughly 15 GW of announced 2027 capacity not yet under construction, while an October 2026 audit still expects 38 GW online in 2027.

### Cited Findings

**Demand and forecasts**
- A Harvard-led study (Nature Sustainability, reported 16-17 Nov 2026) estimates 403 US hyperscale data centers used about 82 TWh in May 2024-April 2025 (range 68-99 TWh), about 1.8% of US electricity use; fossil fuels supplied about 54%; emissions about 25.7 Mt CO2; Virginia hosts 142 of the sites and about a quarter of the load. The figure is modeled from site capacity and utilization, not metered, and covers only the 403 validated sites. — [Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/how-much-power-do-hyperscale-data-centres-consume); [Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/harvard-study-hyperscale-data-centers-fossil-fuels/); [Yahoo/Wired](https://www.yahoo.com/news/science/articles/403-hyperscale-us-data-centers-103000177.html); contradicted on venue and CO2 (arXiv preprint, ~45 Mt) by [Gadget Review](https://www.gadgetreview.com/americas-403-largest-data-centers-consumed-an-estimated-82-terawatt-hours-of-electricity-54-powered-by-fossil-fuels)
- EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook (released 10 Nov 2026): US power demand 4,216 billion kWh in 2025 (record), 4,306 in 2026, 4,370 in 2027. Commercial sales (the category containing data centers) 1,503 → 1,557 → 1,600 billion kWh, passing residential sales. EIA's own text attributes growth to the commercial sector and weather; the "AI data centers" attribution is Reuters' framing. **Next STEO: 8 Dec 2026.** [search summary] — [EIA STEO](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/pdf/steo_full.pdf); [Reuters via BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2026/11/10/us-power-demand-to-beat-record-highs-in-2026-and-2027-as-ai-use-surges-eia-says/)
- One analysis computes that the commercial sector accounts for 97 of the 151 billion kWh rise in US power sales from 2025 to 2027 (64%). [search summary] — [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-sector-with-data-centers-gets-64-of-us-power-sales-growth/)
- A Dallas Fed working paper estimated data centers raised wholesale electricity prices by 6.9% as of 2025. [search summary] — [Al Jazeera, 7 Nov 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/7/how-the-us-midterm-elections-reflected-resistance-to-data-centres)

- FERC did not issue a generic large-load interconnection rule. After the Energy Secretary's October 2025 directive (final action sought by 30 Apr 2026), FERC in June 2026 issued six tailored show-cause orders giving each RTO 60 days to justify or revise tariffs; large load defined as peak above 50 MW connected above 69 kV. Docket RM26-4 remains open. [search summary] — [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/ferc_acts_advance_data_center_large_load_integration_six_rto_regions_0626.html); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ferc-doe-data-center-interconnection/823360/)
- Gas-turbine and step-up transformer lead times: three to four years vs a historical ~18 months (SemiAnalysis); other sources say about three years for new GE Vernova units and near five years for a new combined-cycle plant. Utilities that promised 2027 load ramps have come back offering 2029. ### Inferences
- Power is the binding constraint for grid-connected capacity in PJM and ERCOT, but not uniformly for 2027 deployments: turbines for 2027 sites were ordered in 2023-24, and self-powered and already-under-construction projects sit outside the Texas freeze. The constraint bites hardest on 2028-2029 additions.
- A defensible range for 2027 slippage: lower bound a few GW (2.3 GW held by local bans plus about 4.0 GW cancelled/delayed in PJM's forecast), upper bound about 15 GW (21.5 GW announced minus 6.3 GW under construction, Sightline). The two headline 2027 totals (21.5 GW vs 38 GW) use different definitions and should not be netted against each other.
- The 22 Dec 2026 PJM result is close to pre-determined on price (a fourth cap-clear is the base case given a 6.8 GW shortfall and a stalled backstop); the informative variables are the shortfall MW and the large-load forecast treatment.
- Texas is the largest single policy swing: 66.4 GW of "base load" projects wait on decisions that pass to a governor who campaigned on keeping the freeze.

### Gaps
- No verified, current (Oct-Nov 2026) estimate of GW delayed for 2027 from a primary tracker; Sightline's figures are months old and repeated across outlets.
- Goldman Sachs (31 GW in 2025 to 66 GW in 2027) and Gartner (40% of AI data centers power-constrained by 2027) figures surfaced only in search digests of low-authority pages; not verified.
- Transformer lead-time figures conflict (128-144 weeks for large power/GSU units vs 31-51 weeks for pad-mount in a Q1 2026 Gilbane survey); no primary source read.
- Whether PJM made its 29 Oct 2026 backstop refiling, and the 2029/30 price cap, were not confirmed.
- No LBNL or Grid Strategies 2026 update was retrieved; no specific Google demand-response contract was found.

## 2. Local and political opposition; the 3 November 2026 midterms; the incoming Congress

### Takeaway
Data centers and power bills were a top-tier midterm issue: restrictions passed in 46 of 47 local measures, Democrats won both chambers plus governorships in Texas and Ohio, and 32 of 34 winning governors backed tighter rules. No detailed federal Democratic agenda exists yet; the near-term binding actions are at state level (New York freeze, Texas freeze, Ohio and Wisconsin pledges).

### Inferences
- Federal legislation is the weaker channel through 2027 (divided government, presidential veto); oversight hearings, subpoenas and state utility-commission cost-allocation rules are the stronger ones.

## 3. Export controls and US-China chip policy

### Takeaway
On Monday 16 November 2026 Commerce issued an interim final rule licensing AI-chip shipments to any data center that gives China-headquartered firms more than 12 million aggregate TPP of compute, with a 90-day clock for existing sites and new license requirements for Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil. Two items in the brief need re-labelling: the Reuters "China data center curb" exclusive (17 Nov) is an FCC-led block on importing Chinese data-center equipment (optical transceivers), not a chip rule; and the "lawsuit exposure" for chip buyers comes from Nvidia's warning about the pending Remote Access Security Act, not from the Commerce rule.

### Cited Findings

**The 16 November BIS interim final rule**
- Requires a license to export, reexport or transfer advanced semiconductors to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand or Brazil ("higher-than-normal risk of diversion"), plus any country the Secretary designates; BIS says it was "written by and at the direction of" Treasury and the National Security Council. Chip stocks (Nvidia, Micron, Intel, AMD) fell that morning. — [CNBC, 16 Nov 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/nvidia-commerce-china-lutnick.html)
- A license is needed to send advanced AI chips to any data center where the result gives China-, Macau- or Country Group D:5-headquartered companies more than 12 million aggregate TPP; existing qualifying data centers have 90 days to obtain a license; compute used only for commercial recommender systems is exempt. — [MLQ (aggregator citing BIS release and rule)](https://mlq.ai/news/what-is-on-the-table-when-trump-meets-xi-in-shenzhen-chips-tariffs-and-the-january-truce-deadline/); [TrendForce, 17 Nov 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/17/news-u-s-targets-chinas-ai-compute-loophole-tightens-chip-exports-to-malaysia-singapore-and-three-others/)
- US companies operating data centers in the five countries are exempt, as are systems run by online retailers and social-media companies (Reuters). The rule formally rescinds the Biden-era AI diffusion framework, which Commerce had said in early 2025 it would not enforce; in the interim "billions of dollars' worth" of restricted Nvidia chips reached data centers serving Chinese AI firms. — [Reuters via RTE, 16 Nov 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/business/2026/1116/1599437-us-to-curb-chinas-access-to-ai-chips/); [TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/17/news-u-s-targets-chinas-ai-compute-loophole-tightens-chip-exports-to-malaysia-singapore-and-three-others/)
- Reaction: Chris McGuire (CFR, ex-NSC) called it "a major step forward" and said a follow-up rule is needed for activity inside the US; Rep. Moolenaar: "Our most advanced AI chips are simply not for sale to our foremost enemy"; Sen. Cantwell wrote that Alibaba and ByteDance trained models on overseas Nvidia-equipped data centers. — [CNBC, 16 Nov 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/nvidia-commerce-china-lutnick.html)

**"Lawsuit" exposure and Nvidia's customer rules**
- CNBC (17 Nov 2026, two unnamed sources): Nvidia told customers in recent months that the proposed Remote Access Security Act (RASA) could expose Nvidia and them to US "legal action", and that it will not sell to customers renting more than 50% of their GPUs to Chinese companies "for now". The House passed RASA in January 2026; the Senate has not. The FT reported earlier in November that Nvidia told data-center clients to direct at least 51% of services to non-Chinese customers. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-china-cloud-ai-chip-access.html)
- Tom's Hardware's headline ties the lawsuit exposure to the "new US rule"; the article body could not be retrieved. — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/policy/nvidia-warns-customers-of-new-us-rule-that-could-expose-them-to-lawsuits-for-serving-chinese-clients-commerce-department-releases-interim-regulations-preventing-foreign-actors-from-using-advanced-compute-based-on-american-tech)
- Nvidia reportedly cut its approved Asian buyer list by more than half, capped Chinese clients at cloud partners at 49%, and requires Southeast Asian cloud partners to enroll in chip-location tracking. — [Asia Times, 17 Nov 2026](https://asiatimes.com/2026/11/china-hoards-nvidias-ai-chips-as-us-curbs-remote-access/)
